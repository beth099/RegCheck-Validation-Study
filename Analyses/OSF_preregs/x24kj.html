Interpersonal Reappraisal Study

Study Information

Title

Friendship Study

Authors

Razia Sahi, Emilia Ninova, Jennifer Silvers

Description

The overall aim of this study is to help answer the question: How does our ability to regulate our own emotions using reappraisal compare to the efficacy of social reappraisal, i.e. when someone else helps us regulate our emotions using reappraisal? In a recent study on this topic (Sahi, R., Ninova, E., &amp; Silvers, J. A. (2018, September 18). Interpersonal Reappraisal Study. Retrieved from osf.io/m79a2), our lab found that (a) social reappraisal is more effective than reappraising our emotions in response to negative images on our own, and (b) the efficacy of social reappraisal is highly correlated to our ability to use reappraisal to down-regulate negative affect on our own. In response to this finding, the present study will examine whether the difference between the social reappraisal condition and self-reappraisal condition that we found was due to the fact that one condition was social, providing an inherent source of comfort or distraction regardless of the reappraisal. To answer this question, this study will include a counting task as part of the self block (i.e. count up or down from a particular number while looking at the image) and social block (i.e. listen to the friend count up or down from a particular number while looking at the image). The addition of this task to the social block can serve as a social baseline condition for the social reappraisal condition, and including it in the self block can serve as a control for the social baseline condition.

Hypotheses

Our primary hypothesis is that social reappraisal will be more effective in reducing negative affect than social counting. In other words, the effect of social reappraisal is not entirely due to the fact that the task is social in nature, providing a source of comfort or distraction regardless of the reappraisal. We also hypothesize that we will replicate our findings from study 1 (Sahi, R., Ninova, E., &amp; Silvers, J. A. (2018, September 18)) such that negative affect will be significantly lower in the negative-reinterpret as compared to the negative-look conditions, and significantly lower in the social reappraisal as compared to the self reappraisal condition.

Study design

This study will follow a 2 x 2 x 3 incomplete-within subjects design with the following dependent variables: (i) regulator: self vs. friend, (ii) stimulus: negative vs. neutral, (iii) task: reinterpret vs. look vs. count. We will begin by recruiting pairs of female participants who report having a supportive relationship. Participants from each dyad will be randomly assigned to be either the “experiencer” or the “helper” in the study. The experiencers will be told that their friend, aka the helper, will help them feel less negatively during some of the trials. The helper will be told they will be creating audio recordings to help their friend feel less negatively during some of the trials. The experiencer will begin by completing questionnaires about emotion regulation and general mood and traits. Then the experiencer will go over the study instructions and practice reappraising and counting with experimenter A. Meanwhile, the helper will create recordings in a separate room with experimenter B. These recordings will include reinterpretations, as well as counting up or down from a specific number. Helpers will be told that the counting task is intended to be calming. Before creating the recordings, the helper will complete a brief memory task designed to make their friendship more salient. After creating the recordings, the helper will complete questionnaires while experimenter B prepares the stimulus to include the recordings. Thus, the stimulus will be prepared by the time the experiencer is ready to begin their task. After the experiencer completes the questionnaires, they will complete the emotion task, which includes 8 conditions: (1) self-negative-reinterpret (12 trials), (2) self-negative-look (12 trials), (3) self-neutral-look (18 trials), (4) self-negative-count (12 trials), (5) friend-negative-reinterpret (12 trials), (6) friend-negative-look (12 trials), (7) friend-neutral-look (18 trials), and (8) friend-negative count (12 trials). During each of the 8 conditions, participants will be presented with an instructional cue, followed by a negative or neutral social image. Next they will provide a negative affect rating (how bad do you feel) on a scale of 1-4 (1 being not at all and 4 being very bad), and then relax before the next trial. The instructional cue for self-negative-reappraise will be ‘reinterpret’ and for self-negative-count will be ‘count’. The instructional cue for friend-negative-reappraise and friend-negative-count will be ‘listen.’ Meanwhile, the instructional cue for self-negative-look, self-neutral-look, friend-negative-look, and friend-neutral-look will be ‘look.’ Participants will be instructed ahead of time on what to due following each of these cues. Following ‘reinterpret,’ participants will think about the image in a way that makes their emotional response decrease. Following ‘count’, participants will slowly count up or down from a specific number. Following ‘listen,’ participants will listen to the helper either describe the image in a way to make their emotional response decrease or slowly count up or down from a specific number. Following ‘look,’ participants will look and let themselves respond naturally to the image. The order of the conditions will be partially randomized such that the four self and four friend conditions are consecutive (self block and friend block). The order of presentation of the self and friend blocks will be counterbalanced across participants. Images will be randomized into four different picture presentation orders to reduce the effect of idiosyncratic assignment of picture to instruction and picture order. Within each order, pictures will be counterbalanced into the self-negative-reappraise, self-negative-look, self-negative-count, other-negative-reappraise, other-negative-look, and other-negative-count conditions. The self-neutral-look and other-neutral-look conditions will also be counterbalanced. Instruction and picture types will be pseudo-randomized with the constraint that no more than three of any instruction type or picture type follow each other sequentially. The experiencers and helpers will complete a brief debrief at the end of the study to report on their experience during the study.

Explanation of existing data

Data collection procedures

Participants: Participants will include pairs of females aged 18-39 who report having a supportive relationship. Recruitment: Participants will be recruited from UCLA and the surrounding area using flyers, emails, and online advertising (e.g. Facebook). Inclusion/Exclusion Criteria: Participants must be females age 18-39, speak fluent English, have no pre-existing psychological disorders, have normal/corrected vision and hearing, and have a close same-gender friend with whom they can participate. Compensation: The "experiencer" will receive $20 and the "helper" will receive $10 for their participation in this study. Study Timeline: We plan to begin collecting data in Winter 2020 and hope to complete data collection by Spring 2020. We will analyze data and interpret the results during Spring-Summer 2020. *Note: We will restrict our sample to females because previous research has indicated that there may be gender differences in reappraisal implementation (e.g. McRae et al., 2008), and in social support provisions (e.g. Neff &amp; Karney, 2005). Given that we will not have the power to examine gender differences in this study, we will restrict our sample to females only for this study.

Sample size

Our total sample size for this study is 40 dyads (80 participants total).

Sample size rationale

The rationale for this sample size draws on previous work conducted on reinterpretation-style reappraisal (e.g. Ochsner et al., 2004; McRae et al., 2008; Ray et al., 2005). Since previous studies found an effect of reinterpretation-style reappraisal using a sample of 20-25 participants, we approximately doubled the sample size to account for our two within-subjects conditions of interest: self-reappraisal and social-reappraisal.

Stopping rule

Data collection will cease when we reach 40 dyads. Data for the main study will not be analyzed until all 40 observations have been collected.

Variables

Manipulated variables

Our primary manipulation is completing an emotion task that involves down-regulating negative affect either by reinterpreting the stimulus yourself (self-reappraisal), or by hearing a close friend reinterpret the stimulus for you (social-reappraisal). The self-reappraisal task will follow a standard reinterpretation paradigm (e.g. Ochsner et al., 2004), whereas the social-reappraisal task will be a slightly modified version of this task created for this study. The inclusion of the counting task in both of these conditions (self-count and social count) will provide us with a social baseline condition, and a control for the social baseline condition.

Measured variables

The following variables will be used in confirmatory analyses: - Self-reported negative affect for each trial (primary outcome variable) - Task version (covariate) - Order of blocks (covariate) - Regulator (predictor) - Stimulus type (predictor) - Task (predictor)

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

We will use mixed-effects general linear models to test our primary hypothesis with regulator (self vs. social), stimulus type (negative vs. neutral), and task (reappraisal vs. look vs. count) as independent variables, and self- rated negative affect as the dependent variable. We will check for main effects and interactions between these variables, with a particular interest in a specific contrast between social-negative-reappraise vs. social-negative-count.

Transformations

Inference criteria

We will regard p-values less than 0.050 as criteria for statistically significant results. We will regard p-values between 0.050 and 0.100 (inclusive) as being marginally significant. All reported p-values will be two tailed. We will use and report p-values obtained using robust standard errors.

Data exclusion

Data will be excluded if participants fail to comprehend the task or comply with experimenter instructions. Evidence of this may involve repeatedly making responses when inappropriate to do so, or not making responses at all when required.
