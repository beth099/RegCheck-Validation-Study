Exploring identity-safety transfer among Black women: The role of shared adversity

OSF ID: v59um | Registered: 2021-08-02T22:08:34.539898

Study Information

Hypotheses

We predict that the identity-incongruent safety cue (i.e. the LGBT+ Rainbow Pride Flag) will result in greater anticipated identity-safety relative to the control flag, but only when participants first learned about the overlapping history between the Black Freedom &amp; Labor Movement. We also predict that learning about the overlapping history between the Black Freedom &amp; LGBT+ movements (vs. Black Freedom + Labor movements) will result in greater perceptions of shared adversity, and we will explore shared adversity as a mediator of the identity-incongruent safety cue’s effect on identity-safety.

Study design

This study will utilize a 2 (Shared history with LGBT+: Present versus absent) × 2 (Rainbow Pride Flag: Present versus absent) between-subjects experimental design. Participants will be randomly assigned to one of four conditions: Shared history with LGBT-Rainbow Pride Flag, Shared history with LGBT-Control flag, Shared history with Labor-Rainbow Pride Flag, Shared history with Labor-Control flag. Participants will begin the experiment by being randomly assigned to read one of two news articles, a news story highlighting the overlapping histories of the Black Freedom Movement and LGBT+ Movement, or a news story highlighting the overlapping histories of the Black Freedom Movement and Labor Movement. Next, participants will view the webpage for a fictitious consulting company, Global Enterprises Consulting. After viewing the webpage, participants will view their randomly assigned DEI statement, featuring either the LGBT+ rainbow pride flag or the control flag, before reporting their perceptions of organizational commitment, attraction, and trust. Participants will then report their perceptions of procedural fairness for sexual and gender minorities, as well as Black individuals, followed by perceptions of shared adversity between Black and LGBT persons.

Explanation of existing data

Data collection procedures

Cisgender and heterosexual Black women will be recruited from the general population via a crowdsourcing platform (e.g. Prolific). Participants will be paid approximately $2.38 for a 15-minute study. The study will consist of a single, approximately 15-minute session per participant. The study will be advertised via the crowdsourcing platform to recruit the selected demographic. We will end data collection once we have reached our minimum sample size needed.

Sample size

To determine sample size, we conducted a pilot test on our news article (both created by the researchers) to ensure they were well-matched, and that they encouraged perceptions of shared bias. Our pilot test (N = 80) revealed an effect size of d = 0.56 among Black women participants for the difference between the Shared history with LGBT versus the Shared history with Labor conditions. Based on an a priori power analyses using G*Power with this effect size for a between-subjects 2 × 2 ANOVA, we would need a total sample of 210 participants. To account for potential exclusions due to failed manipulation checks, and to ensure our sample size is consistent with other studies in this line of research, we will recruit a minimum sample of 326 cisgender, heterosexual Black women.

Stopping rule

Variables

Manipulated variables

Cisgender and heterosexual Black women participants will be randomly assigned to learn about the overlapping histories of the Black Freedom &amp; LGBT+ Freedom movements, or the overlapping histories of the Black Freedom &amp; Labor movements in the United States. Participants will then be randomly assigned to view a fictitious organization’s diversity, equity, and inclusion statement which will either include a LGBT+ Rainbow Pride Flag or a control flag. Thus, the present experiment has 2 manipulated variables, with 2 levels each.

Measured variables

For all of the following outcome measures, we will average the items, with higher scores indicating more of the measured constructs. Participants will be told to respond as if they were employed for the company and to report their perceptions of organizational attraction (3 items), organizational commitment (5 items), and trust in the organization (4 items). Because past work has found that the inclusion of identity-safety cues can encourage perceptions a company is procedurally fair (i.e. treat their employees with respect and apply personnel procedures consistently), we will also have participants complete items assessing the extent to which the company is procedurally fair for sexual and gender minorities (4 items), as well as perceptions the company is procedurally fair for Black persons (4 items). Finally, to examine the role of shared adversity, participants will complete items assessing the extent to which Black individuals and LGBT+ persons have faced similar bias (4 items).

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

To examine our main question, we will conduct a 2 (Shared history with LGBT: Present versus absent) × 2 (Rainbow Pride Flag: Present versus absent) between-subjects ANOVA on each key dependent variable. Utilizing this analysis will allow us to examine both main effects and the interaction. We will also utilize a PROCESS mediational model to examine shared adversity as a mediator of the effect of the identity-incongruent safety cue (i.e. the LGBT Rainbow Pride Flag) on our composite measure of identity-safety.
