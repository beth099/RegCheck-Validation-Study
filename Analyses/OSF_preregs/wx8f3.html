Does order matter? Harmonic priming effects for (un)scrambled chord sequences: Experiment 2

OSF ID: wx8f3 | Registered: 2020-12-17T17:40:15.652971

Study Information

Hypotheses

First, scrambling the order of chords will affect the speed of processing. Specifically, unscrambled sequences (low temporal incoherence) will elicit faster responses compared to scrambled sequences (medium and high temporal incoherence) (H1). However, musicians will not be faster than nonmusicians (H2). Additionally, musicians will not be more sensitive to the task (measured with d-prime) than nonmusicians (H3).

Study design

Like Experiment 1, Experiment 2 adopts a mixed design with fixed factors of temporal incoherence (within-subject: low, medium, high), modality (within-subject: major, minor), and musical training (between-subjects: musician, nonmusician). Random factors include participant and musical stimulus (crossed).

Randomization

The experimental session will be divided into two phases. In the first training phase, participants are presented with a randomized set of 40 trials featuring stimuli representing the low temporal incoherence condition, with 25 trials featuring out-of-tune target chords. These trials measure the out-of-tune discrimination threshold of each participant using the Quest adaptive staircasing procedure. In the second experimental phase, participants will be presented with a randomized set of 96 trials (16 stims (8 in major and 8 in minor) x 3 incoherence levels x 2 intonation levels) in the main experimental session, with trials from the out-of-tune condition reflecting the participant's estimated discrimination threshold. The 96 trials were randomized such that the in-tune and out-of-tune conditions of each stimulus were not presented within 5 trials of one another.

Explanation of existing data

Data collection for Experiment 2 has just been completed for 60 participants (30 musicians). Data analysis to examine the a priori hypotheses has not begun. The language model architectures, training corpora, stimulus sets, and anonymized experimental results have been stored on a secure, password-protected server that is only available to the contributors on the project.

Data collection procedures

Participants were 60 adult members of the Lubbock community recruited through the School of Music and the Texas Tech University classified ads (TechAnnounce). Thirty participants with musical training equivalent or superior to second-year-university level form the musician group, and 30 participants with less than 1 year of musical training comprise the nonmusician group. All participants will provide informed consent, and the study was certified for ethical compliance by the Texas Tech Institutional Review Board. Data collection also took place following the outbreak of COVID-19, so the investigators followed additional safety protocols established by the Office of Research and Innovation at Texas Tech University. Participants will be excluded from the study if they are not US citizens or permanent residents, or if they do not report normal hearing, which will be confirmed with a standard audiogram administered before the experiment.

Sample size

Participants were 60 adult members (30 musicians) from the Lubbock community (at least 18 years of age).

Sample size rationale

Unfortunately, it is not possible to conduct a power analysis for the linear mixed-effects models (LMMs) in Experiments 1 and 2 because we do not have a priori estimates for the random effects (participants and stimuli) in the power simulations. Thus, we conducted power simulations for a 3×2×2 RM-ANOVA with the power level set to .80, and with a large effect size of partial eta-squared = .14 for the within-subject and between-subject fixed factors. The power analysis indicated that participant pools of 24 and 54 participants would be necessary to detect main effects of the within-subject and between-subject factors, respectively, and a participant pool of 20 participants to detect a within-between interaction. We therefore selected a participant pool of 60 participants.

Stopping rule

Variables

Manipulated variables

Independent variables include temporal incoherence (WS: low, medium, high), mode (WS: major, minor), and musical training (BS: musician, nonmusician).

Measured variables

Dependent variables include logRT (i.e., log response time), d-prime, and Bias c. Random effects include participant and stimulus (crossed) in a maximal random effects structure (i.e., intercepts and slopes for both random effects).

Indices

Discrimination performance on the intonation task will be estimated using sensitivity (d’) and bias (c) measures from signal detection theory. We will follow the guidelines described by Kingdom &amp; Prins (2016).

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

Dependent variables (logRT, d-prime, bias c) will be analyzed with linear mixed effects models using a full random effects structure as specified by the design of the experiment. Specifically, the models will include crossed random effects for participants and stimuli, with intercepts for each participant and by-participant slopes for the within-subject fixed effects, and with intercepts for each stimulus and by-stimulus slopes for the between-subjects fixed effect.

Transformations

Response times will be log-transformed to ensure normality of the residuals in the LMMs in both experiments. Visualizations will feature estimated means and standard errors for the back-transformed RTs.

Inference criteria

Omnibus tests will consist of Type III Wald F tests with Kenward-Roger approximation for the denominator degrees of freedom with p&lt;.05 criteria for the WS and BS factors and interactions. To examine more specific hypotheses about the potential differences between the levels of each fixed effect, we will include a planned comparison corrected with Bonferroni adjustment to examine the predicted linear trend for temporal incoherence (low &lt; medium &lt; high). We may also include a contrast for low vs. high.

Data exclusion

Because RT data typically feature long-tailed distributions that violate assumptions of normality and homogeneity of variance in repeated-measures designs, Ratcliff (1993) recommends eliminating the top 5% of the RTs in the long tail (i.e., the slowest, outlier responses across participants and experimental conditions). If necessary, we will follow this recommendation.

Exploratory analysis

We will conduct a mixed effects stepwise regression analysis to predict logRTs from both groups (musicians, nonmusicians) using predictors related to the information content (surprisal) and Shannon entropy (uncertainty) of the target chord, as well as the number of chords between the target chord and the penultimate chord (V7) from the unscrambled (low temporal incoherence) condition. The information-theoretic predictors will be estimated by the probabilistic model that was used to select the scrambled versions of each stimulus for the medium and high temporal incoherence conditions.
