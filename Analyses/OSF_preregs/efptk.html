Hope vs. Hopelessness on Meaning in Life

OSF ID: efptk | Registered: 2024-09-10T17:55:01.754930 | Showing: original version (1 version(s) on OSF)

Study Information

Hypotheses

We predict that those in the hope (vs. hopeless) condition will report greater meaning in life. We will also explore the hypothesis on purpose, coherence and significance. We will explore how hopeful feelings and cognitions (agency/pathways) uniquely mediate the effect of condition on meaning (purpose, coherence, and significance).

Study design

This study is a between-person experiment with 2 conditions: hope vs. hopeless.

Randomization

Participants will be randomly assigned to a hopeful or hopeless condition. Measures of emotion (happy, hopeful) and cognition (agency and pathways) will be counterbalanced.

Explanation of existing data

Data collection procedures

Participants will be recruited on Cloud Research. Participants must be at least 18 years old. Recruitment will begin around September 2024 and will continue until approx. 800 participants have been collected. Participants will be compensated with $1.00 for completing a 10-minute study online.

Sample size

We will aim for 800 participants.

Sample size rationale

A previous study used the same hope vs. hopeless induction on meaning in life and the smallest effect size produced (which was for significance facet) was d = .20. Using G*power, to detect an effect size of d = 0.20, with 80% power and an error probability of 0.05, it suggests a sample size of N = 788. Therefore, we will round to N = 800 to allow for data exclusion and potential smaller effect sizes.

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

An independent samples t-test will be used to compare conditions on DVs of happy, hopeful, agency and pathways, as a manipulation check. An independent samples t-test will be used to compare conditions on DVs of meaning in life. A one-way ANOVA will be used to compare conditions on meaning, with happiness as a covariate. A one-way ANOVA will be used to compare conditions on DVs of purpose, coherence, and significance (the other two facets are added as covariates when testing each one). This will be done with and without adding happiness as a covariate. PROCESS model 4 will examine potential mediation pathways, between X = condition (1=hopeful, 0=hopeless) on Y=meaning. Testing the following mediations (assuming that condition had an effect on hopeful, happy, agency and pathways. If condition did not have an effect on any listed mediator, it will not be tested): 1. M = hopeful 2. M = happy 3. M1 = hopeful, M2 =happy 4. M1 = agency, M2 = pathways 5. M1 = hopeful, M2 = agency, M3 = pathways 6. M1 = hopeful, M2 = agency, M3 = pathways, M4 = happy
