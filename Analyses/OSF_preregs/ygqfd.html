Perceived Responses to Capitalization Attempts and Gratitude in Daily Life: Do Age and Closeness Matter?

OSF ID: ygqfd | Registered: 2022-02-08T18:46:53.031123 | Showing: original version (1 version(s) on OSF)

Study Information

Hypotheses

H1: Trait-level perceived active-constructive responses to capitalization will be positively associated with momentary gratitude. H2: Age will moderate the association between trait-level perceived active-constructive responses to capitalization and momentary gratitude, such that older adults will demonstrate a stronger positive association between trait-level perceived active-constructive responses to capitalization attempts and momentary gratitude compared with younger adults. H3: Closeness with social interaction partner will moderate the association between trait-level perceived active-constructive responses to capitalization and momentary gratitude, such that there will be a stronger positive association between trait-level active-constructive perceptions of capitalization attempts and momentary gratitude during interactions characterized by higher social closeness (within-person effect) and for individuals who have closer social interactions (between-person effect). H4: Trait-level perceived active-constructive responses to capitalization are positively associated with more frequent momentary capitalization attempts. H5: Age will moderate the association between trait-level perceived active-constructive responses to capitalization and momentary capitalization attempts, such that older adults will demonstrate a stronger positive association between trait-level active-constructive perceptions of capitalization responses and momentary capitalization attempts compared with younger adults. H6: Closeness with social interaction partner will moderate the association between trait-level perceived active-constructive responses to capitalization and momentary capitalization attempts, such that there will be a stronger positive association between trait-level active-constructive perceptions of capitalization attempts and momentary capitalization attempts during interactions characterized by higher social closeness (within-person effect) and for individuals who have closer social interactions (between-person effect).

Study design

The purpose of this study is to investigate whether perceived positive responses to capitalization attempts are positively associated with momentary gratitude and momentary capitalization attempts, and whether older age and relationship closeness augment these associations. We will accomplish this aim by examining a sample of individuals between the ages of 25 and 85 years old from the community using a cross-sectional survey administered with Qualtrics and ecological momentary assessment surveys (6/day for 10 days).

Explanation of existing data

Basic descriptive statistics have been calculated. In other analyses with this dataset, we have examined age differences in momentary gratitude, capitalization, and social closeness. No other analyses relevant to this preregistration have been conducted.

Data collection procedures

We recruited participants from the community to participate in a larger study. Participants needed to be between 25 and 85 years old, read and speak English in their homes, and score below 2 on the self-report Ascertain-Dementia (AD8) questionnaire. Participants were compensated for their time. First, participants visited the lab and completed a demographics form, reporting their age and perceived responses to capitalization attempts. After this in-lab session, participants completed six daily surveys per day for 10 days to report their momentary gratitude, closeness with their social interaction partners, whether they engaged in capitalization, the recency of their last social interaction, and other constructs which are not the focus of the present study.

Sample size

Sample size rationale

Measured variables

Independent Variable: Perceived Responses to Capitalization Attempts We measured participants’ perceptions of how their close others typically respond to when the participant shares a positive event using the Perceived Responses to Capitalization Attempts (PRCA; Gable et al., 2004). This 12-item measure is separated into four subscales: active-constructive, passive-constructive, active-destructive, and passive-destructive. Scores for each PRCA subscale will be calculated by averaging the three items of each subscale. A total composite score for perceived responses to capitalization will be calculated by subtracting the scores of the passive-constructive, active-destructive, and passive-destructive subscales from the active-constructive score. Instructions: Please take a moment to consider how your family, friends, and romantic partner (if you have one) respond when you tell them about something good that has happened to you. For example, imagine that you come home and tell your friend about receiving a promotion at work, having a great conversation with someone, finishing a project, meeting someone interesting, winning a prize, or doing well on an exam or project at work or school. Please consider to what extent your family, friends, and partner do the following things in response to your good fortune. 7-point Scale: 1 (not at all true) to 7 (very true) (1) Active-constructive (averaged, 3 items) a. “They usually react to my good fortune enthusiastically.” b. “I sometimes get the sense that they are even more happy and excited than I am.” c. “They often ask a lot of questions and show genuine concern about the event. (2) Passive-constructive (averaged, 3 items) a. “They are silently supportive of the good things that occur to me.” b. “They try not to make a big deal out of it, but are happy for me.” c. “They say little, but I know he/she is happy for me.” (3) Active-destructive (averaged, 3 items) a. “They point out the potential problems or down sides of the good event.” b. “They often find a problem with it.” c. “They remind me that most good things have their bad aspects as well.” (4) Passive-destructive (averaged, 3 items) a. “They don’t pay much attention to me.” b. “Sometimes I get the impression that they don’t care much. c. “They often seem disinterested.” Dependent Variables: 1. Analysis Plan

Statistical models

•We will calculate bivariate correlations among perceived responses to capitalization attempts, age, frequency of momentary capitalization attempts (across all prompts), average momentary gratitude, and average momentary closeness. •We will conduct multilevel modeling (MLM) to test effects of trait active-constructive PRCA on momentary gratitude and momentary capitalization attempts. Random slopes will be included unless there are convergence issues, in which case random slopes will be selectively removed. •In all models including age, we will include physical health (SF-36v2 PCS) as a covariate. We will examine interactions using simple slopes testing as described by Aiken &amp; West (1991). •Model 1: Using MLM, we will predict momentary gratitude from trait active-constructive PRCA (grand-mean centered). H1: There will be a main effect of active-constructive PRCA predicting higher gratitude. •Model 2: Using MLM, we will predict momentary gratitude from trait active-constructive PRCA (grand-mean centered), age (grand-mean centered), and the interaction between active-constructive PRCA and age. H2: There will be an age X active-constructive PRCA interaction predicting gratitude, such that active-constructive PRCA is a stronger predictor of gratitude among older adults than among younger adults. •Model 3: Using MLM, we will predict momentary gratitude from trait active-constructive PRCA (grand-mean centered), closeness with social interaction partner (separated into within and between person effects: person-mean centered and grand-mean centered average individual rating, respectively), and the interactions between active constructive PRCA and each component of closeness. H3: There will be closeness X active-constructive PRCA interactions (between- and within-person) predicting gratitude, such that active-constructive PRCA predicts higher gratitude more strongly when social closeness is higher. •Model 4: Using logistic MLM, we will predict momentary capitalization (a binary variable) from trait active-constructive PRCA (grand-mean centered). H4: There will be a main effect of active-constructive PRCA predicting higher capitalization attempts. •Model 5: Using logistic MLM, we will predict momentary capitalization (a binary variable) from trait active-constructive PRCA (grand-mean centered), age (grand-mean centered), and the interaction between active-constructive PRCA and age. H5: There will be an age X active-constructive PRCA interaction predicting capitalization, such that active-constructive PRCA predicts higher capitalization more strongly among older adults. •Model 6: Using logistic MLM, we will predict momentary capitalization (a binary variable) from trait active-constructive PRCA (grand-mean centered), closeness with social interaction partner (separated into within and between person effects: person-mean centered and grand-mean centered average individual rating, respectively), and the interactions between active-constructive PRCA and each component of closeness. H6: There will be closeness X active-constructive PRCA interactions (between- and within-person) predicting capitalization, such that active-constructive PRCA predicts higher capitalization more strongly when social closeness is higher. •As a robustness check, we will re-run all models including covariates indicating within- and between-person components of social interaction recency (-1=no social interaction within 30 minutes of the prompt and 1=social interaction within 30 minutes of the prompt and then person-mean centered, and grand-mean centered average individual frequency of reporting a social interaction within 30 minutes of the prompt, respectively). Additionally, in all models including age, we will include physical health (SF-36v2 PCS) as a covariate.
