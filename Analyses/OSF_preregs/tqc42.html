Who gives a criterion shift? A uniquely individualistic cognitive trait

OSF ID: tqc42 | Registered: 2019-07-01T22:34:24.717517

Study Information

Title

Criterion shifting in recognition memory: explicit shifting with base rate manipulation vs. confidence judgments

Authors

Evan Layher, Michael Miller

Description

This study follows the same outline as the study described in the pre-registration: "Criterion shifting in recognition memory: explicit shifting vs. confidence judgments." During one session participants will make recognition judgments on a 6-point confidence scale and during another session participants will make "old" and "new" recognition judgments when explicitly told that there is either a 24%, 50%, or 76% chance that a test image was previously studied before.

Hypotheses

We predict there will be no significant relationship between the degree individuals shift their decision criterion in response to base rate information vs. the degree they shift between high confidence old/new judgments when making confidence ratings.

Study design

See pre-registration: "Criterion shifting in recognition memory: explicit shifting vs. confidence judgments." The explicit criterion shift manipulation will consist of a base rate manipulation during the test blocks (either 24%, 50%, or 76% old) instead of instructions stating only respond with old or new with "high confidence." The sessions will be evenly counterbalanced so half the participants perform confidence judgments during the first session and the other half during the second session.

Sample size

We will collect data from at least 124 participants who complete both sessions to its entirety. Any participant who misses a session, or does not fully complete at least one of the sessions will be excluded from analysis.

Sample size rationale

We will collect data from at least 124 subjects (who successfully complete both sessions), which gives us 80% power to find a significant correlation (p &lt; .05) when r = .25. Since there is potential for dropout, we will collect data from up to 134 subjects. This will leave us with a final sample of 124 to 134 participants.

Stopping rule

We will stop data collection once we collect between 124-134 participants
