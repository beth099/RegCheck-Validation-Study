Study Information

Hypotheses

We predict that participants will make different types of memory errors for the videos in different conditions. We predict people will remember more (extend) from what was shown in the short condition but will remember less (omit) of the ending from the videos in the long condition. Specifically, similar to previous work we hypothesise that participants will make more memory extension errors (remembering a completed action when an incomplete action was seen) when watching the videos under the short condition. On the other hand, we predict that participants will make more memory omission errors in the long condition.

Study design

This is within subjects design with 1 regressor of interest - an indicator variable whether the video was encoded in a short or a long condition (short: 0; long: 1). This study will be conducted online. There will be two separate sessions held at least 1 week apart. In the encoding session we will present participants with 24 videos. 12 will be in the short condition and 12 will be in the long condition. In the short condition the videos will be stopped just before showing a completed action. In the long condition the videos will be stopped after showing the completed action plus a scene change with the same actor in a new situation presented for a brief period of time. For instance, one video might be showing a girl sunbathing in the park. The video will show the girl having a snack, applying suns cream and grabbing book. In the short condition the video will stop just when she takes out the book of her bag. On the other hand, in the long condition participants will see the girl reading the book for a few seconds and then will include a scene change, showing the girl in her apartment about to start cleaning. Only a 2-4 seconds of the new scene will be presented to the participant in order to prevent them from building a detailed event model of the new situation. Notably, the videos in the short and long conditions will have the same duration. This will be achieved by including more of the start of each video in the short condition. This will be achieved by sliding a temporal window over the videos. For instance, if a whole video that contains both the completed action and the new scene is 60 seconds. For the short condition we might select the first 50 seconds (0-50) that do not include the new scene, but for the long condition we might include (10-60) seconds from the video that show both the completed action and the new scene.

Randomization

We will use block randomisation to randomly assign participants to two counterbalancing lists. All participants will encode videos in both the short and in the long condition. The counterbalancing list will allow us to vary across participants which video is in which condition (short or long). For instance, if in list A video 1 is in the short condition and video 2 is in the long condition. In list B the conditions will be reversed (video 1 will be in long condition and video 2 will be in the short condition).

Explanation of existing data

We have yet to collect data for this study. However, we have run two similar studies that have focused on examining extension memory errors. Different videos were used in previous projects that did not include a scene change.

Data collection procedures

The study consists of two sessions that each take approximately 30 minutes to complete and are spaced a week apart. Participants will be recruited through an online participant pool for behavioural scientists, called Prolific (https://www.prolific.co/). Here, a study advert will be posted including a brief description of the task and the payment offered. The study will only be visible to members of the participant pool who are eligible to take part. We will select participants to be in the age range of 18-35 and who are native English speakers. We are restricting recruitment to native English-speakers since the task requires the understanding of relatively complex short stories. Participants will be required to have normal or corrected to normal vision. Participants would also be required to have not been diagnosed with any conditions that can cause literacy difficulties. This is particularly important since instructions will be provided in text form.

Sample size

We will collect initially 100 participants for both sessions. Our target sample size is 80 people. We will collect 100 participants initially, since there is possibility of drop out. Particularly the study has two sessions spaced over a week apart, so we expect that some participants will not complete session 2 (the main session of the experiment). Furthermore, we expect that some of the participants that complete session 2 will have very bad memory / not good responses for the videos. In a previous study we excluded participants that recalled less than 9 videos (max 24), which was about 5% from the people that completed both tasks.

Sample size rationale

Based on previous studies with similar design we observed a logOdds effect of -2.2 and -2.0, for the memory extension errors which translates to approximately cohen's d of -1.2. The conversion was calculated as LogOdds * sqrt(3)/pi as recommended by (Sánchez-Meca, J., Marín-Martínez, F., &amp; Chacón-Moscoso, S. (2003). Effect-size indices for dichotomized outcomes in meta-analysis. Psychological methods, 8(4), 448.). Power simulations ran with the simR package in R suggest that with 80 participants we should have achieve 99.9% (98.17, 100.0) power to detect an effect size that is twice smaller in magnitude)(logOdds: -1.2). Note the effect is in negative direction because we have coded presence of memory extend errors as 1 and used the short condition as the reference category. Therefore, a negative logOdds can be interpreted that memory extension errors are less likely to occur for videos in the long condition, which is our hypothesis for this study as well. Note that the sample size rationale is based on previous effects for memory extension errors. We have no previous effect estimates for the memory omission errors. However, simR simulations suggests that logOdds effect of -0.4 (Cohen’s d of -0.22) and effect 5.5 times smaller than the previously observed effect for memory extension errors can be detected with 71% power.

Variables

Manipulated variables

Dependent Variables: Whether people make a memory extent error and whether they make memory omission errors. Independent within-subject variable: Whether video shows complete or incomplete action.

Measured variables

Outcome variable we will measure is whether participants make a memory extent error. This will be measured with a binary rating 0 - no error; 1- memory extent error. Similarly, we would also have outcome variable of whether participants make a memory omission error; 0 – No error. -1 memory omission error.

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

Given the multiclass nature of the data we will run a mixed multinomial regression comparing memory extension errors, memory omission errors vs correctly remembered clips as a reference category and examining how these errors are affected by the video condition (short vs long). We note that mixed effects multinomial regression will be fit with newly developed R package (mclogit). The outcome variable would be the different classes of memory errors and we will use the correctly remembered trials as a reference category. We will include a random slope and intercept for the subject grouping variable. In case of convergence issues we will first remove the random slope variable. We would also run two separate mixed effect logistic regressions for each binary outcome variable (memory extension errors and memory omission errors). The separate mixed effect logistic regressions will be fitted in order to allow for random effects for both video and subject. Furthermore, the logistic models will be included for completeness since, previous work has reported mixed logistic effects models. Although the separate mixed effect logistic models should provide similar conclusion to the mixed multinomial regression, given that the package for this analysis was recently developed we opt for also running the individual logistic regression models. First step in the analysis will be that we will rate all trials with a Remember or Not Remembered rating. If on a given trial a participant responded that they did not remember the clip or remembered the wrong clip this trial would be marked as Not Remembered. We will focus all our main analyses only on trials marked as Remembered since participants cannot make memory extension errors or memory omission errors for Not Remembered trials. For each remembered trial we would also provide a binary rating of whether participants made a memory extension error. Memory extension errors occur when a participant remembers something that was not shown in the original video. Specifically, we will focus on intrusions near the end, and participants reporting remembering a completed action when the video stopped before the outcome of the action was seen as completed. For example, in one video a girl in the park is seen taking out a book. In the short condition the participants won’t see what she does with the book. In the long condition participants will see that the girls starts to read the book and then would see a scene cut and see briefly the girl in her apartment starting to clean. An example response from a participant who falsely extended their memory might be: “A girl is seen in the park sunbathing; she puts sun cream on her face and takes out a book and reads for a while”. On the other hand, responses that specifically state that the girl does not start reading, but simply takes out the book would be given a rating of 0 for the extent memory error. Note that participants could make extent errors also on videos in the long condition that showed completed actions. The same criteria will be applied such that trials on which participants reported falsely remembering events that did not happen would be scored as extent being present. An example response for the video of the girl in the park might be that despite them seeing the girl starting to read they also say that she finished reading the book and then used the book to swat flies. Apart from memory extension errors, we would also rate memory omission errors. In the long condition memory omission errors will count as omitting to report seeing the new scene – when the girl is in her apartment. In the short condition, we would rate omission errors failure to report the last few actions just before the end of the video (e.g. the girl taking out the book, or the girl eating snack). The last actions that would count towards the omission errors will be selected based on the timing of the new scene. If the new scene (girl in her apartment) is presented for 2 seconds, then we will count missing reports for the actions seen in the last 2 seconds of the video in the short clip as omission errors. Note we have uploaded an excel sheet with all the actions for each video annotated by a researcher. We will initially pilot this omission rating with responses from 20 participants and will adjust the rating method if necessary. Apart from the memory responses participants will be asked to provide subjective memory ratings such as vividness and confidence.
