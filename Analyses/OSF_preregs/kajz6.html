Study Information

Title

Responses to the Leadership Team of a Social Justice Organisation

Authors

Aarti Iyer

Description

Members of high-status groups (e.g., men or White people) often take up leadership positions in social justice efforts that seek to achieve equality for low-status groups (e.g., women or ethnic minority groups). Little is known about how observers evaluate these visible examples of influential solidarity by the high-status group: does the presence of these leaders inspire members of the low-status group or alienate them? Our first two studies found that observers from low-status groups report more negative responses to leadership teams composed of mostly high-status group members, compared to leadership teams composed of mostly low-status group members. Exploratory analyses indicated that participants’ perceptions of leaders’ awareness of inequality accounted for their negative responses (felt inspiration and collective action intentions). To provide a stronger test of this mediation model, the present study experimentally manipulates leaders’ awareness of inequality and assesses observers' responses.

Hypotheses

1. The leadership team will be perceived as being more aware of gender inequality in the high awareness conditions compared to the control (no mention of awareness) conditions. (Manipulation Check) 2. Participants will report higher levels of felt inspiration about the mostly male leadership team in the high awareness condition compared to the control (no mention of awareness) condition. 3. Participants will report higher levels of collective action intentions in response to the mostly male leadership team in the high awareness condition compared to the control (no mention of awareness) condition. 4. The indirect effect of the awareness manipulation (for the mostly male leadership team) on collective action intentions via felt inspiration will be significantly different from 0.

Study design

Between-participants design with two orthogonally manipulated independent variables: composition of leadership team: mostly female vs mostly male awareness of gender inequality: explicitly stated as high vs no statement control Four conditions will be created in this design: Condition 1: Mostly male leadership team + high awareness of inequality Condition 2: Mostly male leadership team + control (no mention of awareness) Condition 3: Mostly female leadership team + high awareness of inequality Condition 4: Mostly female leadership team + control (no mention of awareness)

Explanation of existing data

Data collection procedures

Participants will be recruited for this online study via a UK crowdsourcing platform, Prolific Academic. The study will be advertised on the platform with an invitation to participate. Participants will be paid £1 to complete this 8-minute study. Participants must self-identify as women, as the aim is to recruit members of the disadvantaged group in the context of gender inequality. They must also be at least 18 years of age and residents of the United Kingdom. Individuals who have participated in any of our previous studies on this platform (2016 - present) will be excluded from participating in the present study. The current advertisement will not be displayed in these individuals' list of available studies.

Sample size

Our target sample size is 400 participants, to give us 100 participants in each condition. To ensure that we achieve this target of participants who have completed the study, we will recruit 500 individuals.

Sample size rationale

We used the G*Power software program to conduct an a priori power analysis. Our aim is to obtain .80 power to a small effect size (0.15) at the standard alpha error probability of 0.15.

Stopping rule

The study will be closed after the first 500 participants submit their questionnaires.

Indices

All measures were developed in previous studies that were conducted in this line of research. We will create composite measures by taking the mean of designated items for each multi-item measure: perceived awareness of inequality, felt inspiration, felt anger, collective action intentions.

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

Manipulation Checks Two-way between-participants ANOVAs will be used to assess the impact of each manipulation. The aim is to show that responses on each manipulation check measure are systematically influenced by the main effect of the corresponding independent variable/factor, rather than the main effect of the second independent variable/factor or the two-way interaction between the two independent variables/factors. Test of Experimental Effects A one-way between-participants ANOVA will be used to compare the four groups on the focal outcome measures. A pairwise comparison will then be used to test differences between the two mostly male leadership conditions: Condition 1 (mostly male + high awareness of inequality) vs Condition 2 (mostly male + no mention of awareness control). Test of Direct and Indirect Effects A mediation model was developed using exploratory anayses in two previous studies. The consistent indirect effect that emerged in these studies will be tested here using the PROCESS Macro to investigate responses to the mostly male leadership team (solidarity context): awareness manipulation (high vs. control) --&gt; felt inspiration --&gt; collective action intentions

Transformations

Given the results of the previous studies in this research program, we do not expect any variables to require additional transformations.

Inference criteria

We will use the standard "p &lt; .05" criterion in all tests to determine if the results are indicated to be significantly different from those expected if the null hypothesis were corrrect. The post-hoc Tukey-Kramer test adjusts for multiple comparisons.

Data exclusion

Participants who fail the gender composition manipulation check will be excluded, as this response indicates that they did not properly comprehend the stimulus materials. Outliers will be included in the analysis.

Missing data

Participants who fail to answer 50% or more of the questionnaire items will be exluded from the study.

Exploratory analysis

For the focal outcome variables (felt inspiration, collective action intentions): Compare Condition 1 (mostly male + high awareness) to conditions with mostly female leaders: Condition 3 (mostly female + high awareness) and Condition 4 (mostly female + control) For additional outcome variable (felt anger): conduct tests of experimental effects and direct/indirect effects
