Probing Semantic and Affective Accounts in Crossmodal Associations between Visual Textures and Temperature Concepts - Experiment 2

OSF ID: qjm3t | Registered: 2022-06-16T13:30:27.215052

Study Information

Hypotheses

Even though the visual textures used in this experiment are not associated with temperature concepts, we expect that if the influence of the mechanism is strong enough, participants learning to associate the visual textures with the underlying affective or semantic content behind those visual textures that are associated with temperature concepts, will lead to the formation of association with temperature concepts. That said, the experiment aims to shed light on the magnitude of the influence of each mechanism. More formally, we hypothesized that: H1A: Participants will classify whichever visual texture as hot (vs. cold) more often when it is mapped to the affective stimuli related to high (vs. low) temperatures than when it is mapped to affective stimuli related to low (vs. high) temperatures. H1B: Participants will classify whichever visual texture as hot (vs. cold) more often when it is mapped to the semantic stimuli related to high (vs. low) temperatures than when it is mapped to affective stimuli related to low (vs. high) temperatures. Since the visual textures are not normally associated with temperature concepts, they are neutrally valenced, and they do not hold semantic associations related to the stimuli being used, we did not expect to see any difference in reaction times.

Study design

The experiment will follow a 2 (Mechanism: affective, semantic) × 2 (Pairing: cold-related, hot-related) × 2 (Time: before learning, after learning) mixed design, where mechanism and pairing are between-subjects factors and time is a within-subject factor.

Randomization

Participants will be randomly assigned to one of the four possible groups resulting from the interaction between the mechanism and pairing factors.

Explanation of existing data

Data collection procedures

Participants will be recruited from Prolific (https://www.prolific.co/) and will be compensated with GBP 1.20. Participants will be between 18 and 40 years old, residents of the UK, and native English speakers. The experiment will be conducted in Gorilla (https://gorilla.sc/), and it is expected to last approximately eight minutes. Participants will only be able to use a laptop or desktop to complete the experiment.

Sample size

The total target sample size is 300 participants (75 participants per block).

Sample size rationale

The required sample size was determined via a power analysis based on goodness-of-fit test using G*Power (Faul et al., 2007). We aimed to obtain a statistical power of .80, using a medium effect size of Cohen's w = 0.3 with an alpha level of .05. This calculation yielded a required sample size of 277.

Stopping rule

Variables

Manipulated variables

The manipulated variables will be mechanism (affective, semantic) and pairing (cold-related, hot-related). The variables will be manipulated by modifying the stimuli with which each of the visual textures will be paired in the learning phase. As per the mechanism factor, in the affective conditions, the stimuli will consist of the facial expressions of two emojis taken from the EmojiGrid (Toet et al., 2018), namely the negative valence, low arousal (V1A1) and the positive valence, low arousal (V5A1) ones. In the semantic conditions, the stimuli will consist of the words "Fur" and "Metal". As per the pairing factor, since the visual textures are not normally associated with temperature concepts, the pairings a be are no congruent or incongruent. Nevertheless, we will use the same stimuli and call them "hot-related" (i.e., the positive valence, low arousal emoji expression and the word furry) and cold-related (i.e., the negative valence, low arousal emoji expression and the word metal).

Measured variables

The measured variables comprised participants' temperature classification (cold vs. hot) and the response time in every trial. The two variables will be measured at two points in time: before and after the learning phase.

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

To obtain an overall perspective of the classification of the visual textures under the different conditions, we will first conduct a log-likelihood ratio goodness of fit test with Williams’ correction. Subsequently, we will perform separate logistic Generalized Linear Mixed Models (GLMM) for each visual texture to determine the probability that they are classified as either hot (for the Furry visual texture) or cold (for the Crystalline visual texture) with the interaction of mechanism, pairing, and time as fixed effect and with participant's ID as random effect. Then Bonferroni-corrected pairwise comparisons will be performed. To analyze participants' response times, we will perform two separate GLMM with Gamma distributions splitting the data for each mechanism on response time with the interaction of visual texture, time, and pairing as fixed effect and participant ID as random effect. Then Bonferroni-corrected pairwise comparisons will be performed.

Transformations

Inference criteria

We will use standard p &lt; .05 for determining a significant log likelihood ratio goodness o fitness test for the classification data. Furthermore, we will use standard p &lt; .05 for determining significant coefficients in the GLMM models. In addition, the full GLMMs models will be tested against null models with only the random effects via likelihood ratio test.

Data exclusion

We will winsorize participants' response times more than three median absolute deviations (MADs) plus or minus their median response time.
