Contextual modulation of task switching

OSF ID: 83npu | Registered: 2021-05-12T13:40:30.329195 | Showing: original version (1 version(s) on OSF)

Study Information

Hypotheses

In this study, we hypothesize that when people perform a group of tasks in contexts associated with different task switching probabilities, over time, the switch cost is lower (i.e., lower lower reaction time/error rate difference between switching trials and repetition trials) in the high switching probability context than in the low switching probability context.

Study design

We will use a behavioral cued task switching paradigm with a within-subjects design (high switching probability 90% vs low switching probability 30%). Task switching probability indicates the probability that next task is different from the current one. If the current task is different from the previous one, it is considered a switch trial, otherwise it is a repetition trial. This experiment consists of several categorization tasks with 5 tasks: Clothing, Electronics, Foods, Liquids and Tools. We selected 240 object pictures from these categories, 48 in each. Switching probabilities are bound to a context, airport security or supermarket, respectively, which is counterbalanced among subjects. In the instruction, participants will be informed this difference, i.e., in one context, they will experience more switches than the other. After instruction, there are several confirmation question to ensure participants get the key point. The experiment has three sections: a practice section (2 blocks), a test section (4 sessions, each session has 12 blocks with total 240 trials) and a questionnaire section (Likert scales). In the practice and test sessions, the context is interleaved block by block, and the order is also counterbalanced among subjects. In the test session, each session has some probe blocks where the switching probability is 60% for a direct comparison of switch costs associated with the two contexts.

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

To test our hypothesis, we will run a 2 x 2 x 2 ANOVA with the within-subjects factors context (high versus low switching probability), task sequence (task repetition versus task switching), and block type (training versus test) on correct reaction times and error rates. We expect to observe a two-way interaction between context and task sequence indicating larger switch costs in the low switching probability context. Although the above will be our main analysis approach, we will also evaluate our effect for training versus testing block types separately (irrespective of whether we find an interaction with this factor in the overall ANOVA). We will also run a mixed effect model to account for potential confounding variables such as task identity and item.

Inference criteria

Data exclusion

Participants who don't get the context difference (measured in questionnaire) will be excluded from all analyses. Participants with overall accuracy lower than 75% or below Q1 - 1.5*IQR of group accuracy will be removed. We will remove the first trial in each block. For RT analysis, error, post-error trials and trials with RT outliers (defined as out of Q3 + 1.5*IQRs or Q1 - 1.5*IQR) will be removed from the main statistical analysis. If, after removal, the remaining data is less than 50% of total trials or below Q1 - 1.5*IQR of group data remaining rate, then this participant will be excluded. Additionally, all data that are incomplete due to technical problems are also excluded.
