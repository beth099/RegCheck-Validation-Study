Cancellation of Planned Actions: Manipulation of Discard Time

OSF ID: a5ze7 | Registered: 2022-10-19T14:42:09.817487

Study Information

Hypotheses

We aim to find out what happens to action files once they can be discarded. The main hypothesis is that partial overlap costs (i.e., the difference between partial overlap and no/full overlap conditions) will be larger in continue than in discard trials. Possibly, the discard signal triggers the unbinding (disintegration) of still-activated features of action A. If no binding exists anymore, no retrieval of still-bound features can occur. Instead, there might be even overlap-benefits which might be stronger the more activated, but no more bound, overlapping features there are, hence with full as compared to partial overlap. A pilot study (n = 20) indeed yielded reduced partial overlap costs as well as general speeding in discard as compared to continue trials. With respect to the discard time manipulation we derived the following hypothesis: Discard processes should have a higher chance to operate the more time they have to unfold, which should lead to relatively fewer traces of partial overlap costs the longer the interval between discard signal and stimulus B. Thus, partial overlap costs should be smaller with more discard time.

Study design

Overall, this is a 2 (Hand: Overlap vs. No Overlap) X 2 (Duration: Overlap vs. No Overlap) X 2 (Signal: Continue vs. Discard) X 2 (Discard Time: Short vs. Long) within-subjects Design, with the dependent variables reaction times and error rates. With the levels of the first two independent variables, however, one can calculate partial overlap costs, the measure of interest for most analyses, which helps to slim the design to a 2 (Signal: Continue vs. Discard) X 2 (Discard Time: Short vs. Long) design. Which colour signals whether the plan for action A is to be retained or discarded will be counterbalanced across participants. With a total of 896 trials and with a 2 (discard time) x 2 (hand overlap) x 2 (duration overlap) design, we will have 28 trials per discard condition and 84 trials per continue condition.

Explanation of existing data

Data collection procedures

Data will be collected in the Würzburg lab, and participants will be recruited from the general population via Sona. There will be two 1-hour sessions, taking place on different days. Participants will be paid €20 for the two sessions.

Sample size

We will collect data from n = 52 participants.

Sample size rationale

Prior to this experiment, we conducted two pilot studies (one online, n = 8, and one in the lab, n = 20), with constant discard time (400ms) to ensure the principal functionality of the paradigm. In both, we found similar effect sizes for the comparison of the two signal conditions with respect to the partial repetition costs (i.e., the difference between the mean of partial repetition conditions and the mean of the full and no repetition conditions). Specifically, we found slightly larger effects in the reaction times (dz = 0.92, online, and dz = 0.91, lab) than in the error rates (dz = 0.40, online, and 0.49, lab). A small- to medium-sized effect (dz = 0.40) is both a conservative estimate for the effect of discarding in general (i.e., the reduction in partial overlap costs), and a good starting point for exploring the effect of discard time on the size of the partial overlap costs reduction (Brysbaert, 2019). It requires a minimum of 52 participants to find this effect size with a power of .80, and alpha = .05.

Stopping rule

Variables

Manipulated variables

As typically in this paradigm, we will vary whether actions A and B share a location feature (Hand: Overlap vs. No Overlap) and/or a duration feature (Duration: Overlap vs. No Overlap). Importantly, in 25% of the trials, the plan for action A can be discarded before stimulus B onset (Signal: Continue vs. Discard): The time for discarding the action A plan after the discard signal, or for continuing to plan action A after the continue signal (Discard Time: Short vs. Long) will be varied.

Measured variables

We will measure reaction times and error rates in action B.

Indices

Partial Overlap Costs will be calculated as follows: Mean (Partial Overlap) – Mean (Full, No Overlap).

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

To test for effects of discard time, we will conduct 2 (Signal) x 2 (Discard Time) rmANOVAs for partial overlap costs (in reaction time and error rate differences). We will use paired samples t-tests to test for simple main effects. Then, we will disentangle performance in full, no, and partial repetition trials for continue and discard conditions separately. To do so, we will conduct 2 (Signal: continue vs. discard) x 3 (Overlap: full, no, partial) rmANOVAs for reaction times and error rates either collapsed over both discard time conditions or separately (depending on if we find a significant reduction of partial repetition costs in both conditions or not) .

Transformations

In the 2 (Signal: continue vs. discard) x 3 (Overlap: full, no, partial) rmANOVAs, a quadratic contrast will compare partial overlap conditions to full/no overlap conditions and a linear contrast will compare full and no overlap conditions.

Inference criteria

p &lt; .05

Data exclusion

We will exclude participants with more than 30% errors overall, and with less than 10 correct trials per cell in the analyses. Reaction Times smaller than 150 ms and those deviating more than 3 SDs from the respective cell's mean will be excluded from the reaction time and the error rate analyses.

Missing data

Exploratory analysis

We will analyze action A performance exploratory, that is, whether partial repetition costs with respect to action B occur in continue trials, and whether incorrect action A executions in discard trials will depend on action B feature repetition. In case of inconclusive or contradicting results using reaction times and error rates, both will be summarized into Balanced Integration Scores (BIS, Liesefeld, &amp; Janczyk, 2019).
