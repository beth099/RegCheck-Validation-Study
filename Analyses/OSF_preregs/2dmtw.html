Affective Prediction Errors in Maintenance and Escalation of Harmful Policies

OSF ID: 2dmtw | Registered: 2021-03-01T15:50:35.810812 | Showing: original version (1 version(s) on OSF)

Study Information

Hypotheses

Affective prediction errors (APE)—the difference between expected and actual emotion ratings—are positively associated with the maintenance (policy choice) and escalation (policy exploitation) of a given policy (earning or stealing). APEs about aggressive (stealing) behavior are more positive (higher mean) than APEs about non-aggressive (earning) behavior.

Explanation of existing data

Data collection procedures

Participants will be recruited through Prolific and paid about $8 per hour including a bonus for their performance.

Sample size

The target sample size is 800 participants. We will recruit 1000 participants based on exclusion criteria established in a pilot study.

Sample size rationale

A bootstrapped power analysis of the models corresponding to our principal hypotheses indicated ~80% power at 800 subjects in a pilot sample equivalent to the study proposed here. About 15-20% of participants are expected to be excluded for one or more of the following reasons: 1. Failed attention check (e.g. stating to have had more than one fatal heart attack in one's life) 2. Fewer than 5 button presses on average across trials, given that each trial requires 10 presses that can be easily completed with the allotted time per trial. 3. For analyses conditioned on past stealing behavior: Never having chosen to steal 4. For analyses involving affect predictions: No variability in responses

Stopping rule

Variables

Manipulated variables

We are manipulating 1 between-subjects factor: target group; the factor has 2 levels: Democrat vs. Republican.

Measured variables

Prior to the main block of trials, we ask participants to provide us with the following information before matching them with another participant: 1. Participant age, political party, and gender. 2. How much participants like, value, and feel connected to members of each political party included in the study (3 items per political party, for a total of 6 items; party order randomized). Note that main analyses are conducted using these identification ratings. Then, on each trial of the main block, we measure: 1. Whether participants decided to steal or to earn 2. The amount that participants decided to steal or earn (between 1 and 8) 3. Whether participants completed the subsequent task necessary to obtain the reward (based on button presses) 4. Number of times participants pressed the button on each trial 5. How good participants expected to feel after the trial (0-100 scale) 6. How good participants actually felt after the trial (0-100 scale) Following the main block, we once again measure how much participants like, value, and feel connected to members of each political party included in the study (3 items per political party, for a total of 6 items; party order randomized).

Indices

Like, value, connect measures about each political party will be averaged (within-political party) and standardized across the sample. Affective prediction errors will be calculated as the difference between predicted and actual affect on each trial. Learning rates will be calculated as the proportion of the change in predicted affect between t and t-1 relative to the affective prediction error at t-1.

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

All of our analyses are based on repeated measures within individuals. We will use mixed effects regression to analyze relationships between affective prediction errors and different indicators of aggression. Indicators of aggression can be binary (stole / didn't steal; switched from prior policy / didn't switch) or continuous (amount stolen; difference in amount stolen relative to previous trial; learning rate). The format of the response variable will be reflected in the type of regression model (logistic / linear). Random effects will be included where appropriate to account for variance explained by different subjects, trial numbers, and successful completion of (prior) trials by having pressed the button a sufficient number of times. The main models of interest predict: 1a) the probability to stick to the chosen policy at t, using affective prediction errors at t-1; we hypothesize a positive relationship 1b) increases in the amount chosen within a given policy from t-1 to t, using affective prediction errors at t-1; we hypothesize a positive relationship 2) affective prediction errors at t using policy choice (steal/earn) as a predictor; we expect steal trials to be associated with more positive prediction errors As manipulation check, we will also test whether aggression increases as identification with the target decreases. Analyses that don't rely on repeated measures (e.g., group identification by political party, where participants are expected to identify more with their in-group) will be conducted using standard linear regression.

Inference criteria

We will rely on 95% confidence intervals and the p &lt; .05 criterion to infer statistical significance, including one-sided tests according to predictions, as well as Bayes Factors &gt; 10, where appropriate.

Exploratory analysis

Learning rates have not shown systematic patterns in prior studies but will be explored for policy/reward/group-based differences.
