Study Information

Title

Intentionality, speaker’s attitude and the processing of verbal irony

Authors

Camilo Rodriguez Ronderos, Ira Andrew Noveck, John Michael Tomlinson Jr

Description

The literature on irony processing has often looked at whether ironic sentences are easier, just as easy, or harder to understand than equivalent literal sentences, resulting in mixed findings. In this study, we investigate how information about a speaker's attitude towards an upcoming utterance and expectational biases about the answer to a polar question influence reading times of sentences that are understood either ironically or literally. By doing this we wish to investigate whether these two factors can modulate reading times of ironic and literal sentences and help explain why their respective reading speed varies. The study is thus a 2X2 repeated-measures experiment with the factors Speaker's Attitude (sincere vs. insincere) and expectaitonal bias type (zero bias vs negative bias). The present experiment is a direct replication of a previous study conducted by the authors: The only change made is the number of participants tested. The current number of participants was determined via simulations using the previous data as the starting point.

Hypotheses

We have 3 sets of hypotheses: 1. answers to comprehension questions: Participants should overall understand a speaker's insincere attitude towards an upcoming sentence as a reliable cue for ironic intent, as indicated by the answer to the comprehension questions in the critical trials. This should translate to an effect of speaker's attitude on the answers to comprehension questions, with answers in the sincere condition being moslty indicative of a literal understanding of the target sentence and answers in the insincere condition showing an ironic understanding of the target sentence. 2. Reading times: Our main hypothesis is that we should should find a significant interaction between the two factors, such that the insincere-negative bias reading times are shorter than the insincere-zero bias reading times, and the sincere-zero bias reading times are shorter than the sincere-negative bias reading times. This interaction effect should appear in a model using sum-contrast coding. 3. Reading times: Additionally, in a model of the same data using a slider-contrast coding, we make specific predictions regarding the differences between the individual conditions. We predict that: 1. The insincere-zero bias condition will show significantly longer reading times than the insincere-negative bias 2. The sincere-negative bias condition will show significantly longer reading times than the sincere-zero bias condition 3. The reading times in the insincere-negative bias condition will not be significantly different than the reading times in the sincere-zero bias condition 4. The sincere-zero bias will show significantly shorter reading times than the insincere-zero bias condition 5. the insincere-negative bias condition will show significantly shorter reading times than the sincere-negative bias condition

Study design

The experiment has a repeated measures, latin-square 2X2 design (every participants sees every item but only in one condition and every participant sees every condition at least once) with the factors Expectation Bias (zero vs. negative expectation bias) and Speaker’s Attitude (sincere vs. insincere attitude). There are 4 experimental lists that participants can be assigned to.

Randomization

The order of presentation of critical and filler sentences is randomized for every participant. The assignment of participants to indivual lists is randomized.

Explanation of existing data

Data collection procedures

Participants will be recruited online through the website Prolific and will be tested using the Ibex experimental platform. Participants will be American or Canadian monolingual speakers of English between the ages of 18-35. They will all be right-handed. They will receive 0.85 pounds Sterling for their participation. Participants who answer less than 75% of filler comprehension questions correctly will be excluded from the analysis. The reading time data will be log-transformed. Individual observations will be removed from the analysis if they are more than 3 standard deviations away from the condition mean.

Sample size

220 participants will read 9 critical stories and 10 filler stories each.

Sample size rationale

The number was determined via a power analysis through simulation using pre-existing data as a starting point, following the guidelines provided by Green &amp; Macleod(20016) and using the corresponding R package SimR. For the power analysis, we set the minimum Cohen's d effect size of interest at 0.2 and calculated how many participants we would need to achieve 80% power based on this effect size for every one of the comparisons described in the hypotheses section. We settled on 220 participants because with this number all comparisons should reach at least 80% power.

Measured variables

We will measure reading times of our target utterances. The reading times will be log-transformed prior to analysis, so that log(RTs) will be the dependent variable used. We will also measure the question-answering behavior of the questions following both the critical items and the filler items. The accuracy of responses to the filler items will be used as an exclusion criterion, whereas answers to the question in the critical conditions will be used to determine comprehension of either a literal or ironic intent of the critical sentences.

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

The data will be analyzed fitting a mixed-effects logistic regression model for the accuracy data and a mixed-effects linear regression model for the reading times data. Both models will include a 'maximal' random-effects structure (Barr et al.,2013) with random intercepts and slopes by items and by participants. If any of the models fails to converge, the random effects structure will be reduced in the following order: 1. Remove random correlation of random effects by subjects and then by items 2.Remove random intercepts by subjects and then by items The model for the reading times will be fitted twice with two different contrast coding schemes. The first one will use a sum-contrast coding scheme (testing main effects and interactions) and the second one a slider-contrast coding scheme (testing simple effects, or differences between individual conditions).

Transformations

The reading times will be log-transformed, following the procedure used for analyzing the data of the previous identical experiment, in order to help normalize the residuals of the regression model.

Inference criteria

Statistical inference will follow the model of null-hypothesis significance testing, with an alpha level set at 0.05: P-values below this threshold will be taken as evidence against the null-hypothesis. Additionally, we will consider statistical significance in relation to the size of specific effects: We expect the predicted effects to have a cohen's d value of at least 0.2.

Data exclusion

Data will be excluded in 3 ways: 1. Participants who answer less than 75% of the filler comprehension questions accurately will be excluded from the analysis. 2. log-transformed reading times more than 3 standard deviations away from the condition mean will be excluded. 3. If a participant answered a critical comprehension question incorrectly (i.e. understood the sentence literally in the insincere condtions or ironically in the sincere conditions), the reading times from this trial will be excluded from the analysis.
