Study Information

Title

Effects of vowel harmony on visual word recognition

Authors

Jukka Hyönä, Manuel Perea, Ana Marcet

Description

A phenomenon called vowel harmony exists in several languages (e.g., Finnish, Turkish). It refers to a linguistic feature whereby front and back vowels cannot appear in the same lexeme. For Finnish (the language studied in this project), it has been shown that native Finnish listeners use vowel disharmony as a word boundary cue in spoken language comprehension (Suomi, McQueen &amp; Cutler, 1997; Vroomen, Tuomainen &amp; de Gelder, 1998). In reading, vowel disharmony facilitates the recognition of two-constituent compound words by marking the morphological boundary between the constituents (Bertram, Pollatsek &amp; Hyönä, 2004). In this project, we will examine the extent to which vowel harmony affects the recognition of monomorphemic words as studied by the visual lexical decision task combined with the masked priming paradigm. With this task, we will be able to tap into early effects of vowel harmony in visual word recognition, provided that they exist. In Finnish, the front vowels are ä, ö and y, while the back vowels are a, o and u. The front and back vowels cannot appear in the same lexeme. On the other hand, the neutral vowels e and i can appear both with front and back vowels. Diacritical marks in ä and ö are visually salient signals of vowel quality. Thus, the target words always contain front vowels containing diacritical marks. The target words are preceded by masked primes that contain either vowel harmony or disharmony with the target word. The primes and targets share all other letters but one vowel whose identity is manipulated with 5 different prime conditions.

Hypotheses

Comparison and Hypothesis 1: To test the role of diacritical marks in signaling vowel harmony, the target word (e.g., "PÖYTÄ" = table) is briefly preceded (prime duration is 50 ms) either by an identity prime "pöytä" or a disharmonious prime where the diacritical marks of one vowel are missing ("poytä"). If the diacritical marks play a significant role in visual word recognition, then the disharmonious condition should produce slower lexical decision latencies than the identity condition. Comparison and Hypothesis 2: In Comparison 1, the base letter is repeated in both prime conditions (the letter o in the examples above). Thus, if Hypothesis 1 is supported, it is not clear whether the difference is due to the repetition of the diacritical marks or the base letter, or both. In Comparison 2, we test whether repeating the base letter across the prime and target facilitates visual word recognition. We compare two disharmonious primes against each other, one sharing the same base letter (poytä), the other not sharing the base letter (paytä) with the target word (PÖYTÄ). If base letter repetition facilitates visual word recognition, the poytä prime condition should produce shorter lexical decision times than the primes not repeating the base letter (paytä). Comparison and Hypothesis 3: If the diacritical marks play a significant role in signaling vowel harmony without the need of repeating the base letter, the prime condition with diacritical marks (päytä for PÖYTÄ) should produce shorter lexical decision latencies than the prime condition without diacritical marks in the manipulated vowel (paytä for PÖYTÄ). Comparison and Hypothesis 4: To test whether the diacritical marks play a role in signaling vowel harmony when the base letter is not repeated between the prime and target, we will compare two harmonious prime conditions to each other, one with diacritical marks (päytä) and another without (peytä). If so, the päytä prime condition should produce shorter lexical decision latencies than the peytä condition.

Is there any additional blinding in this study?

The masked primes are presented at the subconscious level (the prime duration is 50 ms, and the prime is preceded by a visual mask and followed by the target stimulus), so the participants will not become aware of the primes. The existence of the primes will not be mentioned in the instructions either.

Study design

The participants will be presented all the five prime conditions (i.e. a within-participants design). Five lists of materials are created so that the target word appears in List 1 preceded by Prime 1, in List 2 preceded by Prime 2, etc. Each stimulus list contains an equal number of trials for each prime condition. The presentation of the 5 stimulus lists are counterbalanced across the participants.

Randomization

Within each block of trials, the order of trials is individually randomized.

Explanation of existing data

Data collection procedures

The participants will be recruited from an introductory psychology course at the University of Turku, Finland. The participants will get course credit for their participation. The experiment will be run in September-October, 2019. To be included, the participants need to be native speakers of Finnish. Participants with reading disability or abnormal vision will be excluded.

Sample size

A total of 50 participants will be recruited.

Sample size rationale

Each prime condition contains 34 trials (target words). Thus, with 50 participants, each condition will have 1700 observations, which is considered sufficient for pairwise within-participants comparisons (Brysbaert &amp; Stevens, 2018).

Stopping rule

Variables

Manipulated variables

The target words are primed using 5 prime conditions. The primes differ from the targets in one vowel, typically in the first syllable in 2-syllable target words (nouns or adjectives of 5-6 letters long). The primes for the target word PÖYTÄ (table) are the following: (1) identity prime ("pöytä"), (2) repetition of the base letter without diacritical marks ("poytä"); vowel disharmony, (3) different base letter + no diacritical marks ("paytä"); vowel disharmony, (4) vowel harmony but not signaled by diacritical marks ("peytä"), (5) vowel harmony signaled by diacritical marks, but base letter not shared ("päytä").

Measured variables

The key dependent measure is the reaction time in the lexical decision task, but we will also measure and analyze the accuracy of the responses.

Indices

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

Linear mixed effects models will be used to analyze the data. Participants and items will be entered as the random effects. The planned pairwise comparisons detailed in the hypotheses will be tested as the fixed effects.

Transformations

Inverse transformations (-1000/RT) will be computed on the reaction times.

Inference criteria

t values greater than 1.96 will be considered statistically significant. We will also use the lmerTest package to obtain the approximate p values.

Data exclusion

Reaction times shorter than 250 ms or greater than 3 standard deviations from the overall mean will be excluded from the analyses. 2000 ms is the deadline to make a response, and thereby response times above 2000 ms will be automatically categorized as errors by the program (DMDX; Forster &amp; Forster, 2003).
