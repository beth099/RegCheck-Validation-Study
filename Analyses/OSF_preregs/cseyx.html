Examining left-wing authoritarianism in a U.S. nationally representative sample.

OSF ID: cseyx | Registered: 2020-10-05T20:29:41.543721

Study Information

Hypotheses

We plan to assess the following variables in relation to authoritarianism (left and right): Social cohesion, past use of force or violence for a political cause, autocratic orientation, support for violent collective action, past involvement in violent collective action, lethal partisanship, general personality, dogmatism, intellectual humility, motivational values, social media behavior, willingness to sign petitions endorsing authoritarian actions, and willingness to donate to extremist political parties. We predict that LWA will be correlated as follows with these variables. Social cohesion: r = .30 Past use of force or violence for a political cause: r = .35 Autocratic orientation: r = .35 Support for violent collective action: r = .50 Past involvement in violent collective action: r = .35 Maladaptive political social media use: r = .30 Lethal partisanship: r = .40 Honesty-humility: r = -.14 Emotionality: r = .25 Extraversion: r = -.13 Agreeableness: r = -.12 Conscientiousness: r = -.25 Openness: r = .06 Dogmatism: r = .30 Intellectual humility: r = -.25 Willingness to sign petitions endorsing authoritarian actions: r = .45 Willingness to donate to extremist political parties: r = .35.

Explanation of existing data

Data collection procedures

We will use Prolific Academic to collect a sample of roughly 1,000 people whose sex, race, and SES are proportional to the U.S. population. We will pay them $3.25 for 30 minutes of their time.

Sample size

1000 individuals.

Sample size rationale

A minimum of 750 people is needed for a nationally representative sample - we will aim for a sample size of 1000 to allow for exclusions and inconsistent responding as needed.

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

We will use ESEM to provide a confirmatory test of the LWA Index (following the procedures described in Costello et al. [2020]) and use regression-based analyses to examine LWA's relations with external criteria. We plan to control for political ideology in secondary analyses. We will provide formal tests of LWA's construct validity using the quantifying construct validity procedures described in Costello et al. (2020), which are based on Westen &amp; Rosenthal's (2003) work on this subject.

Transformations

Inference criteria

Data exclusion

Participants will be excluded on the basis of their HEXACO validity scores and a failure to correctly answer more than one attention check question.

Missing data

Missing data will be imputed using EM in cases where less than 10% of the items comprising a variable are missing. Otherwise, pairwise deletion will be used for our correlational analyses.
