Tactile Meta-Agency: How does tactile information influence our metacognition of agency?

OSF ID: ur73j | Registered: 2023-03-07T11:43:42.468487 | Showing: original version (1 version(s) on OSF)

Study Information

Hypotheses

In the Agency task, we hypothesise that the level of tactile manipulations will be inversely related to agency ratings, similar to temporal and spatial manipulations. In the MetAgency task, we hypothesise that metacognitive efficiency (measured as M-ratio) will be different between conditions that differ in the experimental manipulation (described below), once we account for first-order discrimination performance. In particular, if tactile information contributes especially to the subjective experience of agency, we expect to find higher metacognitive sensitivity in an agency discrimination task that involves violations of tactile predictions, as compared to other (spatial or temporal) prediction violations. If, on the other hand, tactile predictions do not play a special role on the subjective experience of agency, and predictions on any sensory modality are equally important, we will see no differences in metacognitive sensitivity.

Study design

We will have a repeated-measures design where each participant will complete two tasks — each of which will include trials from all three conditions. Due to the duration of the tasks, testing will take place in two sessions. Two conditions of the MetAgency task will be tested in the first session. The remaining MetAgency condition, along with all conditions of the Agency-task will be tested in the second session.

Randomization

In each task, trials of each of the three conditions will be organised in separate blocks and the order of blocks will be pseudorandomized independently for each participant.

Explanation of existing data

As of the date of submitting the preregistration, data from 8 participants were collected for the Agency-task and 10 for the MetAgency-task. We have only examined the staircased values used to control the discrimination performance in the MetAgency-task from the first 5 participants, to establish the five levels of manipulation to be used in the Agency task. Given that the staircase procedures employed did not stabilise the performance of three participants, these three datasets will be excluded. We have not performed any further analyses with these datasets or any further sanity checks with the remaining ones.

Data collection procedures

Participants will be recruited through the subject pool of the university and graduate school subject pool or common contacts, Facebook, etc. Pre-selection criteria are: • 18-40 years old • Right-handed • No neurological or psychiatric history • Normal or corrected-to-normal vision • Good English speakers • No motor impairment on either hand. Data will be collected at the HU by the PhD student leading the project. We will not communicate the aim of the study to participants.

Sample size

We will aim to collect data from 40 participants that pass the criteria for inclusion (described below).

Sample size rationale

To keep in line with the previous sample size from our lab using different experimental conditions to study the metacognition of agency, we plan to collect data from n=40 participants. This sample will be enough to detect an effect with sufficient power: Based on the t-statistic of data from Arbuzova, et al. (2022) we calculated an effect size of said study of cohens’ d=0.866. With a type-I error probability of 0.05, a sample size of n=12 is required to reach power equal to 0.8 for pairwise t-tests. The larger, and therefore conservative, sample size of n = 40 will allow us to detect smaller effect sizes and will provide us with enough power for an ANOVA with the three factors included in our study.

Stopping rule

We will stop data collection once we have reached the sample of 40 participants that, after initial data checks, passed the exclusion criteria (described below) and can be included in the dataset.

Variables

Manipulated variables

We will include the same manipulations in both tasks. We will manipulate the online feedback participants receive while performing the movement. In three conditions, either the timing of the movement, the location of the hand in virtual space, or the direction of the ridges on the physical plate will be incongruent with the real movement or tactile sensation. In the MetAgency-task, all the manipulations will be controlled by separate 2-down, 1-up staircases to keep participants’ accuracy at approximately 71% on each condition. The starting value of each staircase will be determined for each participant and condition separately with a training session of 16 trials prior to the main session of each condition. In particular, for the temporal manipulation, the virtual hand will move with a temporal delay relative to the movement participants perform. The starting value of the temporal staircase will be 200 ms and the step-size of the staircase will be 20 ms with no limit on how large the temporal delay can be. In the spatial condition, the virtual hand will move synchronously with the participant's hand, but the position of the hand will deviate a few rotational angles to the left or to the right of the participant's real hand. Specifically, the starting value of the spatial staircase will be 3.25 degrees and the step-size of the staircase will be 0.15 degrees. The maximum possible value for the spatial manipulation is set to be 4.25 degrees as values above this magnitude result in participants touching the edge of the disc and thus making the task trivial. In the tactile condition the ridges of the physical discs will deviate a few angles clockwise or counterclockwise from the vertical axis passing. The starting value of the tactile staircase is set to approximately 65 degrees (corresponding to the 150 steps of the stepper motors that rotate the discs prior to each trial. One step is 0.45º) and the step size of the staircase will be 4.5 degrees (10 steps of the stepper motors). The maximum possible value for the tactile manipulation is set to 90 degrees. In the Agency-task we will use five levels of magnitude manipulation in each condition. To determine these levels, we visually inspected the three staircase-plots from the first 7 participants that took part in the MetAgency-task. For three out of these seven participants, the staircase procedure did not work (the staircases reached the maximum possible value) and thus did not control participants’ performance. These participants will be excluded from the analyses. Nevertheless, based on the other four participants, we set the level of magnitude manipulations to be: temporal (ms): [0, 50, 100, 150, 200]; spatial (angles): [0, 3, 3.3, 3.6, 3.9] and tactile (steps of rotating motors) [0, 50,100,150, 200].

Measured variables

In the Agency task, we will measure subjective ratings of agency and reaction times to these reports. In the MetAgency task, we will measure response accuracy and reaction times to the 2IFC, as well as confidence ratings and reaction times in the confidence task.

Indices

We will compute mean subjective ratings for the Agency task. In the MetAgency task, we will compute mean accuracy using percentage correct and d’, as well as metacognitive efficiency using the metad’/d’ ratio).

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

We will evaluate differences between conditions using one-way repeated analysis of variance (ANOVA) and t-tests (both Bayesian and frequentist).

Transformations

No transformations are planned.

Inference criteria

We will base our inferences on p&lt;0.05 and Bayes factors.

Data exclusion

Participants will be excluded: If there is evidence that they did not understand the task instructions. For each analysis separately if the number of trials for that analysis is not sufficient to reach a stable M-ratio estimate. In case they indicate themselves in more than 10% of trials that they made a mistake in responding to the type-1 task, as below. Full conditions will be excluded: In cases of type-1 accuracy under 60% or above 85%. Trials will be excluded: In case type-1 reaction time is under 200 ms or above 8 s. In case subjects signalled (during the type-2 task) that they made a mistake in the type-1 response. In case movement time towards the target is above 5 s (on task 2, this refers to either interval). Exclusion of trials/subjects will be based on decision criteria that are not influenced by the outcome of any analysis that is done on the data.

Missing data

We will exclude the data from participants that, due to a low trial number in at least one condition, cannot be entered into at least one of the paired t-tests between conditions.

Exploratory analysis

We will explore the relationship between subjective agency ratings and metacognitive judgments of agency. By including the three conditions in both tasks we will test how different experimental conditions affect these two processes. In particular, we will measure how the agency ratings at each level of magnitude manipulation correlate with participants' metacognition of agency to test if participants with higher metacognition of agency have equally high sensitivity in their agency judgments provided at separate levels of manipulation.
