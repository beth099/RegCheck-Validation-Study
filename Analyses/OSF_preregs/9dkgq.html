Exploring individual differences in the gradiency of native phonetic perception and in the accuracy of non-native phonetic perception

OSF ID: 9dkgq | Registered: 2021-07-26T13:58:33.685739

Study Information

Hypotheses

Hypothesis 1: Performance on VAS and 2AFC tasks is related through consistency in acoustic cue encoding. If this is the case, shallower 2AFC slopes (lower phonetic sensitivity) will not relate to VAS gradiency but will relate to less consistent VAS responses. Hypothesis 2: The ability to discriminate finely tuned differences in native speech sounds relates to the ability to accurately distinguish non-native speech sounds. If this is the case, greater native phonetic sensitivity (steeper 2AFC slopes) and perceptual gradiency (shallower VAS slopes) will relate to better non-native phonetic perception.

Study design

Observational study with 3 phonetic perception tasks, 1 working memory task, and 1 sustained attention task. The attention task and 2 of the phonetic perception tasks are counterbalanced so that half of the participants see the 2 response buttons in a particular position (e.g. "bet" on the left, "bat" on the right), and the other half see the buttons in the opposite position (e.g. "bat" on the left, "bet" on the right).

Explanation of existing data

Data collection procedures

Participants will be recruited through the platform Prolific.co. They will be pre-screened to meet the following criteria: born in, currently living in, and citizen of Canada or the United States; aged 18-35; right-handed; English speaking monolingual whose only fluent language is English; and no language related disorders, literacy difficulties, head injury, ongoing mental illness, mild cognitive impairment, medication use to treat depression or anxiety, or hearing difficulties. Participants will be paid $12.50 USD (~$16 CAD) for their participation. Because of the popularity of studies on the Prolific.co platform, it is expected that our sample size will be reached within one or two days of posting the study.

Sample size

Our target sample size is 120 participants. Because it is expected that some participants will not complete our tasks as directed, we will recruit 140 participants with the understanding that some will be excluded for low-effort performance. If our sample size after exclusion is smaller than 120, we will recruit additional participants until our target of 120 is reached.

Sample size rationale

Sample size has been estimated through a triangulation of approaches. As an initial step, we reviewed the sample size in comparable studies, most notably that of Kapnoula et al. (2017), which is closest to the current study and included a sample of 120 participants, leading to a significant effect. As a second approach, we relied on Harrell’s (2015) rule of thumb applied to our design, which includes 6 predictors; multiplying the 6 predictors by 15 participants per predictor yields a sample size estimate of 90 participants minimum. Finally, we computed a power analysis based on one of our sub-analyses (multiple regression with 6 predictors, which reflects all of our analyses for our regression models testing Hypothesis 1 and Hypothesis 2), given that conducting such an analysis on a multivariate design such as the present one would be overly complex. This power analysis (with power = 0.95, alpha = 0.05, and number of predictors = 6) using the power.f2.test function from the pwr package (Champely, 2020) in R revealed that a sample size of 120 is required in order to reliably detect effects of a similar size (r &gt;= 0.4) to those reported in related studies (e.g., Clayards, 2018; Grimaldi et al., 2014; Kong &amp; Edwards, 2016). Therefore, we settled on a sample size of 120 participants.

Variables

Manipulated variables

This study does not involve experimental manipulations. However, as described under the design plan, three of the tasks are counterbalanced so that half of the participants see the 2 response options in a particular position (e.g. "bet" on the left, "bat" on the right), and the other half see the options in the opposite position (e.g. "bat" on the left, "bet" on the right). Also, as described in the Measured Variables section, the stimuli for our L1 perception tasks are minimal pairs (bet-bat and dear-tear; from Clayards 2018, publicly available at https://osf.io/369my/) that have been manipulated so that each one varies systematically in two acoustic cues relevant to the contrast (formant frequency and vowel duration for bet-bat, voice onset time and onset F0 for dear-tear). Each cue varies in 5 steps, and each version of the first cue is paired with each version of the second cue, leading to 25 stimuli per pair. This results in some stimuli that sound clearly like one word or the other, and some stimuli that sound ambiguous. The two varying acoustic cues are used as predictors of participant's responses in our analyses (as described under the Analysis Plan).

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

To test hypothesis 1, two types of analyses will be run: canonical correlations and a multivariate multiple regression. The first canonical correlation will examine the relationship between the four 2AFC coefficients (AFC.betbat.stepA.coeff, AFC.betbat.stepB.coeff, AFC.deartear.stepA.coeff, AFC.deartear.stepB.coeff) and the two VAS slope measures (VAS.betbat.slope, VAS.deartear.slope). The second canonical correlation will examine the relationship between the four 2AFC coefficients and the two VAS consistency measures (VAS.betbat.consistency, VAS.deartear.consistency). Following up on these correlations, the multivariate multiple regression will have all four 2AFC coefficients as the response, and six variables as the predictors: the two theoretically motivated VAS slope measures, the two theoretically motivated VAS consistency measures, and the two control measures of AXCPT.bin.score and BDS.level in order to account for attention and working memory respectively. To test hypothesis 2, a multiple regression model will be fit with Oddity.A.score.exp as the response, and six variables as predictors: AFC.PC1, AFC.PC2, VAS.PC1, VAS.PC2, AXCPT.bin.score, and BDS.level. The 2AFC and VAS predictors are theoretically motivated, while the last two predictors are controlling for attention and working memory. Details of these analyses (and of the data preprocessing steps and preliminary analyses needed to obtain the variables of interest) are outlined in the attached document.

Transformations

For the mixed effects logistic regressions that will be used to obtain the 2AFC outcome variables of interest, the fixed effects will be the first acoustic cue (which varies in 5 steps) and the second acoustic cue (which also varies in 5 steps) for the contrast in question, both of which will be coded as continuous numeric variables and centered. For the primary analyses of interest (canonical correlation, multivariate multiple regression, and multiple regression), all variables (2AFC measures, VAS measures, Oddity scores, BDS scores, and AX-CPT scores) will be standardized by subtracting the mean and dividing by the standard deviation. This will facilitate comparison of their regression coefficients. All predictors for the primary analyses of interest will be visually inspected, and will be transformed if their distributions are clearly skewed (log-transformed if positively skewed, exponentially transformed if negatively skewed).

Inference criteria

For each of our analyses (canonical correlation, multivariate multiple regression, and multiple regression), we will use the standard p &lt; .05 criteria for determining whether the results are significantly different from those expected if the null hypothesis were correct.

Data exclusion

Participants will be excluded from all tasks if they report having had phonetic training or exposure to German. Participants will also be excluded on a task-by-task basis depending on performance-based criteria as outlined below. For the 2AFC and VAS tasks, participants will be excluded if their responses at the two endpoints (the least ambiguous bet/bat and dear/tear stimuli) are &gt; 2 SD from the mean. The endpoint stimuli should be easily and reliably perceived as one word or the other, so responses that differ greatly from the mean would indicate inconsistent and low-effort performance. For the 3-I oddity task, participants will be excluded if their number of “None” responses is &gt; 2 SD from the mean, since this reflects a tendency to repeatedly press the same button without following task instructions. For the AX-CPT task, participants will be excluded if their mean accuracy is &gt; 2 SD below the mean, or if the percentage of their reaction times that’s been altered by the cleaning process during preprocessing is &gt; 2 SD above the mean. These criteria reflect low-effort performance (unusually short or long reaction times, and failure to follow the task instructions). For the backwards digit span task, participants will be excluded if they have a score of 3 or less, since this indicates low-effort performance. Participants who are excluded from 3 or more tasks (based on the criteria above) will be excluded from all tasks, since this suggests low-effort performance throughout the whole study.

Missing data

If a participant has missing data from a given task, they will be excluded from that task. If they have missing data from three or more tasks, they will be excluded from the entire study.

Exploratory analysis

Music experience (based on questionnaire responses) will be taken into account in exploratory analyses, in case it relates to our native and non-native phonetic perception measures.
