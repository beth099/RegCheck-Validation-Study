The Minority-Groups Homogeneity Effect

OSF ID: 27k56 | Registered: 2024-03-12T01:48:30.507535

Study Information

Hypotheses

H1: We hypothesize that the experimental conditions that emphasize or de-emphasize common fate will differ significantly from each other, such that participants rate minority groups as more similar to each other when their common fate is emphasized vs. when it is de-emphasized.

Study design

Participants on Prolific will be invited to participate in a 4-minute survey about social groups. Participants will read about a fictional society called "Bimboola" and the five groups of people in this society - one majority group (the Ackians, who make up over 50% of the population) and four minority groups (the Brites, the Cepians, the Drivians, and the Elies). Participants will be randomly assigned to one of three conditions: 1. Majority group similarity (control) 2. Minority groups similarity (common fate) 3. Minority groups similarity (non-common fate) In the control condition, participants will read additional, basic information about similarities and differences between groups in Bimboola. In the common fate condition, participants will read that the minority groups in Bimboola share a similar history in terms of how they arrived in Bimboola and in terms of their economic and cultural status in modern society. In the non-common fate condition, participants will read that each group in Bimboola has a different history and that the economic and cultural status of groups is not directly related to their status as minority or majority groups. Participants will then go through a learning phase in which they will read 15 statements about actions taken by members of each group (3 actions per group: 2 positive and 1 negative), with group-action pairings randomized between participants. Participants in the majority condition will then rate the similarity of members of the majority group, and participants in the other two conditions will rate the similar of members of the minority groups, on nine items assessing similarity in terms of personality, values, and traits. For example, a question will read: “How similar are [the Ackians (majority group condition)/the Brites, the Cepians, the Drivians, and the Elies (minority groups condition)] to one another in terms of how friendly they are?” After completing this measure, participants will answer a brief manipulation check, complete several demographic questions, and optionally comment on the survey.

Randomization

Participants will be randomly assigned to conditions using the Qualtrics randomization feature (i.e., randomly assigning participants to a condition via embedded data and using that embedded data to show them the appropriate condition block).

Explanation of existing data

Data collection procedures

Participants will be recruited via Prolific. We will compensate participants $0.80 for their participation in this 4-minute survey (a rate of $12/hour). We will restrict our sample to participants who have U.S. nationality, live in the U.S., and have not participated in any of the previous studies for this project.

Sample size

We will recruit 450 participants (150 per condition).

Sample size rationale

In a previous version of this study, the comparison of interest had an effect size of Cohen's d = 0.42. We ran a power calculation in G*Power and determined that we would need 150 participants per cell to be able to detect an effect of this size.

Stopping rule

Aside from occasional errors with online data collection (in which more or fewer people participate than requested), we will have full control over our sample size.

Variables

Manipulated variables

Participants will be assigned to one of three conditions: 1. Majority group similarity - control (rating the similarity of members of the majority group with no additional common fate information) 2. Minority groups similarity - common fate (rating the similarity of members of the minority groups after reading about their shared experiences) 3. Minority groups similarity - non-common fate (rating the similarity of members of the minority groups after reading about their unique experiences)

Measured variables

Similarity measure (9 items). We will measure how similar group members are thought to be in terms of: general similarity, how much they value a sense of community, how much they value personal achievement, how friendly they are, how selfish they are, how helpful they are, how risk-seeking they are, how moral they are, and how rude they are. Manipulation check. We will use a one-item manipulation check asking participants to rate their agreement with the statement: The minority groups in Bimboola share a common fate (i.e., a gain for one of the minority groups is a gain for all of the minority groups).

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

Our primary analysis will be a pairwise contrast comparing the perceived similarity of the minority groups in the common fate and non-common fate conditions. As a secondary analysis, we will also compare the similarity ratings in the two minority conditions to those in the majority condition, expecting to find a difference between the majority and common-fate minority conditions (replicating the minority-groups homogeneity effect), but no difference between the majority and non-common fate minority conditions. Given that our main analyses consist of three pairwise contrasts, we will adjust for multiple comparisons using the Tukey adjustment (via the emmeans R package).

We will use a standard alpha of .05 to make inferences.
