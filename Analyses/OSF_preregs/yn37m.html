Exploring the Role of Empathy in Everyday Interpersonal Emotion Regulation

OSF ID: yn37m | Registered: 2022-02-04T16:52:57.829649

Study Information

Hypotheses

Empathy and EER goal formation: Based on Nozaki and Mikolajczak (2020), we assume theoretically that all empathic components influence EER goal formation. Specifically, we posit the following hypotheses: 1. Based on Batson et al. (1983), Christ et al. (2016), Gülseven et al. (2020), Jiang et al. (2014), Light et al. (2015), Lockwood et al. (2014), and Van der Graaff et al. (2018), we hypothesize that all empathic components will be positively associated with hedonic interpersonal regulation goals (i.e., higher mentalizing, higher empathic concern, higher personal distress, and higher experience sharing will imply more hedonic EER goals). 2. Based on Edele et al. (2013), Lockwood et al. (2014), López-Pérez et al. (2017), and Niven et al. (2019), we hypothesize that empathic concern, mentalizing, and experience sharing will be positively associated with counter-hedonic interpersonal regulation goals (i.e., higher empathic concern, higher mentalizing, and higher experience sharing will imply more counter-hedonic regulation goals). Based on Batson et al. (1983), Batson and Shaw (1991), and López-Pérez et al. (2014), we hypothesize that personal distress will be negatively related to counter-hedonic regulation goals (i.e., higher personal distress will imply less counter-hedonic regulation goals). Empathy and EER strategy selection: Based on Nozaki and Mikolajczak (2020), we assume theoretically that all empathic components influence EER strategy selection. However, we do not posit directional a priori hypotheses for these associations and will conduct exploratory analyses instead.

Study design

Ethical approval for this study was obtained from the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of Yale University (New Haven, Connecticut). This study will use SoSci Survey as an online survey tool to send out questionnaires and surveys to participants. Initially, participants will be screened for conditions that have been found to impair EER processes: Depression, anxiety, and alexithymia. Further, participants will have to have permanent access to the internet via their smartphones and speak English as their native language or with equivalent proficiency to be included in the study. After the screening, participants will provide socio-demographic information, answer three short questionnaires, and will watch a 14-minute video training introducing them to the EMA procedure. Participants will then start the EMA protocol the day after they watched the video training. Signal and event contingent assessments will be used to learn about participants’ social interactions and extrinsic EER behavior. Participants will be alerted to the EMA survey three times a day for a total of ten days, asking them about their social interactions. This results in a maximum number of 30 completed surveys per participant. Social interactions will be defined as any contact with one other person independent of the duration and mode of interaction. These dyadic interactions can include in-person contact, video calls, telephone calls, text messages, and voice messages. The full EMA procedure can be viewed as a separate document on OSF and is explained in more detail in section 15 below. Each daily survey will ask participants first about how many social interactions they have had since their last survey. If participants report more than one interaction, they will be instructed to answer the survey for the one interaction in which their interaction partner was most emotional. Subsequently, participants will report situational characteristics of their social interactions (e.g., closeness to, contact frequency, and relationship with the contact person). Next, participants will rate the extent to which they perceived 16 selected emotions from the Positive and Negative Affect Schedule - Extended Form (PANAS-X; Watson &amp; Clark, 1999) in their interaction partners. Eight of these emotions will correspond to perceived positive affect, while the remaining eight emotions will correspond to perceived negative affect in interaction partners. We will assess the empathic components of experience sharing, mentalizing, personal distress, and empathic concern on an 11-point scale (0 = not at all to 10 = very much). The mentalizing measure was successfully used before in EMA studies by Gilchrist et al. (2019) and Ringwald and Wright (2021). Finally, participants will indicate the extent to which they used 17 EER strategies in their interaction and whether and how they wanted to influence the other persons’ emotions to measure regulation goals. After the study duration period, participants will be thanked for their participation, debriefed, and asked to indicate their preferred payment method.

Data collection procedures

Participants will be recruited via flyers and advertisements on social media and online platforms. Advertisements will invite adults between the ages of 18 and 65 years to participate in the online EMA study I feel you about interpersonal emotion regulation. Participants will be required to have permanent access to the internet via smartphones and speak English as their native language or with equivalent proficiency. Participants will further be screened for clinically relevant levels of depression, anxiety, and alexithymia. Participants will receive a total of $7.50 after completing at least 15 daily surveys. For each additional completed survey, they will earn $0.50 on top, resulting in a maximum amount of $15 per participant. Participants will not receive any participation compensation if they complete less than 15 EMA surveys.

Sample size

Our target sample size is 150 participants. Out of these, every participant must have contributed at least 15 entries (50%) in the EMA protocol. Recruitment will be continued until this criterion is reached.

Measured variables

Screening: ● Anxiety: Beck Anxiety Inventory (BAI; Beck et al., 1988) ○ Participants will be excluded if they report a cumulative score of 26 points or higher indicating clinically relevant levels of anxiety on the BAI. ● Depression: Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9; Kroenke et al., 2001) ○ Participants will be excluded if they report a cumulative score of 14 points or higher indicating clinically relevant levels of depression on the PHQ-9. They will also be excluded from the study if they score higher than 0 on the item assessing suicidal tendencies. If current suicidal tendencies are reported, individuals will be provided with the telephone number and link to the chat service of the National Suicide Prevention Lifeline and the emergency telephone number of North America. ● Alexithymia: Toronto Alexithymia Scale-26 (TAS-26; Taylor et al., 1985) ○ Participants will be excluded if they report a cumulative score of 54 points or higher indicating clinically relevant levels of alexithymia on the TAS-26. T1 Questionnaires &amp; Video Training: ● Socio-demographic Questions: Gender, age, ethnicity, marital status, highest level of obtained education, state of residence, time zone, sexual orientation, current occupation, number of children, current living situation, current and past diagnoses of mental disorders ● Trait Empathy Assessments: ○ Perspective Taking Scale of the Interpersonal Reactivity Index (IRI; Davis, 1980) ○ Toronto Empathy Questionnaire (TEQ; Spreng et al., 2009) ● EER Assessment: ○ Interpersonal Emotion Regulation Questionnaire (IERQ, Hofmann et al., 2016) ● Video Training: A training video for participants can be found as a separate attachment on OSF. EMA Protocol: The full EMA protocol can be viewed as a separate document on OSF. The most important assessments will be explained in more detail below. ● Perceived emotions: Participants will indicate on an 11-point scale (0 = not at all to 10 = very much) the extent to which they perceived 16 selected emotions in their interaction partner as assessed by the PANAS-X (Watson &amp; Clark, 1999). Eight of these emotion items will correspond to perceived positive affect, while the remaining 8 emotion items will correspond to perceived negative affect. ● Empathy: Four components of empathy will be assessed with five items using an 11-point scale (0 = not at all to 10 = very much). Following Gilchrist et al. (2019) and Ringwald and Wright (2021), we will assess mentalizing with two items: The extent to which participants understood (1) the feelings and (2) the thoughts of their interaction partners. Experience sharing will be reported as the extent to which participants shared the emotions of their interaction partners. We will also assess empathic concern (“To what extent did you experience concerned feelings for the other person?”) and personal distress (“To what extent did you feel distressed because of the other person’s emotions?”). With each item, participants will be reminded to think back to their experiences when the situation actually occurred. Higher scores on empathy scales will indicate higher empathy. ● Regulation strategies: Participants will rate for 17 EER strategies the extent to which they employed them in their social interaction on an 11-point scale (0 = not at all to 10 = very much): Distraction, positive reappraisal, cognitive reappraisal, acceptance, soothing, validation, touch, rumination, avoidance, approach, problem solving, support, social modeling, perspective taking, humor, reassurance, and worry. ● Regulation goals: Participants will first report whether they wanted to influence the emotions of the other person (0 = not at all to 10 = very much). If participants answered not at all, their survey will be completed as they did not form a regulation goal. In case they score at least 1 point on the scale, they will be asked about the extent to which they wanted to increase, decrease, or maintain the emotions for positive and negative affect emotion items separately (0 = not at all to 10 = very much). Positive and negative emotion items for this question will only include emotions that were rated 1 or higher on the perceived emotions assessment.

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

We will use the most recent version of R for all analyses. Our data has a nested structure (measurement occasions nested in participants), which is why we will use mixed effects models and other analyses suited for nested data. Analyses for EER goals: - 4 separate mixed effects models: ● Upregulation of positive affect (hedonic regulation goals) ● Upregulation of negative affect (counter-hedonic regulation goals) ● Downregulation of positive affect (counter-hedonic regulation goals) ● Downregulation of negative affect (hedonic regulation goals) - Intended predictors for each of the models: Mentalizing, empathic concern, personal distress, and experience sharing - We will check for multicollinearity by examining correlations between all predictors and Variance Inflation Factors (VIFs). In case correlations and VIFs indicate multicollinearity, we will adjust the models accordingly. - Outcomes: Hedonic and counter-hedonic regulation goals for positive and negative affect respectively - We will use likelihood-ratio tests with model comparisons against a random intercept-only model, different goodness-of-fit indicators, and compute standardized beta coefficients as effect size indicators to evaluate the relationship between empathic components and EER goal formation - Other predictors for exploratory analyses might be used after assessing correlations between the following list of variables and outcomes for control analyses: Relationship closeness, digital vs. in-person interaction, interaction duration, relationship to the interaction partner, gender of interaction partner, participant’s gender, and contact frequency with the interaction partner in the last month Analyses for EER strategy selection - To investigate links between empathic components and strategy selection, we will use the statsBy function in the R package ‘psych’, which decomposes the observed correlations between empathy and strategy use into the within-person and between-person associations. This follows the decomposition of an observed correlation into the pooled correlation within groups and the weighted correlation of the means between groups based on Pedhazur (1997) and Bliese (2016). - To explore potentially confounding factors, partial correlations might be calculated using the following list of variables: Relationship closeness, digital vs. in-person interaction, interaction duration, relationship to the interaction partner, gender of interaction partner, participant’s gender, and contact frequency with the interaction partner in the last month

Transformations

- The following predictor variables will be person-mean centered for level 1 and grand-mean centered for level 2: Mentalizing, empathic concern, experience sharing, and personal distress

Inference criteria

Data exclusion

We will include all data from all participants, even from those that contributed less than 15 EMA surveys (i.e., unpaid participants), unless the following criteria apply. Data will be excluded if the missing data definition applies (see below). Further, all participants will be excluded that contributed less than two EMA surveys in total. Single surveys will be excluded if participants answered them too quickly as determined by the Relative Speed Index (RSI) variable in SoSci Survey. Single participant entries will be excluded if they exhibit an RSI greater than 2. We might also exclude all or single surveys from participants who will indicate that their data cannot be used for scientific purposes at the end of the study. We will perform all analyses with all participants remaining after applying these criteria. Following Grommisch et al. (2020), we will re-run our main analyses excluding participants with compliance rates less than 50%. In case that our results do not differ, we will retain those participants in our analyses.

Missing data

In instances when daily surveys are only partially answered with all answers to the dependent variables missing (i.e., strategy choice or regulation goals), they will not be included in the analyses. If one dependent variable was answered, this survey will be included in the analyses for the completed dependent variable.

Exploratory analysis

We do not posit a priori hypotheses for the associations of the empathic components with EER strategies. These analyses will therefore be exploratory.
