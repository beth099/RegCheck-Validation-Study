Examining the mechanisms underlying action slips: A competition between goal-directed processes

OSF ID: 43xfe | Registered: 2021-04-12T13:00:32.860897 | Showing: original version (1 version(s) on OSF)

Study Information

Hypotheses

Hypothesis 1) We expect a higher proportion of mistakes on reversed compared to unreversed trials on choice trials, and that this difference is bigger for participants in the extensive compared to the minimal condition. Hypothesis 2) We expect a higher proportion of mistakes on reversed compared to unreversed trials on expectancy trials, and that this difference is bigger for participants in the extensive compared to the minimal condition.

Study design

Mixed model design with training (minimal vs. extensive) as between-subjects factor and contingency type (unreversed vs. reversed) and task type (choice vs. expectancy) as within-subjects factor. Acquisition phase At the beginning of the study, participants learn to perform a choice task. Each trial starts with one of four differently colored pairs of doors and participants have to select one of the doors. Depending on which side was chosen, a hand knocks on the door of the corresponding side, the door opens and participants either receive a diamond or a rock. Participants will have to respond within 800ms after the onset of the doors, otherwise the doors disappear and they lose their chance to make any choice. Participants are also told that the goal of this task is to collect as many diamonds as possible and that they may receive a bonus payment at the end of the study if they managed to collect a substantial amount of diamonds. At the start of the study, participants are instructed which response leads to diamonds, given which pair of doors. The assignment, for which pair of doors which response leads to diamonds is randomized. However, the right response as well as the left response leads to diamonds for two pairs of doors for each participant. Training (minimal vs. extensive) At the beginning of the study, participants are randomly assigned to one of two training conditions, a minimal or an extensive training condition. The difference between these two conditions amounts to the number of trials participants have to perform in the acquisition phase. Participants in the minimal training condition perform 5 blocks consisting of 8 trials each on a single day. Participants in the extensive condition perform 5 blocks consisting of 20 trials each on the first three days, and 5 blocks consisting of 8 trials each on the fourth day. Reversal phase Contingency type (unreversed vs. reversed) In the reversal phase, participants perform choice trials, however, this time the correct response to receive diamonds has been reversed for two door colors. Participants are instructed for which pair of doors the responses are reversed and which responses will lead to diamonds from now on. To assure that participants learn the new contingencies correctly there is no response deadline and they will have to perform the task until they have responded correctly for each pair of doors on five consecutive trials. Test phase In the test phase, participants will be asked to perform four blocks with two intermixed task types. In particular, each block consists of 12 choice and 16 expectancy trials. Independent of the task type, each trial starts with one of four differently colored pairs of doors. Choice task On each trial in the choice task, participants can select one of the doors. However, participants will not be presented with the outcomes (but are informed that the outcome is still there and diamonds are counted). After participants made a response, they do not see if they received a diamond or a rock. Expectancy task On each trial in the expectancy task, a hand knocks on one of the doors, the door opens and participants see a question mark instead of a diamond or a rock. Using two dedicated keys participants will have to indicate which outcome they expect to be behind the door that was opened for them. Here as well, participants will have to respond within 500ms after the onset of the doors, otherwise the opened door disappears and they lose their chance to respond. Participants are told that the goal of this task is to respond as accurately and fast as possible.

Randomization

- Participants are randomly assigned to one of two training conditions, a minimal or an extensive training condition. - In each phase and for each task type participants will be presented with each pair of doors equally often, while their order of appearance is randomized. - In the test phase, choice and expectancy trials will appear intermixed. Their order of appearance is randomized.

Explanation of existing data

Due to an error in the auto-save function on OSF, the preregistration for this study was saved and thus preregistered in an incomplete version (see previous preregistration of the same project). Before running the study the preregistration was updated with the following aspects which were not saved properly: a) the payment details were filled in (i.e., how high the compensation for each participant was) b) the response time deadline for expectancy trials was set to 500ms c) the sample size was estimated based on a prior power analysis. d) an exclusion criteria was added, whereby participants needed to achieve a minus accuracy of 60% across all acquisition blocks on the final day. We are entering the preregistration exactly as it was before the error occurred and are therefore declaring the preregistration as a "Registration prior to creation of data“. As the data has been collected by now, the error may result in a mismatch between the time-stamp of the data and the time-stamp of the preregistration. Nevertheless, at this point the data has not been analysed nor inspected, as the error became apparent when we opened the preregistration to start following our preregistered analysis plan. In case the preregistration cannot be declared as a “Registration prior to data creation” due to the time-stamp mismatch, the preregistration can be regarded as a “Registration prior to any human observation of the data".

Data collection procedures

Participants will be recruited online using the participant pool Prolific. Participants in both conditions will be paid 2.50£ for their participation on the last day of the study. Participants in the extensive condition will be paid 1.70£ on each of the first three days. To incentivise performance, all participants will receive an additional bonus payment of 0.30£.

Sample size

Our target sample size is 112. We will recruit participants until we reach 112 participants that fit the exclusion criteria.

Sample size rationale

The MorePower 6.0.4 software was used to calculate the sample size. We need 112 participants to have 80% power given the effect size obtained in a pilot study (partial eta² = .068) with the statistical significance defined at the .05 level to detect an interaction effect between condition (minimal vs. extensive) and contingency type (unreversed vs. reversed).

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

To test Hypothesis 1) We will use a mixed ANOVA to analyze the proportion of mistakes on choice trials with condition (minimal vs. extensive) as between-subjects factor and contingency type (unreversed vs. reversed) as within-subjects factor. We are expecting a 2X2 interaction indicating that the proportion of mistakes on reversed compared to unreversed trials is bigger for participants in the extensive compared to the minimal condition To test Hypothesis 2) We will use a mixed ANOVA to analyze the proportion of mistakes on expectancy trials with condition (minimal vs. extensive) as between-subjects factor and contingency type (unreversed vs. reversed) as within-subjects factor. We are expecting a 2X2 interaction indicating that the proportion of mistakes on reversed compared to unreversed trials is bigger for participants in the extensive compared to the minimal condition For the mixed ANOVAs, the sphericity assumption will be checked with Mauchly's test of sphericity. The Greenhouse-Geisser correction will be applied when the sphericity assumption is violated. Significant interactions will be followed up by pairwise comparisons.

Transformations

Inference criteria

Results of all analyses are assessed for statistical significance based on the standard .05 criterion.

Data exclusion

Participants will be excluded from the analyses if they give less than 60% correct responses across all five blocks of the acquisition phase. They will also be excluded if they have more than 30% late responses on the choice or expectancy task.
