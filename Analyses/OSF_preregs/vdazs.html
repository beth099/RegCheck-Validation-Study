The relationship between implicit associations and the social priming of speech

OSF ID: vdazs | Registered: 2021-07-28T15:40:18.175302

Study Information

Hypotheses

1) Race/ethnicity primes (images of a White, East Asian, vs. Latina woman) will affect the perception of American, native-accented English in babble. Specifically, subjects will have better transcription accuracy for the White prime than the East Asian or the Latina prime (to be examined in two separate within-subject analyses). 2) Individual listeners’ implicit associations will be related to the size of their “social priming costs”. That is, for a given listener, the size of the difference between conditions (e.g., performance following a White versus an Asian prime) may be larger or smaller, and we expect that listeners with larger priming effects will have stronger implicit associations between these races/ethnicities and the constructs American vs. Foreign. We will test this prediction with an interaction (Prime x IAT) for the White vs. East Asian and the White vs. Latina cohorts separately. 3) The relationship between social priming costs and implicit associations with the constructs Good vs. Bad will diverge from the relationship with American vs. Foreign associations. Specifically, we expect that the Good vs. Bad IAT will not significantly predict social priming costs. We do note an additional possibility: It may be the case that the Good vs. Bad IAT predicts social priming costs for the White vs. Latina cohort only (and not the White vs. East Asian cohort). If this is the case, it may reflect the separate dimensions of implicit biases and stereotypes in the United States, which indicate East Asian Americans are viewed as high status but culturally foreign and Latinx Americans are viewed as low status and culturally foreign (Zou &amp; Cheryan, 2017). 4) Explicit measures of listeners' attitudes and affect will also be examined. We predict that affect may be more negative following listening blocks with a minority race prime than a White prime. Additionally, we predict that ratings of status (e.g., intelligence) and solidarity (e.g., friendly) traits will be poorer following listening blocks with a minority race prime. Lastly, we predict that speakers' fluency will be rated poorer for listening blocks with a minority race prime. Altogether, we expect that explicit measures will indicate bias in favor of the (supposedly) White speaker over the (supposedly) East Asian and Latina speakers (examined in separate within-subject analyses). Notably, one issue that may occur is an order effect on attitude measures. That is, after subjects are aware that we are asking questions about the speakers, they may suspect that their racial biases are being examined. Thus, we will explore the effect of counterbalancing and conduct a separate between-subject analysis of just the responses following the first speaker blocks, if necessary (for which the same hypotheses would apply).

Study design

Young adult (18-30) subjects will be recruited from Prolific, and then directed to the study hosted on Gorilla. If subjects consent to participate, they will complete a headphone screening, the speech transcription task, two BIATs (assessing American/Foreign associations and also Good/Bad associations), and an attitudes questionnaire. Headphone screening: The headphone screening was designed by Milne et al. (2020). Subjects are played three bursts of noise, and have to determine which contained a tone. Subjects who fail (and, thus, presumably are not wearing headphones as required for the task) will be rejected from the experiment. Speech transcription task: The speech recognition task will include 40 trials blocked by condition (20 White trials, 20 Latinx or East Asian trials). The presentation order of condition, as well as the combination of each prime and native speaker, will be counterbalanced. Prior to each block, subjects will be shown the prime and told that it is a picture of the speaker they are about to listen to; the prime will remain onscreen during the entire block. Each trial, subjects will listen to the auditory stimuli and type what they heard into a response box. For a small subset of trials, an attention check will be inserted; these catch trials will ask the subject to enter a single letter into the box, and ensure that the online subjects are not making random string entries. IATs: After the listening task, subjects will complete two implicit association tasks (referred to as "picture sorting tasks" in the experiment). These tasks require rapid responses, and instruct subjects to sort faces, images, and/or text. Subjects will complete IATs comparing associations for White and East Asian faces, White and Latinx faces, and the constructs American vs. Foreign, and Good vs. Bad. Questionnaires: After each block, affect and attitudes toward the block's speaker (shown again with the image) will be assessed with rating scale questions. At the end of the task, subjects will answer language and demographic questions. Subjects will also be given the ability to report whether they think that their data should be excluded from the study (i.e., "I was not focused on the task") without it affecting their payment.

Explanation of existing data

Data collection procedures

Inclusion criteria (set via Prolific filters) will limit participation to subjects who are White young adults between 18-35 years old, are native speakers of American English, currently reside in the United States, and have normal hearing and vision (or corrected-to-normal vision). Subjects will be excluded and replaced if: they don't meet inclusion criteria (despite Prolific's pre-screening), they fail the headphone screening task (up to 2 attempts allowed), they self-report using computer speakers instead of headphones, they fail any of the attention check trials in the speech transcription task, they perform 3 SD away from the group average in the speech transcription task (collapsing across conditions), or they self report that their data should be excluded (e.g., "I was too distracted").

Sample size

Up to 800 subjects will be recruited through the online subject recruitment website Prolific. The target sample size for Experiment 2 will be 700 subjects, divided equally between two “cohorts”: East Asian vs. White primes, and Latinx vs. White primes.

Sample size rationale

Based on our experience recruiting subjects through Prolific, we expect approximately 15% attrition due to subjects not meeting inclusion criteria, failing attention checks, or failing to complete the task. The target sample size was determined using the function pwr.r.test() in R, with an estimated effect size of r = .20; this estimate was based on a meta-analysis of prior work in social psychology that has shown effect sizes of approximately r = .20 when examining correlations between IAT scores and intergroup behaviors (Greenwald, Poehlman, Uhlman, &amp; Banaji, 2009). With 350 subjects per cohort, we expect that our analyses will have greater than 95% power; however, if the effect size is smaller than predicted, we will still have 80% power to examine an effect as small as r = .15.

Stopping rule

Only up to 800 subjects will be recruited, due to funding limitations. If the target of 350 valid subjects per cohort is reached before the total subject limit (i.e., including subjects who were paid but then excluded from analyses) is reached, then we will stop data collection.

Variables

Manipulated variables

Measured variables

Transcription accuracy: In the speech perception task, words correctly transcribed will be tallied using Autoscore. This is the primary outcome measure of the experiment. Implicit associations: From the Implicit Association Tests (IATs), we will calculate a d score for each subject. These values will be used as predictors in the models of the speech perception task. We will also examine the raw response time data in a separate model (primarily for reporting purposes). Attitudes and affect: A series of rating scale questions about the target speaker of a given block, and the participant's affect, will be measured. Indices (details below) of these measures (status, solidarity, fluency) will be used as predictors in the speech perception task's models. We will also examine these measures in a separate model in which they are the dependent variable (and prime type is the predictor).

Indices

Affect: 5-point rating scale questions (1 = not at all, 5 = extremely) An index of affect will be created following the steps of Dragojevic &amp; Giles (2016): The three positive emotions (interested, happy, enthusiastic) will be averaged and the three negative emotions (annoyed, irritated, frustrated) will be averaged to form positive and negative affect measures, respectively. An affect balance score will then be calculated by subtracting the mean negative affect from the mean positive affect and adding a constant of 4 (to avoid negative values). Range of the indice will be from 0 (high negative affect) to 8 (high positive affect). Attitudes: 7-point rating scale questions (1 = not at all, 7 = extremely) Indices of subjects' impressions of the speaker will be grouped as: status (intelligent, educated, smart, competent, successful), solidarity (friendly, nice, pleasant, honest, sociable), and fluency (comprehensible, easy to understand, clear to understand, effortful to understand). By-subject averages will be created of the sets of traits.

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

Speech perception task: Two binomial linear mixed-effects models (one per cohort) will be fit to the data in R using the glmer() function from the lme4 package and a logit link function. Transcription accuracy will be treated as a grouped binomial variable. Random effects will include intercepts for subjects and items, and slopes by-subject and by-item for the effect of prime. For both models, we will test the fixed effects of prime, American-Foreign d-score, Good-Bad d-Score, affect, status ratings, solidarity ratings, fluency ratings, and counterbalance, as well as interactions between prime and each additional predictor. If the two IAT measures are highly correlated, they will be examined in separate models to avoid collinearity issues. All effects will be tested with log-likelihood model comparisons. If counterbalance interacts with the effect of prime or the affect and attitudes measures, then additional between-subject models (i.e., across counterbalances) will be constructed to compare only the first block. IATs: Response times in the IATs will be modeled with glmer() (one model per cohort and IAT). Fixed effects will include congruency (where combination of White and American is "congruent" and White and Foreign is "incongruent", and so on) and block order. Random effects will include intercepts by subject. Affect and attitudes: Multiple linear models will be used to examine the effects of prime (i.e., race) and counterbalance on the affect and attitudes (status, solidarity, and fluency) indices. If counterbalance interacts with prime, then additional between-subject models (i.e., across counterbalances) will be constructed to compare only the first block.

Transformations

IAT d-scores will be calculated following Greenwald et al.’s (2003) guidelines. Data is analyzed from Blocks 3, 4, 6, and 7 (all other blocks are for practice). First, trials with latencies greater than 10,000 ms are excluded, and participants with more than 10% of trials with latencies less than 300 ms are removed. The mean latency for correct trials is calculated for each block, and error trial latencies are replaced with these values plus an additional 600 ms. One pooled standard deviation is computed for all trials in Blocks 3 and 6, and another for all trials in Blocks 4 and 7. The average latencies for each block are used to calculate differences between Blocks 3 and 6 and between Blocks 4 and 7 (specifically, later blocks minus earlier blocks). These differences are then divided by their respective pooled standard deviations, and then averaged to obtain a final d value. Blocks 3 and 6 are paired together during this process because they contain the earlier trials of each race-attribute pairing, which typically have slower response times than Blocks 4 and 7. Both group-wide and individual d-scores will be calculated (group-wide for scoring purposes).

Inference criteria

We will use a threshold of p &lt; .05 for statistical significance.

Data exclusion

Subjects who do not meet eligibility criteria will be removed from the analysis. Subjects who do not pass the attention-check questions will be excluded. Subjects who self-report that their data should be excluded will be excluded. Participants will also be excluded from all analyses if in any condition their accuracy at the speech recognition task is worse than three standard deviations below the mean accuracy (across conditions).

Missing data

GLMER and LMER are fairly robust against missing data so we don't anticipate this to be an issue.
