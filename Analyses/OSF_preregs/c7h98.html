Depression, Emotion Socialization and Inter- and Intra-Personal Emotion Regulation in Parent-Adolescent-Dyads

OSF ID: c7h98 | Registered: 2022-07-05T10:40:49.595327

Study Information

Hypotheses

(1) Daily diary-actor (within-person) effects. Participants’ (both parents’ and children’s) own intra- and interpersonal emotion regulation (ER) strategy use will be related their own depressive symptoms. More specifically, we hypothesize that 4.1.1. Higher levels of own depressive symptoms will be positively associated with individuals’ own use of inter-personal and intra-personal maladaptive ER strategies and unsupportive emotion socialization behaviors. 4.1.2. Higher levels of one’s own depressive symptoms will be negatively associated with their own use of inter-personal and intra-personal adaptive ER strategies and supporting emotion socialization behaviors. 4.1.3. Individuals whose ER strategy involves a greater proportion of maladaptive ER (vs. adaptive ER) will tend to have higher depressive symptoms. (2) Daily diary-partner (interpersonal) effects. Participants’ own intra- and interpersonal ER strategy use and emotion socialization behaviors will be related to their dyad partners’ depressive symptoms. More specifically, we hypothesize that the use of more generally maladaptive and less generally adaptive ER strategies, as well as less supportive and more unsupportive emotion socialization behaviors will be related to higher depressive symptoms in the dyad partner. (3) Daily diary: Dyad-level similarity. We hypothesize that there will be a within-dyad positive association in depressive symptoms, mood, and ER strategy use. As stated below, parental depression may moderate this associations (e.g., dyads in which parents have a higher level of depression may be more similar in their depressive symptoms). (4) Daily diary: Empathic accuracy: We hypothesize that one’s own depressive symptoms will be associated with lower levels of empathic accuracy. Additionally, we hypothesize partner’s higher levels of depressive symptoms will be associated with lower levels of empathic accuracy. (5) Behavioral task-actor effects: We hypothesize that higher levels of depression and social anxiety in the background questionnaires will be associated with higher negative interpretation bias and lower positive interpretation bias, as well as less flexibility in revising both negative and positive interpretations. (6) Behavioral task – Dyad level similarity – we hypothesize that there would be a positive association between parent and child interpretation biases/inflexibility

Study design

The present study includes an initial zoom meeting with questionnaires and a behavioral task and a subsequent 28-day daily diary for each participant. Dyad members will be asked to fill out daily questionnaires at the same time, whatever fits them best and closest to their bedtime of the options 7pm, 8pm, 9pm and 10pm.

Data collection procedures

Participants will be recruited via flyers and advertisements on social media and online platforms. Advertisements will invite parents with children between the ages of 12 to 18 to participate in a new online study if their child lives in the same household for at least 50% of the time. Participation will involve a one-time zoom session with initial questionnaires, followed by a 28-day daily diary protocol assessing social situations, emotions, intra- and interpersonal emotion regulation, and depressive symptoms. Dyads can earn up to $170.

Sample size

100 parent-child dyads

Sample size rationale

Power analysis suggest that we need to collect 100 individuals who complete at least 75% of the daily diary, which will allow us to find small effects (d = 0.2) with a power of β = 0.8 as determined by a power analysis for multilevel using the R package EMAtools (Kleiman, 2021). While theoretically each dyad includes 2 individuals, we believe that it is highly likely that some effects will be limited to parents alone or to children alone (e.g., parents are more likely to use interpersonal ER strategies than children). Thus, we will aim to enroll a total of 100 dyads.

Stopping rule

We will continue to onboard dyads until 100 dyads complete the behavioral task and at least 60% of the diary entries.

Indices

(1) In adults, metrics have been derived from the emotional BADE task’s disconfirming-the-negative scenarios using the following formulas: Negative Interpretation Inflexibility (Absurd1+Absurd2+Absurd3+LureA3+LureB3-True3), Positive Interpretation Bias (True1+True2+True3), and Negative Interpretation Bias (LureA1+LureB1+LureA2+LureB2). For adolescents, we plan to capture these metrics using the following formulas: Negative Interpretation Inflexibility (LureA3+LureB3-True3), Negative Interpretation Bias (LureA1+LureA2+LureB1+LureB2), and Positive Interpretation Bias (True1+True2). (2) In adults, metrics have also been derived from the emotional BADE task’s disconfirming-the-positive scenarios using the following formulas: Positive Interpretation Inflexibility: Absurd1+Absurd2+Absurd3+LureA3+LureB3-True3+LureA2+True1. Negative Interpretation Bias: True1+True2+True3. Positive Interpretation Bias: LureA1+LureA2+LureB1+LureB2. In adolescents, the formulas will be: Positive Interpretation Inflexibility (LureA3+LureB3-True3), Positive Interpretation Bias (LureA1+LureA2+LureB1+LureB2), and Negative Interpretation Bias (True1+True2). Note that these formulas differ slightly for adolescents and adults – a choice supported by preliminary data from our first study with the adolescent Emotional BADE Task. However, given that the adolescent task version has only been used once, we will re-evaluate whether the adult strategy is also appropriate in adolescents in the present study, and may switch to a unified approach if this is supported by the data. (3) ER ratio index will be computed as follows (as specified in our prior preregistration): (rumination+dampening+positive suppression+negative suppression+behavioral avoidance)/ (rumination+dampening+positive suppression+negative suppression+behavioral avoidance+acceptance+positive reappraisal+problem solving+distraction+emotion-focused positive rumination+self-focused positive rumination) (4) We will measure a participant’s empathic accuracy at a given time point by calculating the pearson correlation between their PANAS-C reports of their co-participant’s emotions and their co-participant’s PANAS-C self-reported emotions. We will then apply a fisher’s z transformation. As fisher’s z can result in infinite scores, we will standardize values over +3 SD or under -3 SD to +3 SD or -3 SD, respectively (SD calculated when removing infinite values).

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

1. To test the first two hypotheses about daily diary actor and partner effects we will run an Actor-Partner-Interdependence-Model using multilevel modeling. The following models will be used similar to what was used in Gadassi et al., (2016) in the Archives of Sexual Behaviors and Rafaeli et al., (2017) in Motivation and Emotion. All models will be run on person and day level (as in Gadassi et al., 2016; Rafaeli et al., 2017): a. Depressive symptoms ~ Own Interpersonal ERs+Partner Interpersonal ERs+Identity (a variable that denotes if this is a child or parent)+ Own Interpersonal ERs*identity+Partner Interpersonal ERs*Identity *interpersonal ERs here include also emotion socialization behaviors that are not usually considered as ER strategies b. Depressive symptoms ~ Own Intrapersonal ERs+Partner Intrapersonal ERs+Identity + Own Interprasonal ERs*identity+Partner Intrapersonal ERs*Identity ***this model will also be used with the ratio of ERs as a predictor ***Day level models will include as predictors the lagged outcome variable and the mean level of predictors and outcome. Day level predictors will be mean centered. ***All APIM model will include family membership within the random symmetry and the covariate structure will be csh (as recommended by Kenny, Kashy, &amp; Cook, 2006). 2. To test the third and sixth hypotheses regarding within-dyad similarities in mood, depressive symptoms, ER, and BADE we will compute multilevel modeling in which one dyad member’s score is predicted from the other person’s score. Random statement specifying family membership (in the daily models – this would be in addition to specifying days as nested within participants) will be included in the model. 3. To test the fourth hypothesis regarding depression and empathic accuracy we will apply an APIM model on day and person level (as specified in #1): Empathic Accuracy ~ Own depression+Partner depression+Identity + Own depression*identity+Partner depression*Identity 4. To test hypothesis 6 (BADE) we will use multiple regression models: Depression ~ Inflexible Interpretations + Positive Interpretation Bias + Negative Interpretation Bias; Social Anxiety ~ Inflexible Interpretations + Positive Interpretation Bias + Negative Interpretation Bias. We will run separate models for disconfirming-the-positive and disconfirming-the-negative scenarios.

Standard cutoffs will be used (e.g., p &lt; .05 (two tailed), bootstrapped confidence intervals that do not overlap with zero, etc.).

Data exclusion

Data from daily diary will be included if both dyad members complete at least 14 daily diary assessments each.

Exploratory analysis

a. Behavioral task – partner effects: We hypothesize that higher levels of dyad partner’s depression and social anxiety in the background questionnaires would be associated with an actor’s higher negative interpretation bias and lower positive interpretation bias, as well as less flexibility in revising both negative and positive beliefs. We expect the effects of parent depression on the child would be stronger than effects of child depression on the parent. b. Behavioral task – dyad level similarity: We will examine if parent and child flexibility in revising positive and negative interpretations are positively associated. c. Parent and child gender and age will be examined as possible moderators of all abovementioned hypotheses. Specifically we hypothesize that parents’ effects on their children will be stronger than children’s effects on their parents, and that parents will endorse interpersonal ER and emotion socialization items more than children. d. If parental depression history (measured by the IDD-L) shows sufficient variance (i.e., if at least 35% of the parents will have an IDD-L score over 25), we will examine parental depression history as a moderator of all abovementioned hypotheses. e. Based on the diary data, we will examine if within individuals there are associations between interpersonal and intrapersonal emotion regulation strategies
