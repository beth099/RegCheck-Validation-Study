Types of Social Media Use in Young Adults: Associations with Momentary and Long-Term Emotion

OSF ID: 8qhjt | Registered: 2022-04-12T23:36:42.814587

Study Information

Hypotheses

1a. Momentary image-based social media (SM) use will be associated with increased momentary positive affect (PA) and will not be associated with momentary negative affect (NA). 1b. Momentary comparison-based SM use will be associated with increased momentary negative affect and decreased momentary PA. 1c. Momentary belief-based SM use will be associated with decreased momentary NA and will not be associated with momentary PA. 1d. Momentary consumption-based SM use will be associated with increased momentary PA and decreased momentary NA. 2a. Comparison-based SM use will be associated with the greatest increase in NA compared to any of the other three types of use. 2b. Consumption-based SM use will be associated with the greatest increase in PA compared to any of the other three types of use. 3a. Image-based SM use tendency will be positively associated with trait-level NA and will not be associated with trait-level PA. 3b. Comparison-based SM use tendency will be positively associated with trait-level NA and negatively associated with trait-level PA. 3c. Belief-based SM use tendency will be positively associated with trait-level NA and will not be associated with trait-level PA. 3d. Consumption-based SM use will be negatively associated with trait-level NA and positively associated with trait-level PA. 4a. Comparison-based SM use tendency will be associated with the greatest trait-level NA, even after accounting for the other three types of SM use. 4b. Consumption-based SM use tendency will be associated with the greatest trait-level PA, even after accounting for the other three types of SM use.

Explanation of existing data

Data collection procedures

Data will be collected from undergraduate students. Students will learn about the study on an online portal that lists active studies that students can complete for course credit. The entire study will be administered online. Anticipated specific data exclusion criteria: a. missing, erroneous, or overly consistent responses b. failing an audio check

Sample size

We will recruit a minimum of 137 participants.

Sample size rationale

A power analysis was completed in G*Power for within-person analyses assessing differences between four conditions with a small effect size (.20) at a power of .80. This revealed a total N of 124. We anticipate 10% attrition, and consequently plan to recruit a minimum of 137 participants.

Stopping rule

Data collection will terminate after April 29th, 2022, the last day of the spring semester, assuming we can collect the full sample by then. If we do not recruit the full sample, then we will re-open the study in the Fall 2022 semester.

Variables

Manipulated variables

We will manipulate the ways in which participants engage in social media. Specifically, participants will be instructed to use their social media four times, and each time, they are told to engage in only specific activities. Instructed activities for image-based SM use: ● Make/share a post or story about something positive that is personally about you ● Look at how many people liked, commented on, shared your content, or followed/friended you ● Read comments to your own content ● Edit and/or delete your own social media content ● Play with photo filtering/photo editing Instructed activities for comparison-based SM use: ● Compare your body or appearance to others' ● Compare your life or experiences to others' ● Reminisce about the past Instructed activities for belief-based SM use: ● Make/share a post or story about something negative that is personally about you ● Make/share a post or story about something negative that is NOT personally about you ● Make/share a post or story about something positive that is NOT personally about you ● Comment unsupportively or dislike/"react" unsupportively on other's post(s) ● Seek out content that you morally or ethically disagree with Instructed activities for consumption-based SM use: ● Scroll aimlessly through your feed(s) ● Look at others’ stories ● Navigate to others' profiles in your social network (e.g., friends or friends of friends) ● Navigate to others' pages who you do not know (e.g., influencers or other famous people) ● Watch videos such as memes, news content, how-tos/recipes, etc.

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

1. To determine how momentary engagement in each type of SM use (IV) is associated with changes in emotion, we will conduct eight paired sample t-tests in which (1) NA and (2) PA pre-SM use and post-SM use are compared for each of the four types of use. 2. To determine whether each of the four types of SM use are significantly different from one another with regard to changes in emotion, we will calculate the change in NA (DV) and PA (DV) for each type of SM use by subtracting NA and PA post-SM use from NA and PA (respectively) pre-SM use for each participant. We will conduct a multivariate analysis of variance (MANOVA) to determine if mean changes in NA and PA across the four types of SM use are significantly different from one another. If the MANOVA yields a significant effect (p &lt; .05), we will conduct a linear discriminant analysis to see differences between each type of SM use. 3. To determine how tendency to engage in each type of SM use (IV) is associated with trait-level NA (DV) and PA (DV), we will first conduct Pearson correlations between trait-level NA and PA and each of the four types of SM use. 4. We will then conduct two linear models in which NA and PA are each predicted by the four types of SM use simultaneously to determine which types of SM use are significantly uniquely associated with NA and PA.
