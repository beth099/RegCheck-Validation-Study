Discrediting Vaccine Disinformation Sources

OSF ID: 7bjre | Registered: 2022-10-17T14:28:58.148271 | Showing: original version (1 version(s) on OSF)

Study Information

Hypotheses

(1) All interventions will reduce (a) belief in disinformation, and (b) source credibility more than the control condition. (2) After a one-week period, significant differences will remain between the control condition and all intervention conditions for (a) belief in disinformation and (b) source credibility. (3) We hypothesize that the conflicts of interest intervention will reduce belief in disinformation more than the other interventions. (4) We hypothesize that (a) there will be no difference between the low expertise condition and correcting disinformation at reducing disinformation belief, and (b) the low expertise condition will be better than the correcting disinformation condition at reducing source credibility. (5) Participants’ belief in disinformation will be correlated with (a) CAMBI scores such that higher CAMBI scores will be associated with higher levels of belief, and (b) vaccine hesitancy, such that higher vaccine hesitancy will be associated with higher levels of belief.

Study design

Our study will be a 3 × 4 between-within design, with the within-subjects factor pre-post intervention (pre vs. immediate post vs. one-week post), and the between-subjects factor intervention (correcting disinformation vs. low-expertise vs. conflict of interest vs. control).

Explanation of existing data

Data collection procedures

Data will be collected using Prolific Academic. In order to recruit people with a range of vaccine opinions we will recruit 50% of participants who reported feeling negatively about the vaccine and 50% participants that felt positively about the vaccine. Exclusion criteria will include participants who complete the task too quickly (i.e. outliers with regards to completion time), participants who indicate that their data should be excluded due to lack of effort, or if participants do not complete the delayed post-test.

Sample size

Our sample size will be 800 people.

Sample size rationale

The current study is a partial replication of Swire-Thompson et al. (2022). We boost our sample from 600 to 800 participants to allow for attrition given it is a two wave study, and to allow for subgroup analysis of people who are vaccine hesitant.

Stopping rule

Variables

Manipulated variables

​​(1) Within-subjects factor pre/post (pre vs. immediately post vs. one-week post) (2) Between-subjects factor intervention (correcting disinformation vs. low-expertise vs. conflict of interest vs. control).

Measured variables

We will measure (1) source credibility, (2) belief in disinformation, (3) vaccine hesitancy, (4) complementary and alternative medicine belief, and (5) demographics.

Indices

(1) Source credibility will be measured using a 12-item composite. This included two questions regarding general credibility, plus Ohanian’s (1990) semantic scale. This has five pairs of adjectives for both expertise (expert, experienced, knowledgeable, qualified, and skilled), and five pairs for trustworthiness (dependable, honest, reliable, sincere, and trustworthy). (2) Belief in Disinformation will be measured using a 7-point likert scale and asked “If Dr. Davis made the following statements, how much would you believe the following to be true? Four disinformation statements regarding vaccines will be provided. (3) Vaccine Hesitancy Will be measured using the Vaccine Hesitancy scale (Thaker, 2021). (4) Complementary and alternative medicines beliefs will be measured using the CAMBI (complementary and alternative medicine beliefs inventory) which has 17 items, and includes three subscales, participation in treatment, natural treatment, and holistic health. (5) Demographics of age, gender, education level, and political orientation will be measured.

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

(1) In order to test hypothesis 1a, 2a, 3, and 4a, we will conduct a 3 × 4 repeated measures ANOVA with the variables pre/post (pre vs. immediate post vs. one week-post) and intervention (correcting disinformation, low-expertise, conflicts of interest, control) on the disinformation composite. We will follow this up with 3 x 3 repeated measures ANOVA with the interventions only to see whether the interventions differed from one another. Planned comparisons between post-test disinformation interventions will be conducted. (2) In order to test hypothesis 1b, 2b, 3, and 4b, we will conduct a 3 × 4 repeated measures ANOVAs with the variables pre/post (pre vs. immediate post vs. one week-post) and intervention (correcting disinformation, low-expertise, conflicts of interest, control) on the source credibility composite. We will follow this up with 3 x 3 repeated measures ANOVA with the interventions only to see whether the interventions differed from one another. Planned comparisons between post-test disinformation interventions will be conducted. (3) In order to test hypothesis 5a and 5b, we will correlate disinformation beliefs with (a) CAMBI scores and (b) vaccines hesitancy scores. With regards to the CAMBI, if only subscales of "natural treatment" and "holistic health" are correlated, we will create a new composite excluding the "participation in treatment" subscale (CAMBINH) to conduct these correlations.
