Study Information

Hypotheses

In Experiment 2, we will examine the incremental processing proposal by testing Chinese-English bilingual speakers with disyllabic words and by comparing the magnitude of phonological competition effects from different locations (the first character versus second character) of disyllabic words.

Explanation of existing data

Data collection procedures

Forty-five undergraduate students from Chung Yuan Christian University in Taiwan will participate in this experiment. They will be paid 150 Taiwan dollars for participation. All will be native Mandarin Chinese-English speakers with normal or corrected-to-normal vision. They spoke Chinese as a first language and English as a second language. The participants will be also asked to finish the Language Experience and Proficiency Questionnaire (LEAP-Q) (Marian, Blumenfeld, &amp; Kaushanskaya, 2007) that measured their experience and proficiency in their L1 and L2 languages.

Our target sample size is 40 participants. We will attempt to recruit up to 45.

Variables

Manipulated variables

We will manipulate the position of competitors: The competitors in the set either shared the same phonological units in the first character position with their targets or in the second-character position with their targets. In addition, for each of targets, two types of competitors and two types of controls will be selected: (a) a CV competitor (e.g., 組合 /zu3he2/, combination), which shared a atonal syllable (same segmental composition but different tone) with the spoken target (足球 /zu2qiu2/, soccer) in one character position, but differed a syllable in the other character position; (b) an C (onset) competitor (早點 /zao3dian3/, breakfast), which shared onset consonant(s) with the target in one character position, but differed other phonemes in the other character position; (c) a CV control (理想 /li3xiang3/, ambition), which did not share any atonal syllables with the target word in both two characters’ positions; (d) a C (onset) control (染髮 /ran3fa3/, hair coloring), which did not share any onset consonant(s) with the target word in both two characters’ positions. The competitors will be neither semantically unrelated nor orthographically unrelated to the target. Moreover, the controls will be not semantically, orthographically, orthographically related to the target.

Measured variables

Two types of outcome variables will be collected and analyzed: (1) spatial data: maximum deviation (MD) and area under the curve (AUC) values and (2) response data: total response time (TRT), movement time (MT) , and errors.
