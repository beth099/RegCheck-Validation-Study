Further investigating boundary conditions of directed forgetting in WM: The role of the stimulus-cue interval for item memory

OSF ID: hkc4u | Registered: 2021-02-26T12:50:33.474265 | Showing: original version (1 version(s) on OSF)

Study Information

Hypotheses

Note the four different set-size conditions (also see the Study Design for further details): - Set-size 6: always six TBR words (large set-size) - Set-size Y: Y = 2, 3, or 4 TBR words (small set-size) - Set-size 6-X remember: 6-2 (4 TBR and 2 TBF words), 6-3 (3 TBR and 3 TBF words), or 6-4 (2 TBR and 4 TBF words); TBR are tested - Set-size 6-3 forget: 3 TBR and 3 TBF words; TBF are tested We will evaluate differences between specific set-size conditions (Y vs. 6 vs. 6-X remember vs. 6-3 forget) for the following: First, we will investigate participants’ likelihood to select an old over a new word at test [p(old)] and second, given participants selected an old word, we will evaluate participants’ likelihood to correctly identify a match over a mismatch [p(match|old)]. At the same time, we will investigate how the effects between set-sizes change depending on the SCI interval (0s, 1s, or 5s). In addition, we will investigate participants’ response times (RTs). Note that we aim to test all our hypotheses within a Bayesian framework. For all following predictions, we aim to assess Bayes Factors (BFs) of two competing hypotheses: one that assumes an effect in the direction of our predictions, and another that does not assume such an effect. In the following, for reasons of brevity, we will only state one hypothesis but in fact we will test two competing hypotheses against each other. We generally expect that for TBR tests, participants should be able to discriminate between old and new words with great success (p(old) should be high in set-size 6, set-size Y, and set-size 6-X remember conditions). Thus, for the TBR tests, we will focus our analyses on participants’ discrimination performance between old match and mismatch words: T1, main effect of set-size, TBR words only - p(match|old): We will test whether the likelihood to select a match word over a mismatch word differs between the (T1a) set-size 6-X remember condition and the set-size 6 baseline as well as between the (T1b) set-size 6-X remember condition than in the set-size Y baseline. T2, interaction between set-size and SCI (0s vs. 1s): TBR words only - p(match|old): We will test whether the magnitude of the set-size effect (T1; between a) set-size 6-X remember vs set-size 6 and b) set-size 6-X remember vs set-size Y) differs between a SCI of 0s and a SCI of 1s. T3, interaction between set-size and SCI (1s vs. 5s): TBR words only - p(match|old): We will test whether the magnitude of the set-size effect (T1; between a) set-size 6-X remember vs set-size 6 and b) set-size 6-X remember vs set-size Y) differs between a SCI of 1s and a SCI of 5s. One of our main interests lies in the availability of TBF items at test and thus the investigation of performance in TBF test trials. For this, we will first directly test whether item memory as well as binding memory for TBF words depends on the SCI: T3, TBF words only, SCI (0s vs. 1s) - p(old): We will test whether the likelihood to select an old word over a new word differs between a SCI of 0s and a SCI of 1s. T4, TBF words only, SCI (1s vs. 5s) - p(old): We will test whether the likelihood to select an old word over a new word differs between a SCI of 1s and a SCI of 5s. T5, TBF words only, SCI (0s vs. 1s) - p(match|old): We will test whether the likelihood to select a match word over a mismatch word differs between a SCI of 0s and a SCI of 1s. T6, TBF words only, SCI (1s vs. 5s) - p(match|old): We will test whether the likelihood to select a match word over a mismatch word differs between a SCI of 1s and a SCI of 5s. In addition, we will make comparisons between the following set-size conditions: T7, main effect set-size - p(old): We will test whether the likelihood to select an old word over a new word differs between a) the set-size 6-3 forget condition and the set-size 6-3 remember condition as well as between the b) the set-size 6-3 forget condition and the set-size 6 condition. T8, interaction between set-size and SCI (0s vs. 1s) - p(old): We will test whether the magnitude of the set-size effect (T7, between a) set-size 6-3 forget and the set-size 6-3 remember and b) set-size 6-3 forget and the set-size) differs between a SCI of 0s and a SCI of 1s. T9, interaction between set-size and SCI (1s vs. 5s) - p(old): We will test whether the magnitude of the set-size effect (T7, between a) set-size 6-3 forget and the set-size 6-3 remember and b) set-size 6-3 forget and the set-size) differs between a SCI of 1s and a SCI of 5s. T10, main effect set-size - p(match|old): We will test whether the likelihood to select a match word over a mismatch word differs between a) the set-size 6-3 forget condition and the set-size 6-3 remember condition as well as between the b) the set-size 6-3 forget condition and the set-size 6 condition. T11, interaction between set-size and SCI (0s vs. 1s), p(match|old): We will test whether the magnitude of the set-size effect (T10, between a) set-size 6-3 forget and the set-size 6-3 remember and b) set-size 6-3 forget and the set-size) differs between a SCI of 0s and a SCI of 1s. T12, interaction between set-size and SCI (1s vs. 5s), p(match|old): We will test whether the magnitude of the set-size effect (T10, between a) set-size 6-3 forget and the set-size 6-3 remember and b) set-size 6-3 forget and the set-size) differs between a SCI of 1s and a SCI of 5s.

Study design

Stimuli The experiment will be conducted in English as an Online-Experiment. Frequent English nouns with a length of 4-5 letters serve as stimuli. For every participant 462 words are randomly drawn from a pool of 600 words. The participants’ task is to remember the stimuli and recognize them in a three-alternative-forced choice (3AFC) test using the keys (1), (2), and (3) on their keyboard. Procedure After providing informed consent, instructions will be given on screen and are summarized prior to the beginning of a block. The instructions will stress to remember only the TBR words for the subsequent recognition test and that they should try to forget the TBF words. We will inform participants that on very few occasions their memory for TBF words will be tested. In such cases, participants are instructed to do their best to remember the TBF words. Participants are told that they should nevertheless truly try to forget all items in an orange frame, as this will make it easier for them to remember the TBR items, and therefore put them – on average – at an advantage. Participants will be instructed not to use any external help (e.g., use pen and paper) and to respond as quickly and accurately as possible. Following the instructions, participants have the chance to practice their task (3 set-size Y trials, 3 set-size 6 trials, and 3 set-size 6-X remember trials in random order; see below). In the practice block, presentation order of trials will be random, and participants will receive feedback (“correct”/ “incorrect”). They will receive no feedback on their performance for the remainder of the experiment. After the practice block, participants will have the option to reread the instructions and redo the practice block or proceed with the experiment. Next, the main experimental block will follow. The trial structure will be as follows: Participants will first see six squared frames arranged on a circle (60° apart from each other). Frames will have thin black outlines and will be presented on a white background. After presentation of all six, empty frames for 1s, six words will be displayed one by one for 1s each in a clockwise order across a circular arrangement of frames starting with left top one at 10 o’clock. Following each word offset, all frames remain empty for either 0s, 1s, or 5s depending on the current SCI condition. After the SCI, the frame will turn either blue or dark orange for 0.5s indicating a to-be-remembered (TBR) or to-be-forgotten (TBF) word, respectively. Next, all frames remain empty for 0.5s before the next word is presented in the next frame. After the last word, and a retention interval of 1s, a 3AFC test probes participants’ memory. For this, in one of the TBR frames, three test words will be presented: (1) one word will be the same as the one originally presented in the frame (“old-match”), (2) another word will be another word previously presented in one of the other TBR frames (“old-mismatch”), (3) and one word will be a completely new word (“new”). On three trials, the 3AFC test will be presented in one of the TBF frames to test participants’ memory of one randomly selected TBF word. Using the keys [1],[2], and [3] participants task is to select the word that was the same as the one originally presented in the frame (“old-match”). ---- Trial types / Set-size conditions (within participants) ---- There will be four trial types (set-size 6; set-size Y; set-size 6-X remember; set-size 6-3 forget). In the set-size 6 condition, participants are instructed to remember all six words. On those trials, every word will be followed by a remember instruction (blue frame) and the 3AFC test words will be presented in one randomly selected frame from all 6 TBR frames. For the set-size Y condition, Y (= 2, 3, or 4) randomly chosen words will be replaced by “XXXX”, each displayed in the same way as the words. “XXXX”-stimuli are always followed by an orange TBF frame. The other words will be followed by a blue TBR frame. The 3AFC test words will be presented in one randomly selected TBR frame. In the set-size 6-X condition, X (= 4, 3, or 2) randomly chosen words will be followed by a blue TBR frame, and the other 6-X words by an orange TBF frame. For set-size 6-X remember trials, the 3AFC test words will be presented in one randomly selected TBR frame. For set-size 6-3 forget trials, the 3AFC test words will be presented in one randomly selected TBF frame. Using post-experimental questions, we will check whether participants had any suspicion about the purpose of the experiment or the directed forgetting instruction. We will take special care to additionally check whether participants correctly understood the task by asking them about their comprehension of the instructions and the task. Specifically, we will ask what they were supposed to do and what they actually did when they saw the blue and orange frame. Participants will state if and (if so) how often they noticed TBF words to be tested. They will also describe what they thought they were supposed to do when TBF words were tested and if they tried to respond correctly on those trials. We will additionally assess how they responded on TBF tests using a Multiple-Choice question. Furthermore, we will assess how strongly participants tried to remember/ forget the words on Likert scales. Last, we will ask them whether they used external help to remember the words. Participants will be properly debriefed about the reason behind testing both TBR and TBF stimuli.

Randomization

The presentation order of the 3AFC test words within a frame is completely at random. The randomization of conditions and stimuli is as follows: First, for each participant, 462 words are randomly drawn from the set of 600 words. Second, the order of all following trials is shuffled randomly for each participant: - 18 trials for set-size Y: there are 6 trials for each SCI level. For each SCI level, there are 2 trials for each Y. - 18 set-size 6 trials: 6 trials for each SCI level - 18 set-size 6-X remember trials: 6 trials for each SCI level; for each SCI level, there are 2 trials for each X. In addition, there are 3 set-size 6-3 forget trials (1 trial for each SCI level). The three set-size 6-3 forget trials are randomly assigned to either the first (trial 16), second (trial 36), or third (trial 56) position. To reduce the chance that the trial with TBF test can be predicted, these trial positions will be shuffled by adding ± 1 trial at random. Words are randomly assigned to the different trials (6 + 1 new test word per trial)

Explanation of existing data

Data collection procedures

Participants will be recruited via an international web-based participation platform. In exchange for their participation, participants will receive monetary compensation. We will only recruit participants that have normal or corrected-to-normal vision, are aged between 18-35 years, and whose mother tongue is English. We will only recruit participants that did not participate in our previous studies on directed forgetting.

Sample size

NMin =400, NMax =600 (see below)

Sample size rationale

We need relatively large samples because we are interested in recognition accuracy of TBF items, which are rare in this design. The sample size is an informed guess based on experience with similar experiments. As we will use Bayesian analyses, we plan to increase the sample size if we find that some hypothesis tests are ambiguous (i.e., 1/3 &lt; BF &lt; 3).

Stopping rule

BF for hypothesis tests outside the ambiguous range, or NMax =600 is reached.

Variables

Manipulated variables

- Set-size (set-size Y, set-size 6, set-size 6-X remember, vs. set-size 6-3 forget) manipulated within participants - SCI (0s, 1s, vs. 5s) manipulated within participants

Measured variables

For each reaction on the 3AFC test, we will measure reaction times and participants’ key press. In particular, we will measure whether participants (1) correctly selected one of the old words over the new word and (2) given that they selected an old word, we will record whether they selected correctly the match word (word presented previously in the tested frame) over the mismatch (word previously presented in another frame) word. Furthermore, we will measure the following variables, but they will not be used for confirmatory hypothesis testing: the order/location of presented stimuli, the location of the 3AFC test, and trial sequence. In addition, we will collect some demographic data (e.g., age, gender, education, profession, etc.) and information on participants’ suspicion about the forget manipulation and their motivation at the end of the experiment. However, we will not use these measures in confirmatory analyses. Last, using post-experimental questions, we will assess whether participants had any suspicion about the purpose of the experiment (see Procedure).

Indices

For descriptive statistics, accuracy [both for p(old) as well as for p(match|old)] and RT will be aggregated over SCI levels and set-size conditions (also over probe positions) after applying outlier exclusion criteria (see corresponding section). For statistical models, the participants’ responses will not be aggregated as data from each individual trial will be submitted directly to the analysis (after applying exclusion criteria).

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

All analyses described below will be conducted using mixed-effects models in a Bayesian framework implemented in the R package brms (Bürkner, 2017), which provides an interface to Stan (Carpenter et al., 2017). Whenever not explicitly stated otherwise, the default settings of the brms package will be used. We will estimate the posteriors by sampling parameter values proportional to the product of prior and likelihood using the No-U-Turn Sampler in congestion with Hamilton Monte Carlo as implemented in Stan. We will sample generations through 4 independent Markov chains with 50,000 iterations each (2,000 warm up). To investigate convergence, we will inspect the Rhat values (&lt;1.05 for all parameters). All categorical predictors will be coded as sum-to-zero contrasts. All continuous predictors will be mean-centered. For all models, the random effects structure for our design will include random intercepts for participants only. For all accuracy models, we will fit Bayesian generalized linear mixed models on the correctness of participants’ responses in a given trial assuming a Bernoulli data distribution predicted by a linear model through a logistic link function. For these models, we will give the regression coefficients moderately informative Cauchy priors with a scale of 0.353 (recently proposed default priors for logistic models; Oberauer, 2019). For all models reported below, we will calculate Bayes Factors (BFs) to estimate the strength of evidence for the null and the alternative hypothesis. To this end, we will fit two competing, nested models for each hypothesis listed above; one including an effect (main effect or interaction) of interest and one that does not include this effect. We will then compare the two models to calculate the evidence for the effect. For the sakes of brevity, we will not always report both models but only the full model including all predictors of interest. T1, T2, T3 (exclusion of all set-size 6-3 forget trials and trials where the “new” word was selected as response): For the analyses of p(match|old), we will fit Bayesian generalized linear mixed models on the participants’ response correctness (1 = old-match word selected vs. 0 = old-mismatch word selected): One set of models will compare the set-size 6–X remember condition to the set-size 6 baseline (a); the other will compare the set-size 6–X remember condition to the set-size Y baseline (b). The models will include the fixed factors set-size (a: 6-X remember vs. 6; b: 6-X remember vs. Y) and SCI (T2: 0s vs 1s, T3: 1s vs 5s) as well as their interaction. To control for effects of learning and/or fatigue throughout the experiment, we will include the continuous predictor trial (trial number of a given trial) in our model. In addition, we will add the number of TBR items in a given trial (corresponding to 6-X and Y), thus the TBR set-size, as a predictor to our models. For T2 and T3, we will test whether a model including the interaction between set-size and SCI is superior to a model without that interaction. For T1, we will test whether a model including the factor set-size is superior to a model without that factor. Transformations

We will log-transform RTs.

Inference criteria

We will report Bayes factors, as well as the mean and the credible interval of the mean of the effect-size parameters posteriors. A BF10 larger than 1 will be considered evidence for an effect, a BF10 lower than 1 will be interpreted as evidence against an effect. Conventions suggest that BFs &gt; 3 can be considered as substantial evidence for one hypothesis over the other.

Data exclusion

The following exclusion criteria on a participant level (participants who will be completely excluded from the analysis) will be applied: First, participants who report that they did not follow the instructions will not be considered in the analyses. Second, participants who state that they did not participate seriously in the experiment or do not agree that we can use their data will be excluded. Third, participants that experience technical issue will be excluded. Fourth, participants who fail to answer correctly to one attention check that is presented within the instruction will be excluded: The attention check is presented at the beginning of the experiment and asks participants to select a certain option to an easy question. In case the participants fail the attention check, they are excluded from the analyses. Fourth, participants who indicate that they used paper and pen to remember the words will be excluded. Fifth, participants with a mean error rate (old vs. new selection) higher than 30% in the set-size Y condition will not be considered. Sixth, using post-experiment questions, we will check whether participants had any suspicion about the purpose of the experiment, read the instructions carefully, and tried to respond correctly on TBF test trials. Participants who cannot correctly state what they were supposed to do when the frames turned blue/orange, and/or did not try to respond correctly on TBF test trials will be excluded. Last, participants missing &gt; 20% of all trials (due to the exclusion criteria below), will be excluded.

Missing data

Participants who do not complete the whole experiment will be excluded.
