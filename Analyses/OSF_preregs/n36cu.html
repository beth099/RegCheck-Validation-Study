Enhancing Autobiographical Memories in Daily Life

OSF ID: n36cu | Registered: 2019-11-05T16:42:11.674972 | Showing: original version (1 version(s) on OSF)

Study Information

Title

Enhancing autobiographical AMs in daily life

Authors

Christina Haag

Description

Positive autobiographical memories (AMs) have the potential to repair sad mood, but depressed individuals have difficulties in accessing their positive self-affirming AMs. Even if they manage to access their positive AMs, these are often diminished in the fashion in that they are brought to mind (Dalgleish &amp; Werner-Seidler, 2014). Previous research has established that individuals in remission from chronic depression can successfully use a preselected set of positive self-affirming AMs in the service of mood repair through using a mnemonic technique in their daily life (Werner-Seidler &amp; Dalgleish, 2016). That positive self-affirming AMs are perceived as self-concordant has been identified as being key for their beneficial impact on mood (Werner-Seidler, Tan, &amp; Dalgleish, 2017). In the present study we seek to train previously depressed individuals to counteract low or dipping mood in daily life through recollecting AMs of a pre-selected AM repository in a vivid and imagery-rich fashion using experience sampling methodology (ESM).

Hypotheses

Primary hypothesis (hypothesis 1): We hypothesise that participants in the positive self-affirming AM condition will experience a greater intraindividual increase in momentary happiness from pre to post AM recollection compared to individuals in the neutral condition during the practice period. However, we expect an intraindividual reduction of sadness in both the positive and neutral AM conditions, since neutral AMs may have a distracting effect. We will assess whether this effect strengthens over the course of the training period, such that participants experience an increase in momentary emotional benefit following AM recollection in terms of (1) increased happiness and (2) decreased sadness over the course of the study. We will further determine whether momentary happiness and sadness are impacted by baseline wellbeing and depressive symptoms. Secondary hypothesis (hypothesis 2): We hypothesise that an independent set of 6 positive self-affirming AMs retrieved during the second laboratory session will on average be more positive in valence, more happy or satisfying upon recall, more vivid, and more self-concordant for individuals in the positive self-affirming AM condition (that is, that effects of training will generalise to positive memories that are not practiced) whereby controlling for the amount of practice with the diary. Feasibility (hypothesis 3): In the follow-up period, we expect that individuals in the positive AM condition will use the AM diary more often spontaneously. We will explore whether this effect is stronger for those who experience a greater improvement in momentary happiness and greater decrease in momentary sadness during the training period (i.e., a moderation effect). Exploratory: To determine the potential for a future relapse prevention study, we will explore whether the positive self-affirming AM group experiences an increase in wellbeing, relative to the neutral condition, from pre to post and follow-up assessments. We will also evaluate effects on residual depressive symptoms. We will further explore whether a potential increase in wellbeing and a decrease in depressive symptoms over the course of the study is mediated by the average experienced emotional benefits in happiness and sadness.

Study design

Following randomisation to either the a) positive self-affirming AM condition or b) neutral AM condition, participants will complete a laboratory assessment session, followed by a one-week home-based training period during which ESM assessments will be completed, a post-training telephone assessment, a two-week follow-up period during which ESM assessments will be completed, and a final laboratory-based assessment. In an initial laboratory session, study participants will fill in baseline questionnaires and compile and rate a pool of 6 positive self-affirming or neutral AMs, respectively, that they would like to access at times of low mood. Participants will then receive a detailed introduction on the use of an AM diary, which will be used over the following one-week ESM practice period. Following the ESM practice period, an intermediate assessment comprising a phone call and online questionnaires will take place. This will be ensued by a two-week ESM follow-up period. At the end of that period, they will be invited for a second laboratory session where they will fill in again questionnaires. Further participants will again provide ratings on their preselected AM pool and rate 6 new positive self-affirming AMs.

Randomization

Participants will be randomly allocated either to a positive self-affirming AM or a neutral AM condition.

Explanation of existing data

Data collection procedures

Sampling plan (see also section study design) Study participants will individuals with a past diagnosis of a depressive disorder and will be recruited from the MRC Cognition and Brain Sciences Unit panel of research volunteers. The panel is comprised of approximately 500 individuals who have responded to print and online advertisements requesting volunteers to participate in research at the MRC Cognition and Brain Sciences Unit. History of depression and current diagnostic status of recovery will be determined using the Structured Clinical Interview for DSM Disorders - Fifth edition. Exclusion criteria are current Major Depressive Episode, intellectual disability, traumatic brain injury, or current substance/alcohol use disorder. All testing sessions are to be held in a quiet testing room at the MRC Cognition and Brain Sciences Unit, following the provision of written informed consent. ESM data collection procedure 1. Study duration The study comprises a 3-week ESM based daily diary. The assessment period is divided into a 1-week training period and 2-week follow-up period. These assessment durations have been chosen to ensure sufficient practice (training period; around 14 entries minimum per participant) and a sufficiently long time window to balance the likely low base rate of spontaneous diary use with minimising participant burden. 2. Type of sampling scheme We combine an event- with a signal-based sampling design. The ESM practice period aims to capture directed practice and spontaneous AM entries. Participants will be instructed to use the AM diary twice a day at a convenient time to make sure they accomplish the AM entries thoroughly. The rationale for this procedure is that more than two practice AM entries per day may lead to reduced compliance (e.g., skipping AM entries or “clicking through”). Participants will receive one daily, randomly timed (between 9AM – 5PM) reminder via text message. In addition to the two designated AM entries, participants will be encouraged to fill in the diary at any other time that they experience a downturn in mood. During the ESM follow-up period, the requirement for twice daily practice is removed, and instead, study participants are instructed to use the AM diary anytime they experience a downturn in mood. Throughout the whole ESM period participants will be asked to complete a retrospective end-of-day questionnaire to rate their mood throughout the day. To this extant they will receive a second reminder at 7PM. The time was chosen as this is generally after the workday has ended but will not interfere with participants’ bedtimes. 3. Total number and type of items, estimated questionnaire duration An ESM questionnaire consisting of 26 items will be used to assess momentary emotions and to facilitate AM practice, taking about 3 minutes to complete. The end-of-day questionnaire will consist of 10 items on average affect throughout the day and can be completed in about 2 minutes. 4. Time-out specifications Participants will be asked (and reminded) to fill in the end-of-day questionnaire every day at 7PM to reduce heterogeneity in the data. However, all end-of-day questionnaires completed between 6PM and 1AM will be considered during the data analysis to minimise missing data. We will exclude any ESM questionnaires that have been suspended for a longer time than 15 minutes as initial piloting indicated that this was the maximum amount of time a participant is likely to take to complete the practice task. 5. ESM instructions ESM instructions delivered during the baseline laboratory session will involve a training session, and end of day ESM instructions will not involve a training session. Participants will receive a detailed introduction for both practice and spontaneous use of the AM diary to ensure that the study procedure is well understood, the meaning of all items is clear, and they refer to the correct time period for each prompt. During the baseline laboratory session, a member of the research team will guide each participant through completing one daily questionnaire and one end-of-day questionnaire. 6. ESM questionnaires Memory questionnaire: The AM questionnaire will start with a welcome screen and participants will be asked to indicate whether they are about to make an AM entry for practice purposes or a spontaneous entry in response to low mood. Then they will be provided with 4 positively and 4 negatively valenced emotion terms and indicate on a visual analog scale from 1 to 100 the degree that they currently feel the respective emotion. Afterwards they will be provided with their 6 preselected AMs. Once they have chosen a memory to recall, participants will be presented with instructions on how to recollect their respective AM. Both the positive self-affirming and the neutral group will be instructed to recollect their AM in a vivid and imagery-rich way, but only in the positive condition will participants be asked to focus on their positive emotions. Afterwards, they will rate AM vividness, imagery, and again the 8 momentary emotions. End-of-day questionnaire: This questionnaire will comprise 10 items on positive and negative affect that will be rated for their overall experience throughout the day. 7. Hardware and software used Participants will use their personal phone to access ESM questionnaires via their internet browser. This way, participants do not have to carry along an additional device. Using the phone’s browser also avoids potential technical issues with smartphone system software. ESM data will be collected and managed using REDCap electronic data capture tools hosted at the University of Cambridge (Harris et al., 2019; Harris et al., 2009). REDCap (Research Electronic Data Capture) is a secure, web-based software platform designed to support data capture for research studies, providing 1) an intuitive interface for validated data capture; 2) audit trails for tracking data manipulation and export procedures; 3) automated export procedures for seamless data downloads to common statistical packages; and 4) procedures for data integration and interoperability with external sources. 8. Incentivisation and increasing participant engagement Participants will be reimbursed at a rate of £40 for participation. £12 of this will be received at the first laboratory session, with the balance to be paid upon attendance at the second and final laboratory session.

Sample size

We will include a total of 60 participants in our sample resulting in n = 30 for each of the two conditions.

Sample size rationale

The sample size (along with the number of administered practice AM entries) was determined on the basis of a power analysis for the primary hypothesis (hypothesis 1) and guided by considerations pertaining data quality and participant burden and compliance. The primary hypothesis focuses on the cross-level interaction between experimental condition (level 2: positive self-affirming vs. neutral) and the intraindividual change across time in happiness (level 1: pre - post AM recollection) with AM assessments being nested within study participants. Following state-of-the-art recommendations for power analysis in ESM studies (Trull, 2019) we conducted a simulation-based power analysis using the simr package (Green, &amp; MacLeod, 2016) for a two-level model. For the simulation-based power analysis using simr we used standardised estimates as provided by (Arend &amp; Schäfer, 2018). Recent laboratory research found a large interaction effect (partial eta squared = 0.30) between AM valence (positive vs. neutral) on positive mood (Grol, Vanlessen, &amp; De Raedt, 2017) and time (pre. vs. post a mood induction procedure with an AM). For the present ESM study assumed a large cross-level interaction effect for the present study (standardised estimate: .50; i.e., individuals in the positive condition experience a stronger increase in momentary happiness from pre to post). The duration of the practice period (1 week) and number of practice AM entries per day (n = 2) was determined on considerations pertaining to data quality and participant compliance, resulting in 14 AM entries per person (practice entries plus potential spontaneous diary use). Following a review conducted by Arend and Schäfer (2018) pertaining studies were repeated assessments on level 1 will be nested within individuals on level 2 we expect an ICC of 0.50. In line with the authors recommendations, we further assume a medium random slope variance (standardised random slope variance estimate = .09) as we do have not a specific hypothesis with regard to its size. For a more conservative power estimation, we assume a correlation of 0.00 between random intercept and random slope. The power analysis with 10,000 simulations yielded a sample size of 60 participants with 14 AM assessments on level 1 to be sufficient to achieve 85.51% Power (confidence interval: 84.80%; 86.90%) with an alpha of 0.05.

Indices

We will calculate sum scores for positive and negative affect throughout the day as assessed with the PANAS-SF. We will further calculate sum scores for the WEMWBS, the BDI-II, and the VVIQ. In terms of AM ratings, we will compute a mean score for vividness ratings. To assess the average emotional benefit in momentary happiness and in momentary sadness we will calculate the average difference between pre and post AM recollection ratings.

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

For primary hypothesis and the exploratory research question, the data will have a hierarchical structure with AM entries (level 1) being nested in individuals (level 2). Analysis primary hypothesis (hypothesis 1): We will predict happiness and sadness, respectively, by time (level 1: pre vs. post AM recollection), condition (level 2: positive self-affirming vs. neutral condition), and their interaction term using linear mixed models. Further, we will consider the WEMWBS and the BDI-II sum scores as control variables and model with the consecutive AM number whether potential effects increase over the course of the study. Analysis secondary hypothesis (hypothesis 2): We will predict AM valence, happiness/satisfaction upon recall, AM vividness, and self-concordance by group (positive self-affirming vs. neutral) using multivariate linear regression. We will include the number of diary AM entries across the ESM period as a covariate. Analysis feasibility (hypothesis 3): We will predict the amount of spontaneous AM entries per person by condition (positive self-affirming, neutral) using linear regression models. We will further consider the average emotional benefit in momentary happiness and momentary sadness as moderators.

Transformations

The continuous level 2 predictor variables BDI-II and WEMWBS will be grand-mean centered to facilitate model output interpretation. Experimental condition and assessment period will be included as binary variables. Reference categories will be the neutral condition and the practice period, respectively.

Inference criteria

In mixed effects models, we will examine fixed effects estimates, statistical significance, their confidence intervals, random effects, ICCs, as well as explained variance. In linear regression models, we will examine estimates, confidence intervals, their statistical significance, and explained variance. In line with current practice, standard p value below .05 criteria will be used to determine statistical significance (two-tailed tests).

Data exclusion

We will exclude participants from the analyses that did not follow the protocol correctly (e.g., where time stamps reveal “clicking through” questionnaires), do not show any variance in the main ESM outcome variables momentary happiness and sadness, respectively; or that do not provide enough data that are statistically necessary for the models. If participants drop out the study (e.g. cannot be contacted anymore), their data will be maintained for the analyses if they completed at least half of the scheduled practice diary entries.

Missing data

The key focus of the present study is on AM assessments. AM entries follow an event-based design so that no missing data procedure will be applied.

Exploratory analysis

Exploratory analysis: We will predict the WEMWBS and BDI-II scores, respectively, by time (pre, post, follow-up), group (positive self-affirming vs. neutral) and their interaction term using linear mixed effects models. We will include the average experienced emotional benefits in happiness and sadness as mediators in the models. Similar to recent experience sampling research (Kleiman et al., 2017) we will further explore dynamics and variability in ESM measures and end-of-day ratings over the course of the study both visually through individual and group time series plots and statistically using intraclass correlations (ICCs) and root mean squares of successive differences (RMSSD; von Neumann et al., 1941).
