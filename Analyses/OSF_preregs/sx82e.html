Study Information

Hypotheses

We do not have formal hypotheses, but rather will investigate the following exploratory research questions: 1. Which emotion regulation strategies do people turn to in daily life to manage their secrets? 2. How much are emotion regulation strategies to manage secrets associated with one another? 3. Do people vary more between-person or within-person in which emotion regulation strategies they select to manage their secrets? 4. Do certain features of the secret predict daily use of emotion regulation strategies to manage emotions about the secret?

Data collection procedures

11.1. Study duration (number of days): Data collection lasted for 15 consecutive days in total. On Day 0, participants completed the baseline survey. On Day 1 to 14, participants completed one daily survey per day. 11.2. Type of sampling scheme: After completing the baseline survey, eligible participants received one daily survey between 5:30 PM and 6:30 PM for the next 14 consecutive days. Each daily survey expired at midnight. 11.3. Total number and type of items (open-ended or closed) and estimated questionnaire duration: The baseline survey included a combination of validated and purpose-designed scales to assess time-invariant individual difference variables. Following a pilot, we estimated the completion time for the baseline survey to be approximately 30 minutes, although participants appeared to complete the baseline more quickly than this estimate. The daily survey consisted of 62 questions (12 of which were relevant to the current study). Following a pilot, we estimated the completion time for each daily survey to range between 5 to 6 minutes. 11.4. Time-out specifications: Each daily survey was sent out between 5:30 PM and 6:30 PM and expired at midnight. 11.5. Additional passive monitoring: Not applicable 11.6. Incentivization and increasing participant engagement: Participants were compensated monetarily for their involvement via an incentive scheme, contingent on their compliance. The incentive scheme was structured as follows: • Baseline survey completed = £3.50 • 1 daily survey completed = £0.70 (Total £9.80 if all 14 surveys are completed) • Bonus if at least 13 surveys are completed = £2.00 All participants were informed of the incentive structure prior to completing the study and could keep track of their compliance on Prolific. Participants received a reminder via Prolific on the seventh day of daily survey, to increase their engagement and compliance, if they had completed fewer than 6 out of 7 daily surveys. 11.7. Please indicate any additional details that are relevant to the daily diary data collection procedure: Screener session: A description of the project was advertised via Prolific. Participants who had chosen to take part completed a brief 4-5-minute screener survey. Participants were compensated for completing the screener (£0.50). This screener survey was used to determine eligibility for this study, which included participants who: A. Had at least one current secret, and B. Did not report having a diagnosis of a mental health disorder Baseline session: In the baseline session, participants read a plain language statement introducing the study and provided informed consent. Next, they completed time-invariant individual difference measures in the baseline survey. Daily survey period: The 14-day daily survey period began the day after the baseline session. Participants had the option to contact the researchers via Prolific with questions during this period.

Sample size

The target sample size was 250.

Sample size rationale

The target sample size was based on a simulated power curve calculation (Murayama et al., 2022) using multilevel parameters from a similar study. The calculation suggested that we would require a sample size of N = 211 to obtain 80% power with an alpha level of 0.5. Thus, we aimed to recruit 250 participants to allow for attrition and to reach the necessary sample size threshold.

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

1. Which emotion regulation strategies do people turn to in daily life to manage their secrets? We will calculate the mean, within and between standard deviations for each emotion regulation strategy item, and for each strategy index (based on the corresponding 2 items). We will also run multilevel models, constructing 3-level models (i.e., emotion regulation strategy, nested within days, nested within persons). Variables will be recoded so that emotion regulation intensity scores for each person are predicted by a factor variable comparing the different strategies (replacing each strategy as the reference category in subsequent tests). 2. How much are emotion regulation strategies to manage secrets associated with one another? We will calculate within and between person correlations between emotion regulation strategies. 3. Do people vary more between-person or within-person in which emotion regulation strategies they select to manage their secrets? We will calculate ICC for each emotion regulation strategy, differentiating between-person from within person variance. 4. Do certain features of the secret predict daily use of emotion regulation strategies to manage emotions about the secret? We will run mixed effects models. The outcome variable will be each emotion regulation strategy. We will include one person-level predictor variable in each model – each feature of the secret. These predictors will be grand-mean centered. We will include a random intercept for participant. For any model returning significant associations between secret feature and strategy, we will run models including as predictor the interaction between the feature of the secret and emotion regulation strategy, as a predictor of emotion regulation intensity.

Transformations

Level 2 predictor variables will be grand-mean centered.

Inference criteria

Data will be analysed using multilevel models, which include measurement occasions nested within day, nested within-person. We will use p-values (two-tailed) to determine statistical significance of parameter estimates.

Data exclusion

Pre-processing was performed on the data to the following specifications: Compliance and missingness: We will not exclude participants based on compliance or amount of missingness. Compliance is defined as the number of surveys completed divided by the total number of surveys received (over the 14 days). All observations that were sent as per the schedule outlined above will be counted as valid – any observations sent outside this schedule (because of technical issues) will be removed. Attention checks: We included attention checks in the baseline survey and in 5 out of 14 daily surveys. ● Baseline. We precluded participants who failed both instructional attention checks and open-ended attention checks from completing daily diary surveys. o Instructional attention checks: we embedded them in question matrices and asked participants to respond with a specific answer on a Likert scale. Responding with a scale item other than the one instructed to select was regarded as a failed attention check. o Open-ended attention checks: we inspected three questions in which participants were asked to report estimates of their mind-wandering to their secrets, concealing and revealing frequencies. Responses typed as either a number or a word (e.g., either “10” or “ten”) were regarded as an acceptable answer. A coder reviewed the responses and checked for nonsensical or irrelevant responses that did not answer the question. Missing data were not considered as a failed attention check. ● Daily diary. We will exclude participants who fail more than 80% of attention checks that they completed. For example, if participants complete all 5 surveys in which there is an attention check, and fail 4 of those 5 checks, they will be excluded from analysis. Each attention check requires participants to respond 100 on a 0-100 slider scale. We allow for a 5-point margin of error. Failing this attention check involves responding with anything less than 95. Careless responding: ● Baseline. Following a pilot, we estimated that the baseline survey could not feasibly take less than 10 minutes to complete. We revised this down to 8.5 minutes during data collection as participants completed the baseline survey faster than we expected based on our pilot. Therefore, participants who completed the baseline in less than 8.5 minutes were not invited to take part in subsequent parts of the study, and their baseline responses excluded prior to analysis. Survey completion: To be included in the dataset for analysis, participants must have completed the baseline survey and at least one daily diary. Inclusion criteria violations: ● Participants must be over 18 years old ● Participants must reside in the UK ● Participants must pass all checks for attention and careless responding in the baseline and meet the minimum threshold of passed attention checks in the daily diary surveys

Missing data

Based on previous ESM/diary research conducted within our lab, we expected approximately 10-15% attrition due to technical issues or voluntary withdrawal. We planned our study accordingly by seeking to recruit a sample size 15% larger than required (based on power analysis) to allow for this expected rate of attrition. We do not exclude participants based on missing data.
