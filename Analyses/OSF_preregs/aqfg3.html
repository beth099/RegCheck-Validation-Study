Effective Prospective Memory Reminders

OSF ID: aqfg3 | Registered: 2021-01-21T00:13:08.642624

Study Information

Hypotheses

1. We predict that cue-target pair reminders will yield the best PM performance. 2. Because noticing the cue is a prerequisite for the target action, the cue reminders will be more beneficial than the target reminders. 3. Target reminders still be more beneficial than no reminder.

Study design

Four-level (control, cue reminder, target reminder, and cue-target reminder) between subjects design.

Randomization

We will use block randomization, where each participant will be randomly assigned to one of four equally sized groups. QuestionPro survey software will be used to assign participants to each group.

Explanation of existing data

Data collection procedures

Participants will be recruited through SONA systems and compensated with course credit. Participants must be at least 18 years old and fluent in English. Data will be collected online via PsychoPy3.0 experimental software hosted on Pavlovia.org. Exclusion criteria are the same as Peper et al. (in preparation) and include: 1) Failing attention checks 2) Both failing to detect any PM targets and remember the prospective memory task 3) Reporting cheating (writing down any of the learned cue-target pairs 4) Performing below 50% on a math distractor task 5) Getting below 75% accuracy on the PM block ongoing task 6) Participants with PM block ongoing task response times greater than 3 SDs or less than -3 SDs from the group mean

Sample size

The a priori power analysis recommended 168 participants or 42 participants per group. We opted for 200 total or 50 participants per group to anticipate those participants meeting exclusionary criteria after halting data collection.

Sample size rationale

We used G*Power to conduct a power analysis. Our goal was to obtain .90 power to detect a effect of partial eta = .08 observed in Peper et al. (in preparation) at .05 alpha error probability.

Stopping rule

Data collection will cease once there are 50 participants in each group.

Variables

Manipulated variables

We manipulated the PM task information provided in the reminders. The four levels of this categorical variable are: no reminder control, cue reminder, target reminder, and cue-target reminder.

Measured variables

The primary outcome variable measured will be PM performance. PM performance reflects the number of cues participants respond to by typing in the target word. Of secondary interest is cue detection. Cue detection refers to the number of cues participants respond to by typing anything meaningful (something other than F or J - ongoing task responses), and this includes PM performance. Also of interest will be ongoing task performance, both accuracy and response times. Lastly, recognition memory for the cue-target pairs will be assessed.

Indices

PM performance and cue detection will be the proportion of correct responses out of total cue appearances (8). Ongoing task performance will be the accuracy and response time means. Recognition memory will be the proportion of correct responses out of total questions (4).

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

A four level (control, cue reminder, target reminder, and cue-target reminder) between subjects ANOVA will be used to assess the effect of different reminder types on the various outcome variables. Independent samples t-tests will be used to follow up ANOVA. Family-wise Bonferonni correction will be applied to p-values.

Transformations

For ongoing task performance, the first 3 trials and the 3 trials following a PM cue will be excluded from analyses. Further, ongoing task response times outside of 3.0 SDs (+/-) of each individual's mean will be trimmed.

Inference criteria

Data exclusion

Participants will be excluded for meeting the following criteria (Peper et al., in preparation): 1) Failing attention checks 2) Both failing to detect any PM targets and remember the prospective memory task 3) Reporting cheating (writing down any of the learned cue-target pairs 4) Performing below 50% on a math distractor task 5) Getting below 75% accuracy on the PM block ongoing task 6) Participants with PM block ongoing task response times greater than 3 SDs or less than -3 SDs from the group mean
