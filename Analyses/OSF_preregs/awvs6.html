Sample size

200 participants.

Sample size rationale

Our target sample size (N=200) was determined based on a power analysis conducted using the method described in Murayama et al. (2022) and implemented using their shiny app (https://koumurayama.shinyapps.io/tmethod_mlm/). We ran this power analysis using estimates from an existing daily diary dataset (N=171), which included similar measures for interpersonal emotion regulation as the current study. To ensure our planned study had sufficient power to detect small within-person (Level-1) effects, we chose the minimum effect of a Level-1 predictor where t = 2.90 (d = 0.22). These parameters indicated that we would need to reach a sample size of N=162 to achieve 80% power with an alpha level of .05. We therefore aimed to recruit 200 participants to account for attrition.

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

1. INTRINSIC INTERPERSONAL EMOTION REGULATION Analyses will be performed on a subset of participants who indicated an intention to engage in intrinsic interpersonal emotion regulation. a. What motives do people have when they engage in intrinsic interpersonal emotion regulation? First, we will independently recode open-ended responses from observations where participants indicated that they had another motive not listed to determine whether they fit in with any of the existing motives. One coder will review open-ended responses, and check the allocation of a sub-set of responses with a second coder. Any disagreement between coders will be resolved through discussion. Second, after recoding, we will calculate the frequency distribution of all the motive items, and how many participants report these motives. b. What motives frequently co-occur when people engage in intrinsic interpersonal emotion regulation? Occasion-level co-occurrence: we will calculate the phi coefficient for each motive pair. Person-level co-occurrence: For each participant, we will calculate the mean selection of each motive across 14 days to create a person-level variable. We will then calculate nonparametric correlations between all the motive items using Spearman’s Rho. 2. Transformations

Interpersonal emotion regulation intention o For each type of emotion regulation, we will create a binary intention variable based on participants’ response to the corresponding emotion regulation goal question. If participants indicated that they did not try to regulate (i.e., the first category), their intention will be coded as 0. If they indicated that they had one or more emotion regulation goal (i.e., any of the subsequent categories), their intention will be coded as 1. Interpersonal emotion regulation motives o For each motive item, we will create person-averaged variable that reflects the proportion of time a person reports that motive throughout the study.

Data exclusion

COMPLIANCE AND MISSINGNESS We did not exclude participants based on compliance or amount of missingness. Compliance was defined as the number of surveys completed by a participant, divided by the number of surveys they received. All observations that were sent as per the schedule outlined above were counted as valid. ATTENTION CHECKS We included attention checks in the baseline and 5 out of 14 daily diaries. • Baseline. Participants who failed both instructional attention checks or an open-ended attention check would be excluded. The instructional attention checks were embedded in question matrices and required participants to respond with a specific answer on a Likert scale. Failing those attention checks involved responding with another scale item. The open-ended attention check asked participants to describe in one or two sentences the last social interaction they had. One coder would go over the responses and check for nonsense or irrelevant responses that do not answer the question. Any responses identified as nonsense or irrelevant would be checked and confirmed by a second coder. • Daily diary. Participants who failed more than 80% of attention checks that they had completed would be excluded. For example, if participants completed all 5 surveys in which there was an attention check, and failed 4 of those 5 checks, they would be excluded from analysis. Each attention check required participants to respond 100 on a 0-100 slider scale. We allowed for a 5-point margin of error. Failing this attention check involved responding with anything less than 95. CARELESS RESPONDING • Baseline. Following a pilot, we estimated that the baseline survey could not feasibly take less than 10 minutes to complete. Therefore, participants who completed the baseline in less than 10 minutes would not be invited to take part in subsequent parts of the study, and their responses would be excluded from the dataset for analysis. • Daily diary. Following a pilot, we estimated that each diary could not feasibly take less than 2 minutes to complete. Therefore, we excluded surveys that were completed in under 2 minutes from the dataset for analysis. SURVEY COMPLETION To be included in the dataset for analysis, participants must have completed the baseline survey and at least one daily diary. INCLUSION CRITERIA VIOLATION To be included in this study, participants must: • Be over 18 years old • Reside in the UK • Pass all checks for attention and careless responding in the baseline

Missing data

Based on previous experience sampling/daily diary research conducted within our lab, we expected approximately 10-15% of participants to drop out of the study either due to technical issues or voluntary withdrawal. In addition, we expected approximately 15-25% of daily diaries to be missing among participants who complete the study.
