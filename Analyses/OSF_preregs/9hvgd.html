Transfer of Attentional Control Settings - Intermixed

OSF ID: 9hvgd | Registered: 2022-11-07T19:29:57.239698

Study Information

Hypotheses

1. We hypothesize that participants will learn the PC signal carried by the target and demonstrate a significant ISPC effect with the training items. 2. We hypothesize that the ISPC effect will mainly be driven by the incongruent trials compared to the congruent trials for the training items. 3. If the retrieval and execution of item-specific control settings are automatic, for the participants showing an ISPC effect with the training items, they will demonstrate a significant ISPC effect with the transfer items because the same relevant dimension (color) is presented. If the retrieval and execution of item-specific control settings are not automatic (i.e., there is either context- or task-dependency), even for the participants showing an ISPC effect with the training items, they will not demonstrate a significant ISPC effect with the transfer items. 4. In addition to testing the third hypothesis by examining the ISPC effect, we will also test this hypothesis by examining the difference between incongruent items presented with target colors that were MC and MI previously. If the retrieval and execution of item-specific control settings are automatic, for the participants showing an ISPC effect with the training items, RT for incongruent items presented with target colors that were MI previously should be faster than incongruent items presented with target colors that were MC previously. If the retrieval and execution of item-specific control settings are not automatic, even for the participants showing an ISPC effect with the training items, RT for incongruent items presented with target colors that were MI previously should not differ from the incongruent items presented with target colors that were MC previously. Often times the hypothesized differences only show up in RT, but we will also examine accuracy for the same effects to confirm the patterns do not contradict the RT data.

Study design

For the training items: 2 (Trial type: Congruent vs. Incongruent) x 2 (Proportion Congruence: Mostly congruent vs. Mostly incongruent) within-subject design with the Flanker task For the transfer items: 2 (Trial type: Congruent vs. Incongruent) x 2 (Proportion Congruence of the Predictive Cue: Mostly congruent vs. Mostly incongruent) within-subject design with the Stroop task The training and transfer items are randomly intermixed. Before the actual experimental block, we present a learning block that has the exact same design as the actual experimental block. The learning block is 1/4 of the actual experimental block (144 trials) and from the participants' perspective, these trials appear to be part of the actual experimental block. The aim of the learning block is to allow participants to learn about the PCs of predictive cues which is necessary for the transfer. Even though our critical analyses focus on the actual experimental block, we will analyze the learning block to understand when the learning occurs and transfer to a novel task (potentially) starts.

Randomization

We do not have a between-subjects variable, but we have two different counterbalance conditions for the stimulus-PC pairing (i.e., in one condition blue and red are MC items and green and white are MI items; in the other condition blue and red are MI items and green and white are MC items). Participants are assigned to subject numbers and corresponding conditions which were defined randomly according to the order of their participation.

Explanation of existing data

Data collection procedures

Participants: Participants will be university students who are 18-25 years old. Native English speakers who have normal or corrected to normal vision, and who do not have color blindness will be invited as participants. Participants will get course credit for their participation. Recruitment: Participants will be invited to study via SONA system. Data Sampling: Participants will attend the experiment individually. They will be instructed to respond to the color of the word while ignoring the word itself for the Stroop task, and the color of the circle in the center while ignoring the color of the outer circles for the Flanker task as quickly and as accurately as possible. Stimulus presentation and data collection are controlled automatically by a standard PC, E-Prime 2 software, and a standard microphone. They give their responses by speaking into the microphone.

Sample size

Our target sample is 102 participants. We will attempt to recruit up to 120 participants, assuming not all participants will comply with the requirements of the experiment.

Sample size rationale

For the smallest effect that we are interested in (the ISPC effect with diagnostic items), power analysis with the G*Power software showed that a sample size of 102 would provide .95 power to detect an effect size of partial eta-squared .115, with alpha set at .05. As in our prior pre-registered studies, we used the effect size from a previous study finding a significant ISPC effect in the transfer phase in a similar design (Colvett, Wetz, &amp; Bugg, in preparation).

Stopping rule

We will stop at 120 participants. If the participants that fulfill the inclusion criteria fall under 102 (our target N for the experiment), we will recruit new participants to reach 102.

Variables

Manipulated variables

Independent variables (IVs) for training and transfer items: 1. Trial type with 2 levels: a. Congruent b. Incongruent 2. Proportion congruency or proportion congruency of the predictive cue with 2 levels: a. Mostly congruent b. Mostly incongruent The organization of the stimuli is attached (Stimulus Organization.pdf).

Measured variables

Reaction time and accuracy will be measured for each trial and the data will be collected by the E-Prime 2 software.

Indices

We will calculate the average reaction time and accuracy for each within-subject condition. Prior to the calculation for reaction time, incorrect trials, trials without a response, scratch trials, and trials faster than 200 or slower than 1500 ms will be removed. Prior to the calculation for accuracy, trials without a response, scratch trials, and trials faster than 200 or slower than 1500 ms will be removed.

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

There will be two sets of analyses, one for the training items and the other for the transfer items. First, we will test the existence of the ISPC effect for the training items (i.e., the Flanker task). A 2 (Trial type: Congruent vs. Incongruent) x 2 (Proportion Congruence: Mostly congruent vs. Mostly incongruent) within-subject ANOVA will be conducted for RT and accuracy. A significant 2-way interaction would indicate the ISPC effect. We will also check the asymmetrical influence of PC manipulation with separate dependent samples t-tests for congruent and incongruent trials. For the transfer items (i.e., the Stroop task), the same analysis will be conducted (with proportion congruence of the predictive cue as a factor) with the participants having a positive ISPC effect with the training items. Additionally, with the incongruent trials of the transfer items in the Stroop task, we will conduct a one-tailed dependent samples t-test comparing incongruent trials presented with target colors that had an MC predictive cue and incongruent trials presented with target colors that had an MI predictive cue. This analysis will be performed with the participants having a positive ISPC effect with the training items. We will repeat the same analyses for the learning phase (the block before the actual experiment) to observe if and when the transfer occurs in the learning phase. However, our hypotheses focus on the actual experimental block after the learning phase.

Transformations

No transformations will be conducted.

Inference criteria

We will use the p &lt; .05 (p smaller than .05) criteria for determining if the ANOVA results are significantly different from those expected if the null hypothesis were correct.

Data exclusion

Participants whose average percentage of errors is higher than three standard-deviation of the average percentage of error for all participants, and participants whose average scratch trials are higher than three standard-deviation of the average scratch trials for all participants will be excluded from all analyses. For each participant, trials faster than 200 or slower than 1500 ms, scratch trials, and trials without a response will be excluded.

Missing data

Missing data points will not be replaced, the data will stay as missing.

Exploratory analysis

For the transfer set analyses, we will also compare sub-blocks of the experiment, considering the ISPC effect or incongruent trial difference might be observed with the first sub-blocks, and then disappear for the later sub-blocks as participants learn the PC of the transfer items. Because participants switch between two tasks for the training and transfer items, we will also explore the difference between switch and repeat trials. In addition, we will explore the influence of predictive cue repetition across consecutive training and transfer trials.
