Study Information

Hypotheses

H1: We predict that we will replicate our main effect in the first stage of the study. We predict a negative two-way interaction conscientiousness X conservatism in the prediction of sharing a story, such that conservatism has less of a positive impact on likelihood of sharing when conscientiousness is higher. H2: We predict that conscientiousness will have a positive main effect on the extent to which participants decrease their likelihood of sharing news stories in responses to receiving a warning. H3: We predict that the interaction conscientiousness X conservatism will have a positive effect on the extent to which participants decrease their likelihood of sharing news stories in responses to receiving a warning.

Study design

We will ask participants to rate the accuracy of 12 real and 12 fake Coronavirus stories, and how likely they are to share them on social media. Within each group of 12 stories, 4 will be conservative-leaning, 4 democrat-leaning and 4 neutral. For fake news stories that participants indicated they may share (by responding with 'Yes' or 'Maybe' to our sharing measure - see later), participants saw these stimuli again, with fact checker warning messages attached, after completing the CRT, CRT-2 and BFI. We will measure participants’ personality using the BFI personality inventory, as well as their CRT score, CRT-2 score and demographic information. We will collect information regarding individuals' political orientation, both in a 7-point scale of liberal-conservatism, and a binary choice between the democrats and republicans. Additionally, we will collect measures of participants' beliefs regarding the virus. These fall into two categories: how personally worried they are, and their beliefs about the extent to which the virus is dangerous. We will collapse across our six measures to create a single measure ‘ideology’, where a higher score indicates a greater degree of worry regarding / perception of severity of the Coronavirus. We added two additional questions to study partisan differences in the perceived threat of the virus, and support for federally mandated quarantining.

Explanation of existing data

Data collection procedures

We will post this study to Amazon's Mechanical Turk platform. We will exclude participants who answer our comprehension check incorrectly. These participants will be immediately kicked out. We will limit to US only IP addresses, using TurkPrime, Qualtrics and manual verification of IP Addresses. Participants will be paid $1.51 for their time. See Analysis Plan for further details of our exclusion plan.

Sample size

We will target a sample size of N = 1000. Each participant will provide 24 measures (12 real and 12 fake stories). This will be reduced by focusing on only individuals who indicated they would share fake news (see analysis section).

Sample size rationale

If 50% of participants indicate they will share 1 or more fake news story, our population of interest will be N = 500, consistent with our other studies.

Variables

Manipulated variables

Fake: 1 if a story is fake (i.e. 12/24 of measures), 0 if a story is real (the other 12/24 measures). News: 1 if a story is conservative, 2 if a story is liberal, 3 if a story is neutral.

Measured variables

Dependent variable 1: To the best of your knowledge, how accurate is the claim in the above headline?” (response options: not at all accurate / not very accurate / somewhat accurate / very accurate) Dependent variable 2: Would you consider sharing this story online (for example, through Facebook or Twitter)? (response options: No / Maybe / Yes) Participants will see all 24 stories (12 real, 12 fake) once. For some number of stories X, they will indicate whether they might share these stories or not. This is measured by whether they indicated 'maybe' or 'yes' to the DV:2 measure listed above. For all X stories that they indicate they may share, they will have a chance to see these stories again, this time with a facts disputed warning attached to them. When they see these X stories again, they will again indicate whether they are likely to share them. The number of these X stories that they indicate they may / will share is Y. Our measure of the change in their behaviour is (X - Y)/X. Scales: BFI personality inventory (Soto, C. J., &amp; John, O. P., 2017), CRT (Frederick, 2005), CRT-2 (Thomson and Oppenheimer, 2016). Please select the option that best describes your political orientation. (1 to 7; Very liberal to Very conservative.) If you had to choose between Democrats and Republicans, who would you prefer? (choose Dem / Rep) Ideology measure (average of six items, scored 1-5): Facet 1: How concerned are you with the Corona virus? How worried are you about the Corona virus? How frightened are you by the Corona virus? Facet 2: How much do you agree with the statement 'The Corona virus is a global pandemic'? How dangerous do you think the Corona virus is to the world? How big of a problem do you think the Corona virus is to the world? Exaggerated How likely do you think that the Coronavirus (COVID-19) threat is exaggerated? Quarantine support To what extent would you support a federally mandated two-week self-quarantine for people exposed to COVID-19?

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

We will replicate our previous analyses using logistic regression to predict the likelihood of participants sharing a story in their initial 24 responses. The variable 'share' is 0 if respondents indicate 'no' to the sharing question, 1 if they indicate 'maybe' or 'yes'. This is in line with the procedure used by Pennycook and colleagues. We will cluster observations by participant for these analyses. For the main analyses of this study, we will use linear regression to predict the extent to which participants reduced the number of stories they indicated they might share in response to seeing warnings from third party fact checkers. How we will construct this variable is delineated in the 'Variables' section. For this analysis, we will exclude any participants for which X = 0 (i.e. anyone who did not indicate they may share any fake news stories). We treat this variable measuring this change as continuous.
