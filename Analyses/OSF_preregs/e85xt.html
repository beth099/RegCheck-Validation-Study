Grammatical Gender and Anthropomorphism

OSF ID: e85xt | Registered: 2021-09-17T12:14:31.156699

Study Information

Hypotheses

We will test the proposition that grammatical gender congruency facilitates assigning human characteristics to non-humans such that when asked to categorize a non-human entity and provided with two categories of nouns, each set consisting of nouns that refer to either inanimate entitles or humans, and take the opposite grammatical gender, participants will be more likely to assign the non-human noun in question to the human category when the grammatical gender of the entity and those of the nouns in the human category is congruent (vs. incongruent). The study will be conducted in French.

Stopping rule

Variables

Manipulated variables

There are two conditions: congruent and incongruent. In a study ostensibly about predictions and categorizations concerning different animals, participants will be shown 10 different animals (one animal per page, in a random order), and asked to assign the animal in question to one of the two categories. The two categories will include different nouns and in one option they will consist of inanimate nouns (e.g., home, rock, etc.), and the other option will include human nouns (e.g., mother, father, etc.). In the congruent condition, the option featuring human nouns will take on the same grammatical gender of the animal in question, whereas the inanimate nouns option will consist of nouns with the opposite grammatical gender. The incongruent set will consist of entities that take the opposite grammatical gender (both for the human and the inanimate category).

Measured variables

Participants will choose between two options for 10 animals. We will create a composite anthropomorphism score by averaging the way participants assign the animals to one of the two categories, (non-humans vs. human category; coded as 0 and 1), with the higher composite scores indicating greater propensity of associating the animal with human characteristics (i.e., anthropomorphism).

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

To test our hypothesis, we will conduct an independent samples t-test with the grammatical gender congruency condition as the between subject factor, and the composite anthropomorphism as the dependent variable. We predict a significant difference between the conditions such that participants in the congruent condition will be more likely to assign animals to the human category than participants in the incongruent condition.
