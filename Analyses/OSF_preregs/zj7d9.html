Study Information

Title

Reciprocity Creates Fitness Interdependence

Authors

Aleta Pleasant, Pat Barclay

Description

Does reciprocity create fitness interdependence? Many close relationships are grounded in interdependence (i.e. mating relationships, friendships, coalitions etc.). This experiment will test whether superficial reciprocal relationships are enough to produce interdependence. If true, this will provide evidence that reciprocity creates a vested stake in your partner, and thus can help to promote cooperation. A key distinguishing factor between a relationship only concerned with direct reciprocity as compared to one grounded in interdependence is the willingness to help when it cannot be repaid. We hypothesize that a reciprocal relationship will foster interdependence and thus a willingness to help a partner even when that help is given anonymously. Specifically, people will be more willing to pay to help a partner if that means increasing the probability of keeping them around.

Hypotheses

Main Predictions 1. We predict the greatest amount of help to be given in cooperative dyads (groups that have cooperated in 3 or more of the proceeding 5 rounds) when help directly impacts the probability of the partners survival. This is relative to help given in both cooperative and uncooperative dyads (groups that have defected in 3 or more of the proceeding 5 rounds) when help increases the monetary payoff for the partner, as well as uncooperative dyads when help directly impacts the probability of the partners survival. 2. We predict that individuals that report more perceived interdependence with their partner (example: “What was good for my partner was good for me”) will be more likely to pay to help their partner. This will be true when help directly impacts the probability of the partners survival and when help increases the monetary payoff for their partner. Secondary Predictions 1. We predict that individuals that report more perceived interdependence with their partner will behave more cooperatively in the prisoner’s dilemma game. Exploratory Predictions 1. We predict that in uncooperative dyads more help will be given if it directly impacts the probability of the partners survival compared to when help increases the monetary payoff for the partner. 2. We predict that if the partner cooperates in the round proceeding the opportunity to help this will increase the likelihood of help being given.

Study design

Procedure This study will be conducted online on Amazon Mechanical Turk. Participants will play a 7 round prisoners dilemma game in partners. Following the 5th round of the game one of the participants in the dyad will be given an anonymous opportunity to help. The specifics of this opportunity vary by condition: Control Condition: Would you like to pay 3¢ to increase your partners final earnings by 7¢? Interdependence Condition: Your partner has a 10% chance of surviving into the next round. If your partner survives, you will both continue to the next round. If your partner does not survive, then there will be no more rounds because you will have no one to interact with. Would you like to pay 3¢ to increase your partners chance of survival from 10% to 90%? We chose these ratios (3¢ to 7¢ and 3¢ to an 80% increase in survival) to be roughly equivalent in terms of their effects on one’s partner. Because cooperative partners earn 4¢/round, an 80% increased chance of survival results is worth an expected 3.2¢/round, which across two rounds equals 6.4¢ to one’s partner. This is slightly less than the 7¢ to one’s partner in the Control Condition, but that works against our hypothesis because it makes people less likely to give in the Interdependence Condition. Exit Survey: At the conclusion of the study all participants will respond to demographic questions and a comprehension check. They will also respond to four Likert scale questions (-3 strongly disagree to +3 strongly agree) to measure their perceived level of interdependence with their partner. Comprehension check: if you were playing one (and only one) round of this game, what option would give you the highest expected payoff? Only the partners who did not get an opportunity to help during the game will respond to a hypothetical opportunity to help: - Control Condition: Imagine you were still playing the game and you had the opportunity to pay 3¢ to increase your partners final earnings by 7¢, would you? - Interdependence Condition: Imagine you were still playing the game and your partner only had a 10% chance of surviving into the next round, but you could pay 3¢ to give them a 90% chance of surviving. If they survived, you would both continue to the next round. If they did not survive, then there would be no more rounds because you'd have no one to interact with. Would you have been willing to pay 3¢ to increase their probability of survival from 10% to 90%?

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

Analyses We will use log linear analyses and logistic regressions (see analysis for each question). Notes: - We will only analyze real helping decisions with consequences (i.e., that actually confer money or probability of survival); we will exclude hypothetical responses unless otherwise specified - Cooperative dyads will be categorized as those that have cooperated in more than half of the first 5 rounds. Uncooperative dyads will be categorized as those that have defected in more than half of the first 5 rounds. - For cooperation at the individual level, cooperation will be any instance in which they have cooperated irrespective of their partners choice - For cooperation at the level of the dyad a cooperative round requires both participants to cooperate. If one or both partners defect that round is categorized as uncooperative Log linear analysis: - It will have a minimum of 40 observations to ensure at least 5 times the number of cases as there are cells in the data - If the expected frequency of any individual cell is less than one or if the combination of any two cells is less than 5, we will collapse the cooperative/uncooperative dyad grouping into the condition and run a chi-squared analysis to preserve power Logistic regression: - We will test for multicollinearity by calculating a variance inflation factor. If this factor is greater than 5, we will combine the highly related variables for the analysis Main Analyses 1. Are superficial reciprocal relationships enough to establish interdependence? Prediction: If reciprocity creates interdependence, cooperative partners will be more willing to pay a personal cost to anonymously increase their partners chance of survival. This will be greater relative to cooperative dyads whose help only increases their partner’s final payout (i.e., but not the partner’s survival), and all uncooperative dyads. Variables: - Condition (Control, Interdependence) (Categorical) - Dyad Classification (Cooperative, Uncooperative) (Categorical) Outcome Variable: - Help provided (Yes, No) (Categorical) Analyses: A log linear analysis will be conducted to determine the distribution of help provided in the control and interdependence conditions for cooperative and uncooperative dyads. We will test for main effects of condition and dyad classification, as well as whether there is an interaction. Confirmatory Analyses To test the robustness of this effect we will run the same analysis with the inclusion of the hypothetical data from the other participant. 2. Does the perception of interdependence and reciprocity predict a willingness to help a partner? Prediction: If the perception of interdependence and reciprocity is driving the willingness to help, participants who choose to help will report greater perceptions of interdependence and cooperate more frequently. Variables: Predictor Variables: - Total score on the four measures of interdependence (-3 strongly disagree to +3 strongly agree) (Continuous) - Proportion of the total rounds of the prisoner’s dilemma game that cooperation is selected (Continuous) - Condition (Control, Interdependence) (Categorical) Outcome Variable: - Help provided (Yes, No) (Categorical) Analyses A logistic regression will be run to test whether the effect of condition, perception of interdependence, and cooperation significantly predict whether the individual chooses to help. Confirmatory Analyses To test the robustness of this effect we will run the same analysis with the inclusion of the hypothetical data from the other participant. Secondary Analyses 1. Does the perception of interdependence increase cooperation? Prediction: If the perception of interdependence increases cooperation, we predict a positive correlation between reported interdependence and proportion of total rounds of the game that are cooperative. Variables: - Total score on the four measures of interdependence (-3 strongly disagree to +3 strongly agree) (Continuous) - Proportion of the total rounds of the prisoner’s dilemma game that cooperation is selected (Continuous) Analyses The above variables will be used to compute a Pearson’s correlation to determine the strength of the correlation between perceptions of interdependence and cooperation. Confirmatory Analyses To further test the robustness of this effect we will run the same analysis at the level of the dyad. This will be done by pooling the overall interdependence score and proportion of total rounds that are cooperative. We will then compute a Pearson’s correlation to determine the strength of the relationship between interdependence and cooperation.

Exploratory analysis

Exploratory Analyses: 1. Is there value in helping an uncooperative partner? Prediction: If there is no opportunity to interact with new partners, keeping an uncooperative partner can be better than nothing at all. Therefore, we predict that within uncooperative dyads they will be more likely to help when it directly impacts the probability of the partners survival than if it increases the partners final earnings. Variables: - Condition (Control, Interdependence) (Categorical) - Help provided (Yes, No) (Categorical) Analyses A chi-square analysis will be run to determine whether uncooperative partners will be more willing to help a partner in the interdependence condition relative to the control condition. Confirmatory Analyses To test the robustness of this effect we will run the same analysis with the inclusion of the hypothetical data from the other participant. 2. Does a partner’s most recent decision to cooperate or defect have an impact on the decision to help? Prediction: The choice of a partner to cooperate or defect immediately preceding the opportunity to help will impact the decision. We predict that cooperation in the round immediately prior will increase the likelihood of help being anonymously provided. Predictor Variables: - Condition (Control, Interdependence) (Categorical) - Proportion of non-immediately-preceding rounds that are cooperative (Continuous) - Cooperate or Defect in Preceding Round (C, D) (Categorical) Outcome Variable: - Help provided (Yes, No) (Categorical) Analyses A logistic regression will be run to test whether the effect of condition, dyads level of cooperation, and partners decision in the preceding round significantly predicts whether the participant chooses to help independently of each other. Confirmatory Analyses To test the robustness of this effect we will run the same analysis with the inclusion of the hypothetical data from the other participant.
