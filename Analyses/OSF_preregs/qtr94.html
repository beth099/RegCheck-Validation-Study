Study Information

Title

Cultural Appropriation Menu Study

Authors

Ariel J. Mosley, Monica Biernat

Description

The purpose of this study is to examine how White and Black participants perceive an act of appropriation in the context of a restaurant menu. This study will be a 2 (Target Food : White vs Black – between) X 2 (Target Race: White vs Black between) X Participant Race: White vs Black between) between subjects design. In this study, participants will be randomly assigned to one of four conditions (Black target + White-American cuisine, White target + White-American cuisine , Black target + soul food, White target + soul food). Participants will be asked to read through the informed Statement and click if they agree to participate. Participants will read that the purpose of this study is to examine what people think about different social and cultural situations. They will read, “Recent bloggers in the media have been interested in the idea of cultural appropriation. Here is one author’s definition of cultural definition: ‘Cultural appropriation refers to taking on elements of a culture other than one’s own. This can involve taking or using intellectual property, traditional knowledge, cultural expressions, or artifacts from someone else's culture without permission. Often cultural appropriation is demonstrated through music, dress, art, language, behavior, holidays and celebrations, and even social behaviors. After reading this definition, participants will read, “The concept of cultural appropriation is controversial in society. We’re interested in what YOU think. In this study, you will be asked to give your personal opinion about what you think about cultural appropriation.” Participants will then be asked to read a brief restaurant review and evaluate the quality of a menu. The menu will feature pre-tested photos from the pilot study. Participants will then be asked to complete dependent measures and demographics, and receive full debriefing. Scores on the dependent measures will be submitted to a 2 (Target Food : White vs Black – between) X 2 (Target Race: White vs Black between) X Participant Race: White vs Black between) between subjects Analysis of Variance to examine group differences.

Hypotheses

1) Main Effect of Participant Race: Black participants will report greater cultural appropriation, harm, intent, motives to confront than White participants. 2) Main Effect of Target Race: Participants will report greater cultural appropriation, harm, intent, motives to confront when reading about a White perpetrator taking from Black culture (vs. a Black perpetrator taking from White culture). 3) Interaction Effect of Participant Race X Perpetrator Race: Black participants will report greater cultural appropriation, harm, intent, motives to confront than White participants when reading about a White perpetrator taking from Black culture (vs. a Black perpetrator taking from White culture). 4) There will be no interaction effects on perceptions of prejudice.

Data collection procedures

Participants will be able to sign up for the study via Amazon's Mechanical Turk Workforce. Participants from the general population will be given equal opportunity to sign up for participation in this study. We are only interested in recruiting adult subjects (i.e., 18 years or older). Given the nature of this project, only Black and White participants will be included in this study. Participants who fail attention checks will be removed.

Indices

Evaluation (1= Strongly Disagree, 7 = Strongly Agree) 1. To what extent do you like this menu/business? 2. To what extent do you think this menu/business is reasonably priced? 3. To what extent would you visit this restaurant? 4. To what extent would you recommend this restaurant /business to others? Perceptions of Appropriation (1 = Strongly Disagree to 7 =Strongly Agree; Reysen, Landau, and Branscombe, 2012): 1. This chef/person is engaging in cultural appropriation of Black/White-American culture. 2. This chef/person is copying Black/White -American culture. 3. This chef/person is taking from Black/White-American culture. 4. This chef/person is displaying an element of culture that is not their own. Distinctiveness Threat (1 = Strongly Disagree to 7 =Strongly Agree; Schmid et al., 2009) 1. It is not right that cultures are treated as if they are the same. 2. It annoys me when others don’t see important differences between cultures. 3. It annoys me when others see cultural elements as interchangeable. Perceived intentionality of the copier (1 = Strongly Disagree to 7 =Strongly Agree; Reysen, Landau, and Branscombe, 2012): 1. This person is intending to copy Black/White culture. 2. This person is purposefully trying to copy Black/White culture. Perceived extent of harm the actor caused to group identity (1 = Strongly Disagree to 7 = Strongly Agree; Reysen, Landau, and Branscombe, 2012): 1. This person is exploiting Black/White culture. 2. This person is being offensive to Black/White culture 3. This person is being disrespectful to Black/White culture 4. This person is hurting Black/White culture 5. This person is being ignorant to Black/White culture Endorsement of confrontation (1 = Strongly Disagree to 7 =Strongly Agree; Reysen, Landau, and Branscombe, 2012): 1. This person should be confronted about copying Black/White culture. 2. This person should be told to stop copying Black/White culture. Perceptions of Racism (1 = Strongly Disagree to 7 =Strongly Agree) 1. This chef is being racist. 2. This chef is being discriminatory. Perceptions of Celebration (1 = Strongly Disagree to 7 =Strongly Agree) 1. This chef is being celebrating Black/White culture. 2. This chef is being appreciating Black/White culture. 3. This chef is being honoring Black/White culture. 4. This chef is being commemorating Black/White culture.
