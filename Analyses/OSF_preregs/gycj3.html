Thinking about God Discourages Dehumanization of Religious Outgroups, September 3 2021

Study Information

Hypotheses

We predict that participants will rate groups (including their ingroup and both outgroups) as more human the second time they complete the scale (Time 2) compared to the first time they complete the scale (Time 1). Although we expect to find this effect for all groups (Christians, Muslims, and atheists), we will also examine the effect for each group individually. Based on our results from previous, related studies, we expect the effect to be strongest for Muslims.

Study design

This is a repeated-measures design. Participants will complete our modified version of Kteily, Bruneau, Waytz, &amp; Cotterill's (2015) "Ascent of Man" scale of blatant dehumanization, rating the humanity of Christians, Muslims, and atheists. Participants will then be asked to think about God. Specifically, they will view the following prompt: “Please take a moment to think about God. How would God want you to view members of different religious groups? Please take a moment to write your thoughts in the box below.” Finally, participants will complete the modified Ascent of Man scale again. They will view the following prompt before the scale: "In the last part of the survey, we are going to ask you to rate the humanity of groups one more time. Please answer as you see fit."

Explanation of existing data

Data collection procedures

We will collect a sample of 1000 Christians using Cloud Research's Prime Panels. In previous studies, we have sampled only participants who identify as either moderately religious, very religious, or for whom religion is the center of their lives, but that option is no longer available as a prescreening requirement for Prime Panels. To remedy this, we will include our own screener and our primary analyses will involve only moderately to highly religious participants. We will exclude participants who did not self-identify as Christian. The text box in our manipulation will serve as a manipulation check. We will exclude participants who do not write anything, whose responses are clearly copied/pasted, who write gibberish, or whose responses are entirely irrelevant to the prompt.

Sample size

We will collect a sample of 1000 participants.

Sample size rationale

A power analysis based on data from a previous study suggested that a sample of 143 participants would allow us to detect a change in dehumanization ratings corresponding to 4.6 points out of 100 with 90.8% power. We plan to over-sample to account for the possibility that our effect size may be smaller than in previous studies, as well as the lack of the religiosity prescreen.

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

Because participants rate all three groups at both Time 1 and Time 2, we will conduct a multi-level model regressing dehumanization ratings on the target group, time of measurement (before/after the intervention, Time 1 = 0, Time 2 = 1), and their interaction. Target groups will be entered as two orthogonal Helmert contrasts (Contrast 1: Christians = 2, all other target groups = -1; Contrast 2: Christians = 0, Muslims = 1, atheists = -1). This model allows us to test for dehumanization of outgroups relative to the ingroup at Time 1 (using contrast 1), a main effect of time of measurement, collapsed across groups, and an interaction between time of measurement and relative dehumanization (Contrast 1 x Time). Contrast 2 also allows us to determine whether the effect of thinking about God on dehumanization is larger for Muslims than atheists. Participants' intercepts will be included as random factors. As a follow-up, we will also use simple-slopes models with group dummy coded in order to examine simple effects of our manipulation on perceptions of each group.
