The Effects of Linguistic Priming on Children’s Exploration in an Online Search Task

OSF ID: 48x6f | Registered: 2021-08-19T15:38:19.576915 | Showing: original version (1 version(s) on OSF)

Study Information

Hypotheses

We hypothesize that the mindset intervention training children receive will impact their search behavior. This may include overall more effective search behavior (resulting in better game performance, for example, increased likelihood of finding rewards across the duration of the search game), or more strategic search behavior (e.g., increased searching in high versus low reward environments).

Is there any additional blinding in this study?

The experimenter administering the search game will be naive to the mindset intervention condition that the child participated in prior to the search game.

Study design

We have a between-subjects design with 1 factor (condition) with two levels (strategic curiosity condition vs. control condition).

Randomization

Children will be randomly assigned to receive one of two mindset training conditions: (1) a strategic curiosity mindset intervention, which emphasizes the importance of embracing uncertainty, exploring the unknown, and updating prior beliefs in light of new information; or (2) a control condition, which does not teach a novel search strategy but is still highly engaging. The experimenter randomly selects which condition the child will participate in prior to the start of the study session.

Explanation of existing data

Data has been collected prior to registration, however data related to the primary research questions have not been analyzed (i.e., we have not yet examined whether the mindset intervention has a statistically significant effect on children’s search behavior). A subset of the data related to the exploratory analyses (caregiver behavior) was examined in May 2021 as part of a student’s honor’s thesis.

Data collection procedures

Participants will be identified online (via our website and social media pages as well as websites that include postings for research studies such as Reddit.com, CallForParticipants.com, ChildrenHelpingScience.com, and bilimsevercocuklar.com). We will share our flyer with community members (e.g., directors of local schools, teachers, hospital employees) so they can share our study information with families. Participants must be within the target age range (3.5 to 6.5 years old). Families will sign up for an appointment slot and be emailed a link to join an experimenter online over Zoom (https://zoom.us/). On the day of the study, families will join the Zoom call and complete the study procedures (parent consent, storybook task [mindset training], an exploration game, a parent questionnaire, a parent storybook task, the Dimensional Change Card Sort task, and study debriefing). After completing the study, families will be emailed a $10 gift card.

Sample size

Our target sample size in the United States is 132 subjects. We will attempt to recruit 147 subjects assuming a low level of attrition in the full study sample (10%).

Sample size rationale

Previous research has detected a medium effect of linguistic priming in a story context on curiosity (Dubey et al., 2019). Because this effect size is based on an adult sample, we took a conservative approach in our power analysis to correct for bias due to uncertainty (Anderson et al., 2017). A power analysis correcting for uncertainty using the BUCCS package in R revealed that 132 subjects with 80% power and an alpha level of .05 would be sufficient to detect an effect of medium size.

Stopping rule

We do not plan to stop data collection until we reach our target sample size.

Variables

Manipulated variables

Between-subjects manipulations: The key independent variable of the current study is the mindset intervention given to the child (i.e. the storybook that is read to the child prior to their participation in the search game). This independent variable has two levels: (1) Strategic Curiosity Condition and (2) Control Condition.

Measured variables

Search Attempts: The number of hiding spots a child looks in. Switch Factor: The difference between the number of rewards found and the number of search attempts before a child switches environments. Rewards Found: The number of rewards a child finds in a tank. Miss Steak: The number of consecutive misses (i.e., empty hiding spots searched), immediately before a child switches tanks. These variables provide important insight into children’s curiosity (how many times they choose to search), risk tolerance and persistence (how willing they are to continue searching despite failure), and ability to strategically search their environment (i.e, their ability to detect when an environment is low in rewards vs. high and modify search behavior accordingly). We also collected time-based variables of the above measures (e.g., time spent searching; time before switching) See exploratory analyses section for additional variables collected.

Indices

Children will have the opportunity to search for rewards in five different tanks. Some tanks will be low-reward environments (most hiding spots do not have rewards in them); other tanks are high-reward environments (most hiding spots have rewards in them). We are interested in how children’s search behavior varies as a function of the environment’s reward structure. Because we predict that children using a more strategic search strategy will search more in high-reward environments compared to low-reward environments, we will create composite scores that average children’s search behavior across environments. Children will have composite scores for search behaviors exhibited in low vs. high reward environments for each of our dependent measures of interest: search attempts, switch factor, rewards found, and miss streak. To measure children’s search behavior and success across the duration of the experiment, we will also compute total variables that compile children’s behavior across all environments (e.g., total number of rewards found).

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

We are particularly interested in whether children in the strategic curiosity mindset condition show more strategic search behavior than children in the control condition. For this analysis, we will conduct an independent sample t-test. Separate analyses will be conducted for each of our main dependent variables (Search Attempts; Switch Factor; Rewards Found; Miss Streak).

Transformations

If data is either positively or negatively skewed, we will apply appropriate transformations to center the data (e.g. logarithmic transformation in the case of positively skewed data; exponential transformation in the case of negatively skewed data).

Inference criteria

We will use the cut off of p &lt; .05 for determining whether the t-test is significant.

Data exclusion

Trials will be excluded from the analyses for any one of the following reasons: significant procedural errors (e.g. a technological error, researcher error), substantial caregiver interference with experimental protocol, or participant non-compliance (e.g., fails to engage fully in the task). If participants are unable to complete the tasks (e.g., due to physical or cognitive impairment, language barrier in instructions), their data will be excluded.

Missing data

Any missing data will be excluded at the trial-level (no imputations will be made). Any composite scores will be computed without the missing data.

Exploratory analysis

We will examine the relation between children’s search behavior and the following factors: demographic variables (e.g., age, gender), executive functioning skills, cultural context (United States vs. Turkey), caregiver behavior (e.g., parental talk during a joint book reading task), and caregiver survey measures (e.g., curiosity scale). We will also examine the total number of tanks (out of five) that children choose to explore, and how children’s search behavior changes across the five tanks, as it is possible that search behavior in later tanks declines (e.g., due to fatigue) or improves (e.g., due to increased familiarity with the game rules). We will also measure time-based versions of the variables listed in the above measured variables section (e.g., time spent searching within tanks, time spent searching between last ‘miss’ and decision to switch environments), but because we are unsure whether we will be able to reliably measure these variables in the online (Zoom) modality (e.g., due to connectivity issues), they are included as exploratory variables. We will also measure the number of repeat search attempts children make, their overall engagement, features of the testing environment (e.g., device type and screen size, distractions in home testing environment) and their comprehension/memory of the storybook intervention to examine whether these factors impact the effect of the mindset intervention on children’s search behavior. After the primary task (the search game), children will participate in two additional tasks. In exploratory analyses, we will examine whether the mindset training intervention or any of the other caregiver/home environment measured variables impacted children’s behavior on these two additional tasks. One task is an information trade task: children are prompted to trade in rewards gained during the search task to learn something new (dependent measure: number of rewards traded). The second task is a video task: children are shown a video of either a strategic search agent or a non-strategic search agent (dependent measure: children’s verbal prompting of the agent to modify or continue search behavior).
