Perceived and Objective-Descriptive Event Characteristics: Predicting Event-Related Changes in Subjective Well-being

OSF ID: 5dha3 | Registered: 2022-03-21T12:42:44.771257

Study Information

Hypotheses

This study aims to address two research questions: (1) How are perceived event characteristics and objective-descriptive event characteristics related to each other? (2) Which event characteristics are most predictive of event-related changes in subjective well-being? As empirical evidence for specific event characteristics is scarce, we decided not to formulate directed hypotheses for specific event characteristics.

Study design

The planned project is based on data from the Post-Event Changes Study—a longitudinal online study with five measurement occasions spread over 6 months. People interested in the Post-Event Changes Study first had to register online. The registration included providing informed consent and verifying the inclusion criteria. People were eligible for inclusion in the study if they were speaking German, if they were of legal age, and if they experienced one of the following five major life events in the last 5 weeks: death of a loved one, end of a relationship, friendship dissolution, involuntary end of an employment, or failing an important exam. Then, participants provided their email address, which was used to send them email invitations for the following five measurement occasions of the study (i.e., T1 to T5, which took place 0, 4, 8, 16, and 24 weeks after registration). Participants were invited to each measurement occasion regardless of whether they participated in a previous measurement occasion. At each measurement occasion, participants rated their subjective well-being, their personality traits, and Corona-related stress (as the study was conducting during the Covid-19 pandemic). In addition, at T1, participants provided demographic information and rated the perceived characteristics of the negative major life event they had recently experienced using the Event Characteristics Questionnaire (Luhmann et al., 2021). Furthermore, they answered additional questions regarding the causes, content, and consequences of the event (i.e., the objective-descriptive event characteristics). A more detailed description of the study design can be found at https://osf.io/hm3rv/?view_only=21bd4c93496345f783f63f43b369481c. We also attached a PDF-File of the study-design preregistration to this preregistration.

Explanation of existing data

At the time of this preregistration, T1, T2, and T3 of the Post-Event Changes Study have already been completed. To monitor the recruitment phase, the main investigators have extracted the sample sizes and the gender compositions for the five major life events that were considered in the Post-Event Changes Study. Furthermore, they inspected the absolute frequencies of all categorical objective-descriptive event characteristics to determine their final coding in the analyses (see below and the file “Codings_Objective-descriptive event characteristics.pdf” for details).

Data collection procedures

Participants of the Post-Event Changes Study were recruited via social media (e.g., using Facebook, Instagram, Twitter, Reddit). Furthermore, students from several German universities were contacted via email, and flyers were displayed at public spaces (e.g., hospitals, employment agencies, morticians, universities, and churches). As compensation for their participation, participants could opt in to receive individual feedback on their personality traits and partial course credit (if applicable). Furthermore, participants could take part in voucher raffles with increasing value after each measurement occasion and an additional voucher raffle after completion of all measurement occasions. The registration for the Post-Event Changes Study was open from May 2021 to December 2021. A more detailed description of the data collection procedures can be found at https://osf.io/hm3rv/?view_only=21bd4c93496345f783f63f43b369481c. People were able to register for the Post-Event Changes Study if they fulfilled the following inclusion criteria: - Legal age (18 years) - Sufficient German skills to understand the online survey - Experience of one of the following major life events in the last 5 weeks: death of a loved one, end of a relationship, friendship dissolution, involuntary end of an employment, or failing an important exam For this paper, we will additionally apply the following exclusion criteria to ensure data quality: - We will exclude data from all measurement occasions were participants provided either no or incorrect answers to the instructed response items (e.g., “To ensure data quality, please select the response option rather true”) - We will exclude participants who either provided no information on the timing of the experienced major life event or who indicated that the major life event occurred more than 6 weeks ago (i.e., outside the requested time frame: 5 weeks before registration + up to 1 week to complete T1)

Sample size

In total, 1,673 people registered to take part in the Post-Event Changes Study. However, we do not know our final sample size after applying the above-specified exclusion criteria.

Sample size rationale

As explained in more detail in the study-design preregistration (https://osf.io/hm3rv/?view_only=21bd4c93496345f783f63f43b369481c), we aimed for a sample size of 480 individuals at registration for each major life event. However, due to time constraints, the recruitment phase for this study was limited from May 2021 to December 2021. Within this time frame, we achieved the following sample sizes (at registration) for the five major life events: - Death of a loved one: n = 458 - Breakup of a relationship: n = 544 - End of a close friendship: n = 386 - Involuntary end of an employment: n = 140 - Failing an important exam: n = 145

Stopping rule

As preregistered, the recruitment for the Post-Event Changes Study stopped in December 2021. Data collection of T5 will be completed in June 2022.

Indices

1. Employed scales: We will first reverse responses of items if appropriate. Then, we will calculate mean scores for the nine perceived event characteristics, life satisfaction, positive affect, negative affect, and the Big Five personality traits. Means were only calculated if all items of a scale were answered. 2. Objective-descriptive event characteristics: We recoded some of the categorical objective-descriptive event characteristics to obtain higher sample sizes for each category (see the attached document “Codings_Objective-descriptive event characteristics.pdf” for details). For example, for the event characteristic “marriage”, we combined the two categories “are married” (N = 24) and “were married” (N = 3). Furthermore, the information of some of the objective-descriptive event characteristics will be combined into one variable for the analyses (see “Codebook.xlsx” for details). For example, relationship duration was assessed in months and years (in two separate variables) and we combined the information of these two variables. 3. Corona-related stress items (covariates): We will conduct a confirmatory factor analysis to evaluate the fit of our self-created Corona-stress scale. We will use indicator variable method and full information maximum likelihood for model estimation. We will evaluate model fit using goodness-of fit indices (CFI &gt; .05, RMSEA &lt; .08; Schermelleh-Engel et al., 2003). In case of bad model fit, we will look at modification indices to evaluate whether a certain item is responsible for model misfit. In a similar study, one item of this scale had to be dropped to obtain adequate fit. For the main analyses, we will calculate mean scores using the items of the final measurement model. Regarding the proportion of affected life domains, we will calculate the sum of affected life domains and divide this sum by the sum of applicable life domains (see “Codebook.xslx” for details).

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

To address Research Question 1 (association between perceived and objective-descriptive event characteristics), we will compute correlation coefficients (for metric and dichotomous objective-descriptive event characteristics) and one-way ANOVAs (for polytomous objective-descriptive event characteristics). To address Research Question 2 (predicting event-related changes in subjective well-being using objective-descriptive and perceived event characteristics), we will use longitudinal multilevel models with measurement occasions (Level 1) nested in participants (Level 2). In these multilevel models, life satisfaction, positive affect, and negative affect will serve as dependent variables (analyzed in separate models); time (weeks since the event), the perceived event characteristics, the objective-descriptive event characteristics, and their cross-level interactions with time will serve as predictors. For each of the five major life events, we will run separate analyses, as different objective-descriptive event characteristics were assessed for each major life event. Regarding the model specifications of these multilevel models, there are several valid possibilities (e.g., regarding the coding of time or the inclusion of covariates). We will therefore combine the longitudinal multilevel models with specification curve analysis (Simonsohn et al., 2020). Specification curve analysis is a possible way to deal with cases in which there are many possible, valid ways to analyze the data. The idea behind specification curve analysis is that all possible specifications are computed and then the robustness of the effects is tested among different specifications. In the attached file “Analysis Plan.pdf”, a more detailed description of our analysis strategy can be found.

Transformations

For the multilevel models to address Research Question 2, the following codings and transformations will be applied (also see the “Analysis Plan” for details): • We will recode negative affect in a way that higher values represent less negative affect and thus higher subjective well-being so that the interpretation of fixed effects remains consistent across all three components of subjective well-being. • All dependent variables (life satisfaction, positive affect, and negative affect), metric Level-2 predictors (perceived event characteristics, metric objective-descriptive event characteristics), metric Level-2 covariates (Big Five personality traits, age, and income), and metric Level-1 covariates (Corona-stress scale, proportion of affected life domains) will be standardized using their grand mean and standard deviation. • For all dichotomous objective-descriptive event characteristics, we will use dummy-coding. • For all polytomous categorical objective-descriptive event characteristics and polytomous Level-2 covariates (gender, education), we will employ weighted deviation coding using the contr.wec-function of the R package wec (Nieuwenhuis et al., 2017). • The time variable will reflect the time since the occurrence of the major life event. For each measurement occasion, we will therefore compute the temporal distance (in weeks) between the respective measurement occasion and the event occurrence. In the literature on adaptation of subjective well-being after major life events, different codings of time have been employed. We will consider a linear coding (as for example used by Lucas, 2007 and Luhmann et al., 2021) and a logarithmic coding of time (as for example done by Luhmann et al., 2012) in in the analyses.

Inference criteria

We will use a level of significance of α = .05 for our analyses of Research Question 1 and in Step 2 of specification curve analysis (i.e., the evaluation of the statistical significance of fixed effects and model comparisons). However, in Step 3 of the specification curve analysis, three statistical tests are computed that are used to evaluate the same question. Therefore, we will use Bonferroni-corrected level of significance of α = .05/3 = .0167 for this step (for a similar procedure see Beck &amp; Jackson, 2022).

Data exclusion

Except for the above-described exclusion criteria to ensure data quality (correct answers to our instructed response items), no other exclusion criteria will be applied.

Missing data

We will compute mean scores for our scales only if all items for a certain scale are answered. Furthermore, to ensure that all analyses are based on the same effective sample and that model comparisons are possible, we will only use measurement occasions with complete data on all predictors and outcomes for a certain participant.
