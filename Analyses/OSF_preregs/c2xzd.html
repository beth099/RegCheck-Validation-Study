Hypotheses

H1. Active Groups → Decentering → Reappraisal → Well-Being The data will support a model containing Decentering, Reappraisal, and Wellbeing. (see attached 'SEM and Power Analysis' document H1). We will test overall model fit for this hypothesis, although we expect all the paths listed to be significant- main contrast for group is all 3 active [MC, SM, CC] vs. control [AC]. H2. Process Dissociation We will test overall model fit for this hypothesis, although we expect all the paths listed to be significant. Main contrast for group is between [MC] and [SM], and we expect opposing effects for the two groups on interoception and stress mindset (see attached 'SEM and Power Analysis' document H2). H3. We expect both paths from group, i.e., group → interoception and group → stress mindset paths to be significant, indicating that CC contributes to well-being through a combination of improved interoception and improved stress mindset (see attached SEM document H3). Main contrast for group is between [CC] and [SM]. H4. We expect both paths from group, i.e., group → mindfulness and group → stress mindset paths to be significant indicating that mindfulness amplifies the stress mindset intervention effects. Main contrast for group is between [CC] and [SM]. This model will be tested the same as H3, but replacing the interoception variable with a mindfulness measure (see attached 'SEM and Power Analysis' document H4). H5. Daily Arousal and Coping Relationship To examine the relationship between daily arousal and coping as a function of group, we will employ a multilevel model using the lme4 library in R. We will test whether some groups weaken the association between arousal and coping more than others, anticipating that coping will be lowest when arousal is high as a main effect (see attached 'SEM and Power Analysis' document H5).

Study design

This will be a mixed-model, multilevel clinical trial design, with four groups to determine 1) if mindfulness instructions uniquely increase mindful state; 2) if stress management instructions uniquely increase stress mindset scale; 3) if both mindfulness + stress mindset instruction increase reappraisal; 4) if a combined lesson plan increases reappraisal more than either condition alone; and 5) the relative contributions of state mindfulness and reappraisal on decentering + stress level. The intervention period will last for 3 weeks beginning with the first training session and will be completed entirely on an online survey platform (Qualtrics). The between-participant factor will be the experimental group (4 levels): Stress Management [SM] vs. Mindfulness w/Choice [MC] vs. Active Control [AC] vs. Combined Condition [CC]. The within-participant factor will be time. There are 3 different ways to operationalize time: Across study, time has 2 levels, as Participants will be assessed more comprehensively using validated questionnaires at baseline and post-intervention. Across sessions, time has up to 21 levels for arousal and coping, as these measures are included in both the 6 training sessions and 15 optional daily check-ins Across sessions, time has up to 6 levels for other study variables (i.e., interoception, decentering, reappraisal, and stress mindset) measured during the 6 training sessions (participants will do 2 training sessions / week x 3 weeks) Within session, time has 2 levels, as participants complete some check-in measures both at the start and end of the training session. Please refer to the measures section for a detailed description of the measures that will be used at each level of analysis.

Randomization

Participants will be randomly assigned to one of the four conditions after completing the baseline questionnaires and prior to the start of the intervention session. Randomization will be performed autonomously with the Qualtrics survey platform, selecting a random group value (1,2, 3, or 4) without replacement. This is equivalent to block randomization, where each participant will be randomly assigned to one of the four equally sized, predetermined blocks. The random number list used to create these three blocks will be Qualtrics’ internal randomization function. https://www.qualtrics.com/support/survey-platform/survey-module/survey-flow/standard-elements/randomizer/

Data collection procedures

Participants will be undergraduate students from the PSY100 class at the University of Toronto Mississauga and be recruited from the UTM PSY100 participant pool (https://app.utm.utoronto.ca/experiments-signup/). The Psy100 sign-up portal will present students with an overview of the inclusion/exclusion criteria and a link to an online brief screener where participants can assess their eligibility and register for the study. We will not retain screening data, but it will be a web form that is automated to only let people who fit our inclusion/exclusion criteria progress to the study consent form and booking a workshop. Inclusion: Participants must self-describe as being healthy, i.e., students who self-describe as healthy and affirm no ongoing diagnosis of a cognitive, mood, or health disorder which would preclude filling out brief daily surveys over the study period. Additionally, students must believe themselves are able to participate in all aspects of the study. As such, students must be able to communicate and follow instructions in English and see with normal or corrected-to-normal vision. Exclusion: individuals with a current cognitive or mood disorder, including substance use disorders, or previous diagnosis of such a disorder within the past year. While this program may be helpful to participants with ongoing disorders, the present proposal focuses on a low-vulnerability population first as a low-risk proof-of-concept study.

Variables

Manipulated variables

We will have 4 independent variables: Three variables are repeated measures (within person), examining training effects at 3 different levels: Trait effects: Study Period (2 levels: Baseline vs. Post-intervention) Longitudinal effects: Across Sessions (6 training sessions + up to 15 check-ins for stress, coping and practice ratings) State effects: Within Session (2 levels: pre vs. post training lesson x up to 6 sessions) The final variable is the training Group to which participants were randomly assigned (4 levels): Stress Management [SM] vs. Mindfulness w/Choice [MC] vs. Active Control [AC] vs. Combined Condition [CC] Each condition will have 2 training sessions per week for 3 weeks. Each lesson will include both training and assessment (i.e., ratings on state, interoception, decentering, reappraisal, and stress mindset). Each session will be about 20 minutes long and each link will expire when the next link comes out. In the Stress Management (SM) condition, participants will receive training on the power of an adaptive stress mindset. They will learn how to acknowledge, accept, and utilize stress and adopt a stress-is-enhancing mindset within 3 weeks of training. In the Mindfulness with Choice (MC) condition, participants will receive guided meditations and advice for mindfulness practice. At the beginning and end of each lesson, participants will be asked to practice open monitoring mindfulness for 3 min and write down their thoughts briefly. Participants will be provided options so participants can actively choose their preferred mindfulness techniques to practice. In the literature, choice has been shown to improve participant engagement and outcomes. In the Combined Condition (CC), participants will receive alternated mindfulness and stress mindset training each week. The materials used for mindfulness will be the same as in for MC condition and the materials for stress mindset will be the same from SM condition. And similar to MC, at the beginning and end of each lesson, participants will be asked to practice open monitoring mindfulness for 3 min and write down their thoughts briefly. They will also be provided options so participants can actively choose their preferred mindfulness techniques to practice. In the Active Control (AC) condition, participants will spend 3 minutes writing about the cause of their stress for the day and then play tetris for 5min in each training lesson.

Trait Variables [Measured only at Baseline and Post-Intervention] Participants will complete the baseline assessment online pre-study that includes a consent form, brief demographic survey, a training scheduling form, and then the core assessment package (CAP) which measures the main variables we want to track over time. State Variables [Measured Before and After Training Sessions x up to 6 sessions] In order to track state variables across sessions, each group will complete 2-minute brief ratings at the beginning and end of each training lesson (up to 6 lessons). 1) Arousal - Question: How much energy or tension do you feel in your body right now? - Anchors: Low Arousal to High Arousal 2) Coping - Question: How able are you to cope with what is happening in your life right now? - Anchors: Coping Well to Completely Overwhelmed 3) Mood Board (Positive Affect + Negative Affect) Positive Affect and Negative was assessed using the mood board, which is a visual representation of positive and negative emotions on a spectrum, ranging from intense emotions to mild emotions (Walsh et al., 2019). Participants were asked to select at least 1 out of 32 items that represented their feelings at that moment. Interoception, Decentering, Reappraisal, and Stress Mindset are each rated on a 5 point likert scale with options [strongly disagree, disagree, neither agree nor disagree, agree, strongly agree]. Each variable is composed of the simple average of ratings on 2 questions, with reverse scored items indicated below with an (R): 4) Interoception: I am aware of sensations in my body right now I feel very detached from my body right now (R) 5) Decentering: I can watch my thoughts and emotions come and go like clouds I am more than my thoughts and feelings 6) Reappraisal: I am able to make myself think about it in a way that helps me stay calm. I am able to see that my current situation also has its positive sides. 7) Stress Mindset: The effects of stress are negative and should be avoided. (R) Experiencing stress facilitates my learning and growth. 3. Check-In Variables [Measured During the 15 non-training day check-ins] Arousal and coping are each measured with a single 10 point ‘slider’ item. Each group will complete up to 21 ratings, as these measures are included in both the 6 training lessons and 15 optional daily check-ins. 1) Arousal - Question: How much energy or tension do you feel in your body right now? - Anchors: Low Arousal to High Arousal 2) Coping - Question: How able are you to cope with what is happening in your life right now? - Anchors: Coping Well to Completely Overwhelmed

For each of the 3 constructs, we will employ a factor analysis in R using the ‘psych’ and ‘GPArotation’ libraries, and constrain the factor analysis to produce a single factor. The analysis will be run using the ‘oblimin’ (non-orthogonal) rotation using maximum likelihood estimation to generate the factor scores. Transformations

We will first inspect the data for normality and other assumptions required for regression, and we will transform the data accordingly if normality assumptions are violated. We will follow the recommendations by de Jonge and van der Loo (2013). The a priori (central) hypotheses all only employ group as a predictor, and so no transformations of the dependent variables are anticipated. However, for exploratory hypotheses, we will be exploring the role of both across session and within session variables as moderators, e.g., does the within session reduction of stress predict an across session reduction of stress? So for these exploratory tests we will separate out level 1 and level 2 predictors (e.g., within session stress variance vs. across session stress variance. To do this, level 1 predictors (i.e., within session) will be participant-centered, i.e., participant means will be subtracted from each pre and post session score. Conversely, level 2 predictors will average across pre and post session scores to account for the participant mean while ignoring within session variance. Reference: de Jonge, E., &amp; van der Loo, M. (2013). An introduction to data cleaning with R. Statistics Netherlands. Retrieved from https://cran.r-project.org/doc/contrib/de_Jonge+van_der_Loo-Introduction_to_data_cleaning_with_R.pdf

Inference criteria

We will use the ‘lavaan’ package for SEM modeling in R for hypotheses 1-4. To test each of these models, we will use standard procedures for evaluating model fit. There are numerous fit statistics generated by the lavaan package, and we will focus on the Root Mean Square Error of Approximation (RMSEA) to evaluate overall model fit. We will interpret RMSEA &lt; .10 as adequate fit, &lt; .08 as good fit, and &lt; .05 as excellent fit, and discuss the results accordingly. RMSEA &gt; .10 will be considered poor fit and not valid grounds for inference, although alternative models may then be explored after reporting the failure of the a priori hypothesized models. For hypothesis 5, a coefficient from the multilevel model implemented in the ‘lme4’ package will be the grounds for inference, with a p &lt; .01 considered evidence for rejecting the null hypothesis, i.e., that group does not moderate the effect of arousal on coping. For exploratory analyses, we will discuss effect sizes and 95% confidence intervals for all analyses but will not make inferences based p values to protect the familywise error rates.

Data exclusion

Exclusion Prior to Analysis: Participants will be excluded from data analysis if they: 1) incorrectly answer 2 or more of the 3 catch questions during the baseline measure; 2) miss 2 or more of the 6 training sessions; 3) communicate to us that they wish to discontinue participation. No constraints on daily check-ins will be made as the check-ins are not part of the training intervention but instead provide supplemental data for exploratory analysis.

Missing data

Missing data will be left as missing, no imputation.

Exploratory analysis

We will explore latent change score modelling whereby we model the 6 training sessions and in that case will attempt to use the slope of change for the variable decentering, reappraisal, interoception and stress mindset, and state well-being, and if the model fits are adequate, we will include this in the main report.
