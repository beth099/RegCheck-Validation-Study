Study Information

Hypotheses

1) In the MDS solution, hapa faces will be distinct from Asian and White faces high in prototypicality, such that a category boundary will unambiguously separate these groups of faces. Thus, hapa faces will be linearly separable from high prototypic Asian and White faces. 2) Hapa faces will lie significantly closer to low prototypic Asian and White faces than to high prototypic Asian and White faces in the similarity space. We do not have a specific hypothesis regarding whether hapa faces will be completely linearly separable from low prototypic Asian and White faces. 3) Latina faces will be linearly separable from Asian, White, and hapa faces. 4) Latina faces will lie closer to hapa faces than to Asian and White faces in the similarity space.

Study design

This is a MDS study (Kruskal &amp; Wish, 1978). Participants will make pairwise similarity judgments of 14 Asian, 14 White, 14 hapa, and 14 Latina faces. Half of the Asian, White, and Latina faces will be high in protoypicality (based on norms collected by Ma, Correll, and Wittenbrink, 2015) and half will be be low in prototypicality. We will then use PROXSCAL to generate MDS scaling solutions. These solutions will reveal whether category boundaries separate the different face types in the similarity space.

Randomization

120 pairs of faces will be presented in a random order to each participant for similarity ratings. The faces in each pair will be drawn at random for each participant from a pool of 14 Asian, 14 White, 14 hapa, and 14 Latina faces, with the exceptions that a) the same face cannot be both members of a pair, and b) the same face cannot appear more than once in any three consecutive trials.

Explanation of existing data

Data collection procedures

Data will be collected online through mturk. Participants will be recruited from the United States. There are no other exclusion criteria. One session takes approximately 20 minutes to complete, and each participant will be paid $2.50.

Sample size

We will recruit approximately 1412 participants.

Sample size rationale

Our use of 56 faces (14 in each of four race categories) yields 56x55 or 3,080 pairwise comparisons. To produce a stable MDS solution, we will collect 50 observations per pair, requiring 3,080 x 50 or 154,000 similarity ratings total. Because each participant will provide 120 similarity ratings, 154,000/120 or 1283 participants are required. We will recruit 10% more than this total to allow for attrition, resulting in a total sample size of 1412.

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

MDS will be used to depict the locations of the 14 Asian, 14 White, 14 hapa, and 14 Latina faces in similarity space. After the MDS program minimizes the number of dimensions required to create the similarity space, we will correlate the dimensions with subjective and objective ratings of the faces to draw inferences regarding which perceptual variables the axes represent. Mean inter-category distances will be computed to assess the tightness of each cluster. These will be submitted to one-way ANOVA to determine if the types of faces vary in perceptual homogeneity. To determine whether the hapa faces occupy their own position in the similarity space (at the category level), mean distances between every hapa face and every face in each of the monoracial categories will be computed and compared to 0 via t-tests. To determine whether any given pair of categories is completely linearly separable, we will first visually inspect the MDS solution to see whether a line can be drawn that separates all the exemplars of one category from all the exemplars of another. We will then compute the average distance of the two exemplars of each category that are closest to each other and compare that distance to 0 via t-tests. We will also test whether exemplars that are deeply embedded in the similarity space of their category (i.e., exemplars that are perceptually unambiguous members of that category according to the MDS solution) are more likely to be labeled with that category in explicit categorization tasks. To do so, we will compute the ratio of an exemplar's mean distance from all exemplars of all other categories to its mean distance all exemplars of its own category. We will then correlate this ratio for each exemplar with the proportion of concordant race classifications for that exemplar in categorization experiments run in our laboratory.

Inference criteria

We will use an alpha level of .05 to determine significance in all statistical analyses.

Data exclusion

We will remove participants who used the same similarity rating 90% of the time (on the grounds that they were likely not giving proper attention to the task). We will include a captcha to prevent bots from partaking in our study. We also plan to analyze the data with and without participants who completed the entire experiment in 2 and 3 standard deviations less than the median finish time.
