Inner Speech and Sustained Attention: An Online Reaction-Time Experiment

OSF ID: stfn5 | Registered: 2021-06-16T09:24:18.577279

Study Information

Hypotheses

H1: Task-relevant inner experience generally will be associated with faster reaction times to the prompt. H2: Specifically, task-relevant inner speech will be associated with faster reaction times than other types of inner experience. H3: The proportions of types of inner experience will resemble those found in other experience sampling studies (each 20-25 %; Heavey &amp; Hurlburt, 2008). H4 (exploratory): Self-regulatory inner speech may be more important as the experiment progresses. If this is the case, we predict an interaction between the inner speech factor and time with the difference between task-relevant inner speech and task-relevant other experience becoming more pronounced over time.

Is there any additional blinding in this study?

As the experiment takes place online, there will not be any interaction between participants and experimenters prior to or during the experiment itself. The participants will not be informed of the hypotheses prior to participating in the experiment but as there experiment does not include randomized controlled trials, traditional blinding is not involved.

Study design

Within subjects design with experience type (inner speech, inner seeing, sensory awareness, unsymbolized thinking, feelings) and task-relevant focus (yes or no) predicting reaction time.

Randomization

The predictors will occur during the experiment within participant so we will not randomize conditions. The prompts will occur at eight times during the experiment - the eight wait durations are fixed but their order is shuffled for each participant.

Explanation of existing data

Data collection procedures

We will collect data through the online experiment platform Prolific and recruit participants with English as their first language and access to a desktop computer. Participants will be excluded from the experiment if they fail to respond to three prompts and will be paid a show-up fee proportional to the time they spent before being excluded. Participants who complete the experiment - lasting approximately 20 minutes - will be paid £3. Participants who fail to respond to three prompts will be excluded and paid £1.

Sample size

We aim to recruit at least 120 participants who complete the total task.

Sample size rationale

We base our target sample size on a power analysis conducted on pilot data from 10 participants with R package 'simr'. We want to be able to detect a difference of 40 ms between task-relevant experiences task-irrelevant experiences. With 120 participants, we will have a power of 94.00% (95 % CI = [83.45, 98.75])

Stopping rule

Variables

Manipulated variables

At each experience prompt, we measure the following: Two levels of task-relevance: Yes, No. Five levels of experience type: inner speech, inner seeing, unsymbolized thinking, sensory awareness, feelings. Three levels of experience temporality: past, present, or future. Two levels of experience awareness: Yes, No. If participants answer 'Inner voice', we ask them to what extent they agree that their inner voice was dialogic, condensed compared to their normal, out-loud speech, contained other people's voices, and that they were evaluating their own behavior using their inner voice. We also include a free answer question asking whether participants have anything else they would like to add about their experience. At the end of the experiment, we ask participants whether they talked to themselves to keep focused on the task.

Measured variables

Answers to the questions noted above, reaction times to the prompts, number of times a participant misses the prompt. The answers to the questions will be used to predict the reaction times.

Indices

We will take the mode of each participant's answers to the experience type question where they indicated that their thoughts were task-relevant to get a measure of individual strategies.

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

H1: We will use a Generalized Linear Mixed-Effect Model (including random effects for individual participants) with task-relevance predicting reaction time. Because the dependent variable consists of reaction time data, we will use a Gamma distribution or inverse Gaussian to model it (depending on which distribution fits the data best). H2: We will use a Generalized Linear Mixed-Effect Model (including random effects for individual participants) with experience type (inner voice versus not) and task-relevance predicting reaction time. We expect an interaction effect between the two predictors with task-relevance predicting reaction time to a significantly larger extent at the inner voice level of experience. Because the dependent variable consists of reaction time data, we will use a Gamma distribution or inverse Gaussian to model it (depending on which distribution fits the data best). H3: The proportions of types of inner experience will resemble those found in other experience sampling studies (each 20-25 %; Heavey &amp; Hurlburt, 2008). We will assess the proportions of types of inner experience across trials and compare them with the proportions found in previous literature.

Transformations

Inference criteria

We will use the standard p&lt;.05 criteria for determining if the predictors and the interaction are reach statistical significance. When deciding between the different GLLM models (Gamma distribution, inverse Gaussian, etc.), we will use the fit indices like AIC, BIC, etc. to decide the best model.

Data exclusion

We will exclude trials with reaction times below 200 ms as there is evidence that humans cannot react faster than this. We will also exclude reaction times above 1000 ms as this cut-off is part of the attention check in the experiment.

Missing data

Participants who do not complete the experiment because they fail to respond to three prompts will be excluded from the data analysis.

Exploratory analysis

We will get the mode of the individual participants' answer to the experience type question when they reported their thoughts to be on-task to test if different strategies are associated with differences in reaction times. We will use the questions that are not directly pertinent to the hypotheses (temporality of experience, awareness of experience, and the nature of the inner voice if they choose the 'inner voice' option) to compare our study with previous experience sampling and mind wandering studies to see if the online format is associated with interesting differences. We will test whether self-regulatory inner speech is more important later in the experiment by exploring an interaction effect between inner speech and trial progression.
