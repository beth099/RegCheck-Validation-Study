Reducing popularity bias in consumer choice

OSF ID: v734e | Registered: 2020-07-08T01:38:37.544945 | Showing: original version (1 version(s) on OSF)

Study Information

Hypotheses

Hypothesis 1. Preferences for two alternative online products will be positively related to the difference between their online mean quality ratings - with the product that has the higher relative rating being more likely to receive higher preference ratings Hypothesis 2. Preferences for two alternative online products will be positively related to the size of the sample on which online quality ratings are based, with the product having the larger sample of reviews (hereafter the "more reviewed product" or MRP) receiving higher preference ratings. This effect was termed a "popularity bias" by Powell et al. 2017 Hypothesis 3. The hypothesized popularity bias (H2) will be found when the less reviewed product (LRP) has a higher mean quality rating than the MRP Hypothesis 4. Adding an explanation of the differences between the review sample sizes for the MRP and LRP that does not involve product quality will reduce overall preference for the MRP (i.e. will reduce the H2 popularity bias).

Study design

The experiment will use a 2 (sample size explanation) x 5 (online mean quality rating of the more-reviewed product) x 5 (review-score discrepancy: more reviewed – less reviewed) mixed factorial design, with the last two factors manipulated within-subjects. Sample size explanation will be manipulated between subjects. Details of the levels of the within-subjects factors are given in the attached file.

Randomization

We will use group randomization, where each online participant will be randomly assigned to one of the two explanation conditions (no explanation, explanation). The random number list used to assign participants to groups will be created using the java-based programming language jsPsych.

Data collection procedures

Online participants will be recruited via response to a description of the experiment posted on Amazon Mechanical Turk. Participation will be restricted to US residents who have achieved MTurk master worker status. Completion of the task is expected to take approximately 10 minutes and participants will be paid US $2.00 on completion. As noted in the Design Plan, performance on the 4 attention check trial items will be used to determine inclusion in the final analysis sample. Two attention check items will show product pairs where the rating of the MRP is well above that of the LRP (e.g., +1.9 points). The other two check items will be “extreme disadvantage” pairs where the mean rating of the MRP is well below that of the LRP (e.g., -1.9 points). It is assumed that participants who understand task instructions and attend to the task should always choose the MRP for the first pair of check items and the LRP for the second pair. Those who fail both items for either type of check will be excluded from data analyses.

Sample size

We aim to recruit up to 300 individuals via Mechanical Turk.

Sample size rationale

The sample size was based on the goal of matching (or exceeding) the sample size and statistical power of the study by Powell et al. (2017). The Powell et al. (2017) study used a single group design with N= 138. Our study will use two independent groups with an expected n of 150 per group. We expect some attrition (up to 5%) from this sample due to participants failing the attention check questions.

Stopping rule

Data collection will be terminated when 300 complete data sets have been returned via Mechanical Turk

Measured variables

The chief dependent measure will be each participant's product preference rating on the six-point scale for each of the 25 target test trials. This data will be coded and analysed in two ways: 1) As a raw rating on the six-point scale; 2) As a dichotomous response where preference ratings of 4-6 for the more reviewed product are coded as positive preference for that product and ratings of 1-3 are code as preference for the less-reviewed-product. The latter coding allows us to treat this measure as the probability that a participant would choose the more-reviewed product on a given trial. This will allow a direct comparison between our data and the results of Powell et al. (2017).

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

1) We will use a (5) (mean quality of MRP) x (5) (discrepancy between MRP and LRP quality) x 2 (explanation group) mixed-model analysis of variance (ANOVA) to analyze the a) raw preference rating and b) dichotomized preference data. The first two of the analysis factors will be within-subjects. Linear and quadratic trend contrasts will be planned to examine effects on these two within-subjects factors. All group and repeat contrasts will be orthogonal. Decision-wise Type 1 error rate for all main effects, 2-way, and 3-way interactions, will be controlled at alpha =.05. 2) For the three experimental conditions where the discrepancy between the quality rating shown for the MRP and LRP is small (i.e. discrepancy = +0.1, 0 or -0.1) we will run a follow-up comparison of participants' dichotomized preference choices against a chance value of 0.5, using a series of one-sample, two-tailed t-tests with alpha = 0.05 for each test. These tests will be run separately for the 'no explanation" and 'explanation' conditions.

Transformations

The only recoding planned is the dichotomization of preference rating data, which can be treated as the probability of choosing the more-viewed product, as described previously.

Inference criteria

Statistical significance will be determined by ANOVA F values that are below an probability criterion (p) of 0.05. Because all group and repeat contrasts will be orthogonal decision-wise Type 1 error rate will be controlled at alpha =.05, without the need for Bonferroni correction. All statistical tests will be non-directional (two-tailed).

Data exclusion

Prior to data analysis participants will be screened on their performance on the four attention check trials. Two attention check items will show product pairs where the rating of the MRP is well above that of the LRP (e.g., +1.9 points). The other two check items will be “extreme disadvantage” pairs where the mean rating of the MRP is well below that of the LRP (e.g., -1.9 points). It is assumed that participants who understand task instructions and attend to the task should always choose the MRP for the first pair of check items and the LRP for the second pair. Those who fail both items for either type of check will be excluded from data analyses. We do not plan to use any other exclusion criteria.

Missing data

The jsPsych code that will run the experiment will be written in a manner that minimizes the chance of missing data (e.g. a response to an item will be required on each trial before the participant is allowed to proceed to the next trial). If data are missing because a participant leaves the online experiment before completion then that data will not be used and a replacement participant will be recruited.

Exploratory analysis

Powell et al. (2017) report predictions for dichotomized product choices in each of the 25 within-subject experimental conditions based on a formal Bayesian model that gives approximately equal weight to the mean quality rating and sample size information (number of reviews reported) for each product. We plan to compare the fit of this model to obtained product choices by calculating the mean-squared error between predicted and observed probability of choosing the more-reviewed product. This analysis will be carried out separately for the no explanation and explanation groups. We also plan to develop and test an alternative computational model that gives more weight to sample size information. The details of this model are still being finalized. We anticipate that the fit of this model to the data from the explanation and explanation groups will be tested by using a maximum likelihood algorithm to estimate model parameters maximize model fit to the data.
