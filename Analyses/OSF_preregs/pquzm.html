Falling Under the Lens of Suspicion: Expressing “Just Enough” Emotion in 911 Calls

OSF ID: pquzm | Registered: 2020-02-05T00:12:18.904958

Study Information

Title

“Falling Under the Lens of Suspicion: Expressing ‘Just Enough’ Emotion in 911 Calls” Study 2

Authors

Jessica Salerno, Samantha Bean, Nicholas Duran

Description

Anecdotal evidence suggests that people might fall under suspicion when they violate others’ expectations of appropriate emotion by expressing too much or too little emotion. This project focuses on the earliest point someone might become a suspect in a criminal investigation—when they call 911 to report a violent crime. Within this project, this second study will use 911 calls we created with voice actors. We have a call set of 9 actors (4 men and 5 women) who each express low, moderate, and high emotion within a set call script. This study will include measures of emotion, suspiciousness, and support for police resources and the caller to be investigated as a subject in the crime.

Hypotheses

We predict an interaction between caller gender and caller emotion. For male callers, we predict a curvilinear pattern, such that people will be more suspicious of low and high emotion relative to moderate emotion. In contrast, for female callers, we expect that low emotion will be significantly more suspicious than both moderate or high emotion (which we do not expect to differ).

Study design

We have a 2x3 between subjects design: gender of the caller (male, female) and emotion of the caller (low, moderate, high). Participants will be randomly assigned to listen to one phone call.

Explanation of existing data

Data collection procedures

Participants will be recruited through Amazon’s Mechanical Turk and will be compensated $1.50 for their time. Participants must be at least 18 years of age.

Sample size

Based on the effect size for the gender x emotion interaction (pη2=.05), we need 244 participants to achieve 90% power to detect the interaction effect. Based on our prediction that a moderator will attenuate the original effect (i.e., we expect the difference between moderate and high emotion that we predict for male callers, to disappear for female callers), we will follow recommendations to multiply the required sample to detect the interaction effect (Giner-Sorolla, Powering your interaction, 2018), which equals 976 participants.

Variables

Manipulated variables

We will manipulate what level of emotion participants hear in the 911 call (low, moderate, or high), and we will manipulate if this comes from a woman or man expressing this emotion.

Measured variables

Outcome variables for this study will include how suspicious they found the caller, likelihood that the caller was involved in the crime, support for police resources and involvement in investigating the caller. Additionally, we will assess the demographics of participants. Open-ended question at the beginning of survey “Please tell us about your first impressions of the caller. Please write a few sentences describing your impression of the person and behaviors that led you to that impression” (2) Eight impressions scales derived from research identifying markers of deception in 911 callers (Harpster, Adams, &amp; Jarvis; 2009) and linguistic deception literature (Duran et al., 2010). Each scale was based on 5-items with 5-point response scales from Not at all to Extremely assessing the caller’s: Urgency for victim (e.g., “Caller seemed worried about the victim”) Urgency for self (e.g., “Caller seemed concerned for their own safety”) Emotionality (e.g., “Caller was hysterical”; α=.92) Cognitive load (e.g., “Caller seemed to have trouble thinking clearly”; α=.87) Impression management (e.g., “Caller was actively trying to make a good impression on the dispatcher”; (α=.74) Information management (e.g., “Caller seemed to be concerned about how much detailed information they should provide”; (α=.58) Voice characteristics (e.g., “caller’s volume changed a lot”) Suspiciousness/violation of expectations (e.g., “I was genuinely surprised about how the caller acted”) (3) Moral agency and moral patiency scales adapted from the literature. Both scales were based on 5-items with 6-point response scales from Strongly disagree to Strongly agree. Moral patiency (e.g., “The caller seems very vulnerable”) Moral agency (e.g., “The caller seems like someone who could be capable of doing immoral things”) (4) Outcome variables assessing perceptions of suspiciousness, sincerity, and potential role in crime of the caller. Additionally, support for police resources and involvement in investigating the caller.

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

We will conduct between-subjects ANOVA to test the impact of our manipulations on all continuous outcomes. We will conduct a multiple regression to conduct exploratory analyses regarding which of the five impression scales predict suspiciousness and willingness to spend police resources on investigating them as a suspect.

Transformations

Inference criteria

Data exclusion

We will exclude participants who spontaneously indicate that they believe the call was a fake 911 call. This will be assessed in an open-ended question asking for their initial thoughts and comments about the call they heard, before completing other measures for the study. Before conducting our main analyses, we will look to see if any of the actors were not believable or were outliers in terms of their emotionality. First, if more than half of participants spontaneously report that they thought the call was fake, we will consider cutting people who heard that actors’ call. In addition, if participants rate an actor to be +/- 2SD in terms of emotionality (i.e., making them an outlier actor, e.g., happen to have given a much less emotional performance than all other actors in the “high emotion” category), we will exclude participants who heard that actor’s call.

Missing data

Exploratory analysis

We will conduct exploratory analyses regarding which of the eight impressions scales predict perceived suspiciousness and willingness to spend police resources on investigating them as a suspect. Additionally, we will conduct exploratory analyses with moral agency and patiency as potential mediators of the effect of the gender x emotion expression interaction on suspiciousness.
