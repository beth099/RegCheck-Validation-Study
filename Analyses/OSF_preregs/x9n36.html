Study Information

Title

Stimulus vs response task set formation determines the boundaries of the CSE: Experiment 3

Authors

Lauren Grant, Daniel Weissman

Description

Cognitive control is often context-specific. For example, the congruency sequence effect (CSE) in distractor-interference tasks - one measure of cognitive control - is reduced when the sensory modality in which task stimuli appear changes across consecutive trials relative to when it repeats (see Experiment 1 from Hazeltine et al., 2011). Recently, we conceptually replicated this result without feature integration or contingency confounds (Grant et al., submitted, Experiment 1; Grant et al., in progress). We also showed that sensory modalities serve as boundaries for the CSE only when they are linked to different task sets (Grant et al., submitted, Experiment 2). In the present study, we wish to explore whether modality-specific CSEs vanish when the interval separating the initial distractor and the subsequent target in the prime-probe word task (wherein the words left, right, up, and down serve as both distractors and targets) is increased from 750 ms (as in our prior study) to 1150 ms.

Hypotheses

There are 2 hypotheses. 1. The CSE will remain modality-specific as in our prior studies for either or both of two reasons. First, participants can easily categorize each trial as "visual" or "auditory" (Hazeltine et al., 2011). Second, participants will respond only to the probe in each trial, which reduces CSE magnitude, rather than to both the prime and probe, which increases CSE magnitude (Grant &amp; Weissman, 2019). Critically, this hypothesis predicts a 3-way interaction among modality condition (repeat, switch), previous trial congruency (congruent, incongruent), and current trial congruency (congruent, incongruent). 2. The CSE will not remain modality-specific for one of two reasons. First, participants may have time to silently read the distractor word on the screen before the target word appears. If so, they may be able to use a common verbal code in both visual and auditory trials, thereby allowing participants to use a single modality-general task set to process both visual and auditory trials. Second, participants may have time to switch from one task set (e.g., visual) to another (e.g., auditory) before the target appears. If so, they be better able to modulate the response cued by the distractor to engender a CSE before the target appears. This hypothesis assumes that one must switch to the current-trial task set before one can "apply" one's memory of previous-trial congruency to modulate the response cued by the distractor in the current trial. These hypotheses predict an interaction between previous trial congruency and current trial congruency (i.e., a CSE) but not necessarily a 3-way interaction among modality condition, previous trial congruency, and current trial congruency.

Study design

The experiment will have a within-subjects design with four factors: stimulus modality (visual, auditory), stimulus modality repetition (repeat, switch), previous trial congruency (congruent, incongruent), and current trial congruency (congruent, incongruent). In each run of 64 trials (12 total), the trials corresponding to these four factors will be presented in a first-order counterbalanced sequence. There will also be a practice run consisting of 64 trials. The task design will be conceptually similar to that of Hazeltine et al. (2011, Experiment 1) with one major exception. Specifically, participants will have a longer interval between the prime and probe. Thus, the timing in each trial will be as follows: (a) prime for 250 ms, (b) blank screen for 1150 ms, (c) probe for 250 ms, (4) blank screen for 1350 ms.

Data collection procedures

Participants will be recruited through the University of Michigan subject pool. Participants will receive course credit for participation. Participants will be excluded if they have any history of seizures, concussions, neuropsychiatric diseases or disorders, head trauma, or uncorrected visual or hearing impairments. They will also be excluded if they are taking psychoactive medications. The data will be collected starting in November of 2019.

Sample size

The sample will include usable data from 32 participants.

Sample size rationale

The sample size is based on power analyses that we ran using G*Power. Power analyses were based on two interactions. The first interaction was the three-way interaction we observed in the first experiment of this series among modality condition (repeat, switch), previous trial congruency (congruent, incongruent), and current trial congruency (congruent, incongruent). The second interaction was the two-way interaction we observed in the first experiment of this series between previous trial congruency and current trial congruency in the modality switch condition. The results indicated that collecting usable data from 32 participants would provide over 99% power to observe both of these interactions.

Variables

Manipulated variables

We will manipulate stimulus modality (visual, auditory), stimulus modality repetition (repeat, switch), previous trial congruency (congruent, incongruent), and current trial congruency (congruent, incongruent).

Measured variables

We will measure reaction time (RT) and error rate (ER) in each of the 16 conditions defined by our four-factor design.

Indices

Our main hypotheses concern whether (a) the congruency sequence effect (CSE) is smaller when the stimulus modality changes than when it repeats, and (b) the CSE is significant when the stimulus modality changes.

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

We will use a repeated-measures, within-subjects ANOVA to analyze our results. The factors will be stimulus modality (visual, auditory), stimulus modality repetition (repeat, switch), previous trial congruency (congruent, incongruent), and current trial congruency (congruent, incongruent). We will conduct one ANOVA for mean RT and another for mean ER. We will assess the simple effects of any interactions that we observe.

Transformations

N/A

Inference criteria

We will employ significant 2-tailed p-values from the repeated-measures ANOVAs and analyses of simple effects described earlier.

Data exclusion

We will exclude participants whose overall accuracy during the twelve blocks of test trials is lower than 75%. If this occurs, then our final sample size will be larger than 32 participants. However, we will only include data from the first 32 participants with usable data (i.e., overall accuracy greater than 75%) in the final analyses. With regard to these 32 participants, we will exclude the following trials from analysis. In the analysis of mean RT, we will exclude errors, trials following errors, response omissions, trials following response omissions, and outliers (i.e., trials with RTs more than 3 standard deviations from their conditional means). In the analysis of mean ER, we will exclude the same trials with the exception of errors and response omissions, which are the dependent measure.

Missing data

We will replace each participant who performs the task with less than 75% overall accuracy.
