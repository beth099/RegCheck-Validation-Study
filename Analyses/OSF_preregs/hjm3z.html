There’s a Time and a Face: The Time Course of Composite Face Processing

Study Information

Title

There is a time an a face: The time course of composite face processing

Authors

Daniel R. Little, Carmen Lynch, Xue Jun Cheng

Description

Many decades of face processing research have converged on the idea of faces being special objects (Farah, Wilson, Drain, &amp; Tanaka, 1998). One demonstration that highlights this is the composite face effect, where the illusion of a unique face emerges upon aligning the top half of one face with the bottom half of another (Young, Hellawell, &amp; Hay, 1987). In this arrangement, recognition for each half is poorer compared to when the composite is misaligned (Rossion &amp; Boremanse, 2008; Young et al., 1987). The to-be-ignored half inevitably interferes with the attended half, described as a failure of selective attention (Richler, Palmeri, &amp; Gauthier, 2012). This provides one of the many operational definitions of holistic processing (Richler et al., 2012). One issue with studying holistic processing is that there are many different empirical measures and mechanisms, not all of which are consistent. For instance, even composite faces fail to demonstrate results consistent with holistic processing in some tasks (e.g. Fitousi, 2015; Cheng, McCarthy, Wang, Palmeri &amp; Little, 2018). A more direct approach for measuring holistic processing of composite faces involves systematically limiting the amount of time to process a composite face and mapping the resulting changes in accuracy over time. In the present study, we employ a stop-signal task using composite faces to map out the effects of congruency and incongruency over time. That is, does the bottom face interfere with change detection decisions made about the top face and when during the time course of processing does this occur.

Hypotheses

We expect to find that sensitivity to changes grows from near 0 to greater than 0 as the time for responding increases. We expect to find that sensitivity is increased across the entire time course as the strength of the change in the top face changes. To assess this, we will compare d’ computed from Same/Same (indicating a change in the Top/Bottom face) and Low Discriminability/Same trials to d’ computed from Same/Same and High Discriminability/Same trials. When told to focus only on the top half of the face, we expect a change in the bottom half with no corresponding change in the top half to decrease asymptotic sensitivity, reflected by an increase in false alarms. To assess this, we will compare d’ computed form Sane/Same vs Same/Low to d’ computed from Same/Same vs Same/High. We make no specific predictions regarding the growth or start parameters of the speed-accuracy trade-off function. We expect a change in the bottom half of the face that co-occurs with a change in the top half to increase asymptotic sensitivity reflected by an increase in the hit rate. To assess this, we will compare d’ computed from the following: Same/Same vs Low/Low, Same/Same vs Low/High, Same/Same vs High/Low, and Same/Same vs High/High, with d’ expected to increase across those conditions if there is interference. If there is no interference from the bottom face, then d’ should be equivalent between the L/L and L/H conditions and between the H/L and H/H conditions.

Sample size

We will recruit three participants, with each subject being an independent replication of the entire experiment (Smith &amp; Little, 2018). Data are collected over 15 one-hour session so that a large number of trials will be used for each participant, with a minimum of 150 trials collected for each relevant stimulus condition.

Sample size rationale

Trial numbers are set so that we can compute good approximations to the choice distributions for each relevant stimulus condition.

Stopping rule

Data collection will be stopped after 15 sessions.

Transformations

Hit rate and false alarm rates will be converted to d-prime and criterion estimates using a differencing change detection model (Sorkin, 1962).

Inference criteria

We will use nested model comparison (using MLE and Bayesisan model comparison) on the SAT curves of the sensitivity data from each item condition.

Data exclusion

For some analyses, trials will be excluded if the response is (a) before the stop-signal, (b) less than 100 ms after the stop-signal, (c) greater than 300 ms after the stop signal, or (d) incorrect. We may additionally conduct analysis in which we relax criteria c and d.
