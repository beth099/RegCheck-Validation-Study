Study Information

Title

The effects of background complexity, meaning, and structure on parallel processing in visual search

Authors

Kirk Ballew, Alejandro Lleras, Simona Buetti

Description

There is evidence that background information impacts visual search. This study seeks to test how the complexity, meaning, and structure of different backgrounds influence parallel processing in visual search.

Hypotheses

1) Logarithmic search slopes will vary as a function of background complexity; Non-directional. 2) Intercepts of logarithmic search slopes will vary as a function of background complexity; Non-directional. 3) Logarithmic search slopes will vary as a function of background meaning; Non-directional. 4) Intercepts of logarithmic search slopes will vary as a function of background meaning; Non-directional. 5) Logarithmic search slopes will vary as a function of background structure; Non-directional. 6) Intercepts of logarithmic search slopes will vary as a function of background structure; Non-directional.

Explanation of existing data

Data collection procedures

Participants will be recruited from the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign Credit or Paid subject pools, and will be given course credit or pay in exchange for participation. All participants will be prescreened to ensure that they have normal or corrected-to-normal color vision. Before or during data collection: (1) If a researcher error occurs, such as providing the participant with the wrong instructions or not properly setting up the experimental conditions (i.e., program or lighting conditions), participants will be allowed to complete the study. However, a note will be made in the experimental log sheet and the participant’s data will be excluded. (2) If the participant appears to be in an altered state (such as being under the influence of a substance or extremely sleepy), a note will be made on the experimental log and the participant’s data will be excluded. (3) If the participant is not following instructions (i.e., not using proper hand responses or making the same response on every trial), then the participant will be given a warning upon first notice. In addition, a note will be made on the experimental log sheet about this behavior. The data from that participant will not be used. After data collection: If a participant’s error rate is greater than 10% and less than 90%, his or her data will be excluded. This study is expected to be completed over the course of two semesters.

Sample size

The sample size in Experiment 1A will be 20. We will then use the effect size observed in that experiment to determine the sample size in the remaining three experiments.

Stopping rule

Variables

Manipulated variables

Search backgrounds will be manipulated in each experiment. Experiments 1A and 1B will include a beach scene (complex with meaning), a phase-scrambled beach scene (complex with no meaning), and a background with solid color (simple with no meaning). Experiments 2A and 2B will include a child’s bedroom scene (structured with meaning), phase-scrambled child’s bedroom scene (unstructured with no meaning), and upside-down child’s bedroom scene (structured with no meaning).

Measured variables

(1) Accuracy: percentage of correct trials out of total trials (2) Reaction time: time elapsed prior to response (i.e., button press), in milliseconds (3) Logarithmic search slopes (D parameter in Equation 1 of the attached preregistration document) (4) Intercepts (α parameter in Equation 1 of the attached preregistration document)

Indices

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

All statistical analyses will be conducted at a significance level of alpha = .05. Two-way ANOVAs will be conducted on log search slopes and intercepts, respectively, to determine the effect of background condition for each of the four experiments. If these ANOVAs are significant, planned comparisons between the background conditions will be performed to determine differences between the three conditions.

Transformations

If a participant’s error rate exceeds 90%, his or her data will be reverse-coded (i.e., responses of “right” will be changed to “left”, and vice-versa).

Inference criteria

Data exclusion

Incorrect trials and timeout trials will be excluded. Correct trials with reaction times that exceed two standard deviations of the mean reaction time for that participant will be excluded.
