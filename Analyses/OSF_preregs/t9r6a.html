Study Information

Hypotheses

The predictions are that: 1) All of the long-term emotional goals will be desirable (e.g., means at or above the midpoint of the scale), but that goals to increase positive and reduce negative affect would be the most desired, followed by goals to reduce emotional sensitivity and goals to be more emotionally open with others. 2) Greater discrepancies between a person's current and desired emotional emotional selves will be associated with greater negative affect, lower positive affect, and lower life satisfaction. 3) We predict the average intention to work toward goals to be associated with greater positive affect (i.e., reflective of self-efficacy) 4) Greater attention toward long term goals over short term goals will be associated with greater life satisfaction, whereas greater attention to short term goals will be associated with greater negative affect.

Explanation of existing data

Data collection procedures

Data will be collected online from Prolific. Participants will be required to be from the United States and over 18. We will also recruit a balanced sample in terms of gender, per Prolific's balanced sample option. Participants who fail the embedded attention check into the key measure of emotion goals will be excluded. The study is expected (based on pilot testing) to take a median of 12 minutes and participants will be paid at a rate of $12/hr (e.g., $2 for a 10 minute study, $2.50 for a 12.5 minute study).

Sample size

We will recruit 250 people from Prolific and expect to lose 10% to the failed attention check. Note that we also plan to recruit an additional sample from a college subject pool for an ultimate sample size of closer to 400, but we *will* look at the data after the Prolific sample is collected.

Sample size rationale

We have funding to recruit 250 from Prolific and then will add to that in the subject pool.

Stopping rule

We will run 250 from Prolific and then add additional participants in the Fall 2023 semester in the subject pool and run until the semester is over.

Measured variables

We will ask about 13 emotional goals: allowing emotions, cognitive control, cognitive understanding, cognitive confidence, increase positive affect, decrease negative affect, interpersonal compassion, interpersonal boundaries, interpersonal connection, interpersonal expression, reduce sensitivity, reduce emotion driven behavior, and regulation. Each of these will be assessed four times. First we will ask about current emotional self from 0 (not at all like me) to 6 (extremely like me). Second, we will ask about how each of these goals is desired from 0 (not desired) to 6 (extremely strong desire). Third, we will ask about intention to put forth effort toward that goal from 0 (no effort) to 4 (definitely will put forth effort). Finally, at the end of the study we will ask about the perception of malleability of each of those attributes from 0 (not changeable) to 4 (definitely can change with effort). Finally, we will also ask participants to select between 1 and 4 of the 13 goals as their top goals, which will allow us to calculate their goals, efforts and malleability for top selected goals versus unselected goals. We will also give the SPANE (Diener et al, 2010) and a 5-item index of life satisfaction (Diener et al., 1985). The SPANE is a 12-item measure that provides indices of positive affect, negative affect and affect balance (PA minus NA). Finally, we will assess the degree to which people attend to long-term emotional goals versus short-term goals in emotional situations with a new measure constructed for this study. Four items assess attention to emotions in the moment and four items assess attention to long term goals.

Indices

See above--SPANE and life satisfaction items will be calculated per published norms. We will calculate an overall desirability and intention to work toward goals as well as desirability, intention, and malleability for top selected goals versus unselected goals.

Analysis Plan

Statistical models

1) All of the long-term emotional goals will be desirable (e.g., means at or above the midpoint of the scale), but that goals to increase positive and reduce negative affect would be the most desired, followed by goals to reduce emotional sensitivity and goals to be more emotionally open with others. This will be tested with descriptive statistics; there will not be actual comparative statistics here (though we may do an exploratory one-way within subjects ANOVA with all 13 categories). 2) Greater discrepancies between a person's current and desired emotional emotional selves will be associated with greater negative affect, lower positive affect, and lower life satisfaction. We will calculate a difference score between current and desired emotional self (desire minus current) for each of the 13 goals, and correlate those difference scores with NA, PA and life satisfaction. 3) We predict the average intention to work toward goals to be associated with greater positive affect (i.e., reflective of self-efficacy). We we calculate an overall intention to work toward goals (and an intention to work toward TOP goals) and calculate the correlation with those overall scores and SPANE PA. 4) Greater attention toward long term goals over short term goals will be associated with greater life satisfaction, whereas greater attention to short term goals will be associated with greater negative affect. We will calculate an overall attention to short term goals, attention to long term goals and will calculate the correlations with each with PA, NA and life satisfaction as well as the difference between short and long term.

Transformations

Explained above.

Inference criteria

We will use p &lt; .01 due to the number of comparisons.

Data exclusion

An attention check will be included and those who fail it will be excluded.

Missing data

We have included "request response" to reduce missing data. Most of the items are single items and so analyzes with those missing will be excluded listwise but that participant could be included in other analyses.

Exploratory analysis

We plan to look at how (each) goal desirability predicts goal intentionality mediated by perceptions of malleability as exploratory. We will also look at how each goal desirability and goal intentionality are correlated, as well as how discrepancies between current and desired goals are associated with goal intentionality. We will look at top versus non-top goals as well, to see how these are related.
